# ADR 2026-10-15: The Queue — one card per effort, ranked by the person

Status: **agreed, not yet designed** (2026-10-15, Paul in an interactive session; reviewed by Fable
— four rounds, converged, and a final AGREE — and Sonnet — three rounds, READY). The same evening
Paul reviewed a mockup drawn from the real ledger and inbox over five rounds; what he decided there
is folded into §1–§7 and listed in §9 (amended 2026-10-15; Fable and Sonnet rounds on the amendment
follow, recorded below). TD-748 carries it to the design; TD-749 drops the grinders'
file split. Nothing in `docs/design/` changes until TD-748's design PR.

**Paul, 2026-10-15:** *what I really care about is — where is TD/feature X that I added recently
(already done, being worked, waiting to be worked, needs me for something).* And: *as a user I want
to know that my teams are working, and working the things I think are important first (dependencies
considered).* The Inbox's `Due:` dates *seem arbitrary, yet we use them extensively.*

## Why
The person asks: *where is TD/feature X* (done, being worked, waiting, needs me), and *are my teams
working on what I think matters first, dependencies considered*. The Inbox is per message and orders
by `Due:` dates the person finds arbitrary. The Queue is per effort and orders by the person's rank.

## 1. Cards
- **Membership.** Over the open ledgers of every repo the home reads (`docs/technical_debt.md`),
  join every formal `Blocked by:` id (ids before any `decision (…)`; prose after it is never read —
  `ledgerboard.parse_blocked`), `<repo>#TD-n` ids included, in both directions; **a card is a
  connected component, with no exception** (§9): it spans repos and teams when its entries do.
  ledgerboard computes one ledger's components; ShiftLead joins them across repos by the
  `<repo>#TD-n` ids. Entries are read with `ledgerboard.entries()` (which drops the template). On the
  evening's ledger (55–56 open entries) that gave 31–33 cards, the largest of 7 entries; the count
  moves with the ledger.
- **Top entry.** A card's header is its **top entry**: the `design-first` entry that waits on no
  other `design-first` entry in the card, else the entry with no open blocker in the card — the
  prerequisite the rest follow, as Paul first put it (*that TD and all the TDs it blocks*); ties by
  key (§4). `Kind` is a repo-declared field (cadence §2.11–§2.12), so the "is a design" predicate
  comes from the repo; a repo that supplies none takes the second rule alone. A design entry that
  lists its own builds in `Blocked by:` (TD-722, TD-636 today) is still its card's top entry.
- **Rows** are the card's other members, drawn as a tree from the top entry by `Blocked by:` edges
  taken in either direction (an entry under the one it names, or under the design that names it as
  its build), breadth first; a row with two parents is drawn under the better-keyed one and noted
  under the other. The top entry is never drawn again as a row. **Archived rows**: an id named in an
  open member's `Blocked by:` and found in `docs/technical_debt_archive.md` is drawn as a done row
  (the `blocked_ids` / `archived` reading screen 11 *Its builds* already does), one hop only — an
  archived row's own archived blockers are not drawn. Progress *n/m done* counts those plus the
  open rows.
- **Completed cards.** The archive keeps each entry's `**Blocked by:**` field (66 of 653 archived
  entries at filing — the ledger was groomed onto the field on 2026-09-28, so older history has no edges and its
  completed cards are single entries; said on the page as nothing, simply drawn single). The reader
  builds components over the **whole archive** (same rule, `entries()` parses the archive unchanged);
  an archive component none of whose members an open entry names is a **completed card**, placed in
  the window by the **latest `Resolved:` date** of its members (every archived entry carries one), its PR from the
  `(PR #n)` there or §4.4 *What closed*. A component joined to an open entry in either direction (an open entry names an archived one,
  or an archived entry's `Blocked by:` names a still-open id) stays with that open card (as done rows). The archive reading is cached by the archive file's blob sha. Portable:
  computed from the repo, nothing remembered on the home.

## 2. What a card shows
- **The header** is the top entry: id, its Priority badge to the right of the id, its whole title,
  wrapped, and at the right the holder's pill, the entry's state, a count
  and the card's status (below). An open card's header is tinted apart from its body; there is no fold chevron;
  the whole header folds the card. The top entry's own asks and their buttons are drawn **in the
  header**, so a folded card still shows them.
- **Each row**: id, the Priority badge to its right, title, and one state chip — *done #PR* /
  *in review #n* / *#n conflicting* (*· being fixed* while held) / *held by X* / *ready* /
  *after TD-n* / *settling* (TD-707) / *held (person)* (drawn once TD-735/736's hold reads land) / *waits until <date>* or *waits on an event* (the
  `Waits:` reason on hover) / *stalled* — and the holder's pill (its state from the holder state
  reader, TD-734). A row that needs the person has **no chip**: its block below says which kind.
  Rows begin two letters in from the header's id (just past its *TD*), and each level of the tree
  steps two letters further; on a phone the steps are smaller and still even.
- **No red.** A conflicting or failing PR is the worker's to fix and is drawn as a neutral chip.
  **Stalled** (a grey dashed chip) is a held row whose holder state (TD-734) reads idle or stuck, or
  a red PR nobody holds 24 hours after its `red_since` — a new constant: the red-PR work order's
  hour (`ORPHAN_AFTER`) has already told a worker by then. It says a team is stuck, for the techlead
  to clear, and asks the person for nothing.
- **Attached to a row**: asks, steers, looks and notes from the person inbox whose `about` names the
  row's id (`ao msg` defaults `about` from the sender's claim from now on; text-match backfill: first
  TD id = home row, others listed as mentions), a board line with `Context: TD-n`, a red PR whose
  claim/branch names the id. `about` resolves within the repos the home reads; an id archived past
  the window or an unresolved id makes the item a non-TD item (Inbox). An answered ask or steer stays
  on its row, quiet, with its answers drawn, the recommended one marked and the one taken ticked.
- **What needs the person** is highlighted on its row, with its details and one row of buttons
  beneath it — the Inbox's own render functions, not copies:
  - an **ask, steer or board line**: its answers, the recommended one first and outlined, then
    **Reply** in the same row — and **Done** on a board line, **Pause** on a steer. A board line that
    names the entry is the question itself, drawn instead of the entry's own block (TD-299 today);
  - **`Owner: paul`** — *yours to do*, with the entry's **Fix**: **Open**, **Start session**, **Done**;
  - **`Blocked by: decision (paul)`** (the `(paul)` matched without case, as `parse_blocked` reads
    it) — *held until <the words after the dash, as written>*: **Lift the hold**, **Open**. Of the
    eight such entries at filing, four hold an entry while a design round with the person runs
    (TD-704, TD-724, TD-730, TD-748), two wait on *build it when I say* (TD-656; TD-133, whose words
    record its ask snoozed to 2026-10-21 — drawn as written; the date is text, and no code reads it or lifts the hold at it), one on a
    judgement (TD-623) and one on a setting of the person's (TD-641): none is a multiple-choice
    question, so no `Answers:` line is asked for. **Lift the hold** is a new button that sends the
    Inbox's existing Reply road (§4.5 screen 6) with the fixed words *go ahead*: a decided board
    line, whose session drops `decision (paul)`. Once that line exists the row draws it as *waiting
    on them* until the session lands, and no longer needs the person — the moment the Inbox row
    leaves *Later* today. An entry that carries an `Answers:` line still draws its answers first.
- **The Queue has no Snooze** (§9): what it orders is the rank, not a date. Snooze stays on the
  Inbox.
- **The card's status**, one, by need: *needs you* > *steer* > *stalled* > *blocked* > *waiting* >
  *ready* (blocked outranks waiting: a card with one unreachable blocker is not merely waiting). **Waiting**: every open row has a `Waits:` line (`until` / `event`), is a live check whose
  build is not yet live, or is blocked only by open ids of repos the home reads. **Blocked**: some
  row's `Blocked by:` holds a `decision (<who>)` whose `who` is not the person, or an id ledgerboard
  cannot resolve (`cross_repo` *unresolved*, an unknown id — it keeps the block). **A ledger row's
  need** (`Owner: paul`, `decision (paul)`) gives the card *needs you* and puts it in the needs-you
  filter, but enters no count: the top bar, the rail and the Org rollup still sum only
  `inbox_sections`' counted rows (mail, board, state), whichever page draws them (screen 6
  *Counted nowhere*, unchanged). On a folded card it sits
  with a count: *n entries* in Up next (none for a single entry), *n/m done* in In progress in place
  of a moving status unless the card needs the person, its age and *n entries* (more than one) in
  Recently completed; and the age of the **oldest open ask**. Opened, a completed card shows each
  entry's `Resolved:` text with its PR, which is what was done; a card with nothing to add has no fold.
- State rows (a holder's permission prompt / question) stay in the Inbox only; the card shows them
  through the holder's pill.

## 3. Page layout and groups
- `/queue`, a top-bar tab after Inbox. Groups, furthest through first: **Recently completed** (a
  **4 h · day · week · month** picker, each with its count of closed entries, *day* the default: 26–69
  entries close a day; newest `Resolved:` first), **In progress** (any row held, or its PR open; in
  key order), **Up next** (no row held; in key order).
  **Only Up next is numbered**, from 1: the number is the pick order, which In progress has no use
  for. A hold on a PR that names an entry is drawn on that entry's card; a hold on something no
  entry names (`board:<key>`, a PR naming none) is an *other work* line at the end of In progress,
  with its holder, so a worker on one reads as busy.
- **Moving a card**: drag it by its grip, or its **⤒ ↑ ↓** buttons (to the top, up one, down one),
  drawn quietly on every Up next card, folded or open — the same move as a drag, for a phone or a
  keyboard.
- Filters: the Inbox rail's (team → a derived set of repos, needs you, steering, kind), by repo, and a
  **find box** (id or title; falls through to the archive and opens `/repo/<name>#TD-n`).
- An ask is never moved off its card. Starvation is answered by the *needs you* status and filter
  (only cards with an open ask, in rank order), the oldest-ask age on the folded card, and
  Telegram's one telling after 60 s (unchanged).
- **Board lines with `Context: TD-n`**: whether one is a counted needs-you row is the Inbox's existing
  board rule, unchanged (due and counted per the horizon, TD-207; *waiting on them* /
  BOARD_WAIT_DAYS). On the card it is drawn on its row either way, with its due date shown; `Due:`
  never orders cards or rows.

## 4. Rank
- **Keys.** One LexoRank key space across repos. Every open entry has a key: its **line** in the rank
  file if it has one, else an **implicit key** = its Priority's fixed band prefix (High / Medium / Low)
  followed by its **Type index** (today's `pickable` tiebreak, ledgerboard.py:256 `_key`, so
  `--pickable` and the Queue print one order; `_key`'s last component is today the summary table's
  order, and becomes id order — a change to §4.8's "ties in the ledger's order" the build entry says) and its zero-padded id number, then the repo name as a
  last tiebreak across repos — prefixes chosen to sort High < Medium < Low (`1|`, `2|`, `3|`;
  e.g. Medium TD-642 of Type index 1 is `2|1|000642|shiftlead`; a dragged key is any string between two such keys, e.g.
  past the id but before the next id's key; the SPEC fixes an alphabet whose separator `|` is its lowest
  character, and a midpoint function, since `m` < `|` in ASCII would misorder; keys before the first or
  after the last band use the space outside the prefixes) — computed, never stored, stable because ids are.
  So "ledger order" means **id order**, and two undragged neighbours always have room between them.
  **key(card) = the best lined key among its open members when any has a line, else the best
  implicit key** (§8): a dragged card keeps the person's placement, and a member without a line takes
  the card's key. A Priority edit on a dragged card changes nothing; on an undragged one it moves the
  entry's implicit key. New entries land by their implicit
  key, once settled (TD-707).
- **The rank file** (`docs/technical_debt_rank.yml`, named from the ledger's path, §8). **Grammar:** `#` comment lines first, then one line per entry,
  `TD-NNN: <key>`, sorted by key; nothing else. **A drag (or a move button) writes one line for every open
  member of the moved card**, each with the one new key between the drop's two neighbouring cards'
  keys (lined or implicit) — a few lines, never the whole file, and never a neighbour's line. A card
  that spans repos writes each member's line in its own repo's rank file, with the same key. Consequences: an entry
  archiving loses nothing (its siblings carry the key); a card that splits keeps the key on both
  fragments (tie → id order); two cards that merge take the better lined key (a lined card beats an unlined one); a member added
  later (a new build) takes the card's key. **Pruning**: a line is removed
  only when every member of its card is archived, in a flush of its own (subject `queue: prune`), so a
  drag's diff holds only the dragged card's lines. A lined entry archived while its card is still open
  keeps its line until the card is wholly archived; it is ignored (open members only) and harmless.
- Every prerequisite is on its dependant's card (§1), so any order of cards keeps prerequisites first.
- **Within a card**, pickable rows go by dependency (only pickable rows can be taken), then by key
  (lined or implicit: Priority, Type, id).
- **Landing.** A move is applied on the page at once and stored as an intent `(entry ids of the card,
  above_id, below_id)` in the home store; on flush (debounced) the home derives keys against
  `origin/main`'s current rank file in its own tree (board.py `_land` / `still()`), commits the lines,
  opens a fixed-title PR (subject `queue: rank TD-a, TD-b… (<tool>)`) and squash-merges it — **one PR
  per repo per flush**: a drag writes the moved card's members' lines, each in its own repo's file;
  neighbours' keys are read, never written. A lined key is shared verbatim across repos (the implicit
  key's repo tiebreak applies to implicit keys alone). **An unlined open member of a lined card** (a
  build filed later, a card merged into a lined one) gets its line from the next flush on the home
  (subject `queue: join TD-n`), so each repo's file alone gives `--pickable` the card's key; until it
  lands the two orders may differ for that entry alone. An unflushed intent is **re-derived** at flush against the cards as
  they are then (its card's current open members, its neighbours' current keys), not replayed; a
  neighbour that has archived or left the order is replaced by the nearest surviving card in the
  order as it now stands. Two
  flushes conflict only on the same line, and the loser retries. Unlanded intents are lost on a
  machine change (accepted).
- **dev-cadence first.** The file format and the ordering function land in **ledgerboard** (SPEC,
  release, vendored to `scripts/ledgerboard.py` and `src/sessionorc/ledgerboard.py`), so `ledger.py
  --pickable` and the home's `pickable()` print one order; `check_cadence.py` gains a `queue-edit`
  class (a change to the rank file alone, under the fixed subject); a parity row in cadence §7.
- **The rank is the pick order, by convention** (§4.8: nothing checks the pick). Briefs (grinder,
  designer) say *the highest-ranked pickable unclaimed entry in your lane*; `ao repo` prints rank order;
  work orders (`board:`, `pr:`) are picked before the ranked list, and the page draws them on the
  card their PR names, or as an *other work* line (§3). **Passed over**: a worker holding a card
  ranked below an unclaimed pickable card in its lane is flagged on the Queue with the reason from its
  claim's first `ao doing` line; settling / held / `Waits:` rows never trigger it.
- CLI: `ao rank TD-n --above TD-m | --below TD-m | --top` (§4.7).

## 8. Decided (Paul, 2026-10-15)
- **A newcomer to a dragged card: the person's placement holds.** key(card) = the best *lined* key
  among its open members when any member has a line, else the best implicit key; unlined members take
  the card's key. A Priority edit then changes nothing on a dragged card. For a week after a High
  entry joins a dragged card, the fold shows *new: TD-n (High)*, moving nothing.
- **The rank file is named from the ledger's path**: `<ledger stem>_rank.yml`, here
  `docs/technical_debt_rank.yml`, with no config key of its own; one override moves ledger, archive
  and rank file together, and dev-cadence and ShiftLead cannot disagree.

## 9. Decided from the mockup (Paul, 2026-10-15, rounds 1–6)
- **One card rule, no exception**: dependent entries share a card whatever their repo or team. The
  shared-utility rule went (it split TD-112, TD-052 and TD-035 into three cards and needed *pulled up
  by*, refused drops and links between cards to keep them in order); with it went the effective rank.
- **The header is the top entry**, its asks and buttons in the header; the rows are what it leads to.
- **The Priority badge sits to the right of the id**; header titles wrap in full; an open card's
  header is tinted, and there is no chevron; rows begin just past the header id's *TD*, two letters a level.
- **Red is not the person's**: a conflicting PR is a neutral chip; only *stalled* is flagged, and
  not in red.
- **Waiting, not blocked**, for a card whose rows wait on dates, events, live checks or other
  entries; *blocked* only past the org's reach.
- **Counts by group**: *n entries* / *n/m done*, and a completed card its age; groups in process order, completed first;
  a 4 h window beside day, week and month; only Up next numbered.
- **Answers sit with Reply** (and Done, Pause) in one row, the recommended one first.
- **`decision (paul)` is a hold**, drawn *held until …* with **Lift the hold**; no `Answers:`
  convention is added for it.
- **No Snooze on the Queue.**
- **Move buttons** ⤒ ↑ ↓ on every Up next card, folded or open.
- **Work orders** are drawn on the card their PR names; only holds no entry names are *other work*.
- **The look (round 6, from a frontend-design pass)**: a group is one panel of cards divided by rules;
  Up next's rank is set large in its own column, the page's one bold element; amber is spent only on
  what needs the person (a stripe, a tinted header, the one filled *Needs you* badge), In progress cards
  carry a green stripe, and every other state is a word with a small mark; labels are sentence case,
  never tracked capitals; IBM Plex Sans for words (the face `app.css` already names first), the mono
  face for ids only. Recently completed is one line a card that opens to *what was done*. Restyling
  the other pages the same way is not decided here.
