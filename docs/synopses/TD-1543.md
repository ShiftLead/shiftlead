# TD-1543: A Do item a session could do offers to have it done (Have a session do it)

## What it does

A Do item on your board (an act line) gets a second button beside Done: Have a session do it. The session that
writes the item can name who should do its step in a Doer field: a repo, or a team. When the Doer names another
repo or a team, pressing the button hands the step straight to that repo's or team's techlead seat as your own
handed question, and the item closes. When the item names no Doer, or its Doer is this repo or one of this repo's
teams, the press hands the step to this repo's team as work. A session there does it, passes it to the repo it
belongs to, or gives it back to you with the exact steps or command to copy.

## The key choices, and why

- The Doer is a field on the item, as you asked, so the press goes straight to the named seat with no hop. The
  asker's note in words is the fallback when there is no Doer, or when the named seat cannot take the step.
- A Doer names a repo or a team, never a single seat. A repo's or team's techlead seat is the one that turns a
  step into an entry, and a seat with another job, such as a fact-checker, does no steps.
- Before anything is sent, the page reads the item fresh from the board and checks it is still open and still
  yours to press. It sends nothing it read from a page that might be stale.
- The handed question carries the item's own key, so a second press while the first is unfinished does not
  send the step twice; once the seat has long closed, pressing a line still open hands it again. A press whose
  hand-off may already have gone never falls back to the other route.
- The question's first line says which board and which session wrote the step, and when it is due. The seat then
  reads the rest as that session's words, not yours. Its subject is the item's own text.
- Any entry the item was about, if it waits on you, reads "handed to <seat>" until the seat reports back, or for
  at most three days. Its own answer (Lift the hold, or Done) stays in its ⋯ menu.
- The field is a change to ledgerboard's board format, asked of ledgerboard's seat. The Doer half of the button is
  built only once that change reaches this repo through agent-cadence and a pin bump.
- With no Doer, the press only records your answer on the board and makes the item a work order. It changes no
  ledger entry.

## What it rules out

- A press never runs anything. A command in the note is a suggestion that a session reads under its own rules.
  There is no vocabulary of typed actions.
- A step only you can do (a login, sudo, a promote, a team's start or stop) carries no Doer. It ends "only you
  can do this", and the button is not offered on it.
- No other kind of item gets the button, and an item that comes back after three days gets no second one.

## What you will see

The button after Done on act items on a Queue card, the Inbox, the due strip and the Repo page's board. Where the
Doer resolves, the button's tooltip names the seat it goes to. While the hand-off runs the row reads
"handing…". Once the step is handed, the item is gone from the board and the question shows in your Inbox as
handed, with its holder. If the hand-off is refused, the item becomes a work order for this repo's team, and the
toast says why. If the outcome is unknown, the row stays open and says the hand-off did not finish. If
the host agent is older than the page, the press stops, says so and sends nothing; if the question goes without
the item's key, the toast is red, and should closing the item fail too, the row warns a second press resends it.
