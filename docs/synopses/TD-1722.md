# TD-1722: The trial of the cheaper way to design: the cost model, three replays and the pass test

## What it does

It tries the new way of designing (the author's checklist, the prep file and the gate before any reviewer) in two
halves before it goes upstream. First, three past designs you chose, TD-1557, TD-1569 and TD-1470, are designed
again the new way in a sealed copy of the repo as it stood when each began, and two blind scorers check the result
against the findings the original's reviews raised. Then the first ten real designs on the new way are measured
against the ten before it for cost, findings and what escaped into the builds. You read each half and decide.

## The key choices, and why

- A replay runs outside the org, as a headless Claude session on the conformance profile, with no access to the
  live home, the forge or your boards, and an audit of everything it touched afterwards: anything it could read
  from after its start would hand it the answers. A grinder starts each, bounded by a turn limit and a timeout,
  and the replays spend the conformance account's usage, not yours.
- The answer key is only the original's real problem findings, never wording or bookkeeping, nor findings caused by
  an earlier fix, nor ones resting on your answers, which a replay never gets.
- The scorers also score the originals' own finished designs, unlabelled, so their noise is known and subtracted.
- Replays can only show the new way is clearly worse, beyond the scorers' noise: more than one in ten findings left
  silent or wrong, the same over the hard situations alone, or more than one in eight over the whole key with
  findings the result has no place for counted as missed. TD-1470 is read over its first four rounds, since a
  replay stops at the cap.
- The real designs pass when, against the ten before them, they spend less per hundred lines of design, at least
  six of ten come in under the old median, no more problems escape into the builds, and no more hard ones turn up
  after round 1. A few small design-only findings outside the hard situations are counted and accepted.
- The trial accepts two leaks that favour the new way: the checklist and the designer's brief were written after
  the replayed designs began, and drew on later designs.
- The replay is not told of your Hold on TD-1470's round 8; its key stops before the revision.
- Costs are captured every week, because Claude prunes transcripts after about 30 days.
- The models are today's, newer than the originals': the test is whether the new way is at least as good and
  cheaper, not why.

## What it rules out

- No replay under the old way on today's models as a control; you have not asked for one.
- The replays keep Fable then Sonnet in every round; real designs reviewed by TD-1721's rule (Sonnet alone after
  round 1) are shown apart.
- No result decides anything alone: every threshold is proposed, and you decide on each report.

## What you will see

- An item after the replays: go on to the real designs, stop (the checklist and gate come out of the brief), or
  hold with a note.
- A last item, six weeks after the tenth gated design merges and by the end of January at the latest:
  pass (the shape goes upstream to agent-cadence and the reset is spent on it), fail, or hold.
- Each report says what it could not measure.
