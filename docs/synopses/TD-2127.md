# TD-2127: Tell the person when memory runs short or processes outlive their sessions

## What it does

Each host watches its free memory and its free swap. When either stays short, you get one Inbox row and one
message naming what holds the most. Each host also looks for processes that finished sessions left running, and
when they add up you get one row and one message about them. It only tells you. It never stops a process.

## The key choices, and why

- Memory and swap are told apart. Swap stays full long after pressure passes, so a standing swap row must not hide
  a later shortage of memory.
- A row starts when two readings a minute apart are under 10% free, and ends at the first reading over 20%, so a
  short spike tells nobody and a value near the line does not flicker.
- A process counts as ShiftLead's only if it carries ShiftLead's own session or home marker. Having lost its parent
  or its directory is not enough, since live test servers and your own jobs look the same.
- It must also be over an hour old, and either its session has ended or its temporary home is gone.
- The left-behind row starts at 512 MiB or 25 processes on two readings ten minutes apart, and ends once under both
  256 MiB and 10 processes.
- Sessions that ended are remembered for 90 days, because a leftover can outlive its session's record by weeks.

## What it rules out

- No process is stopped or signalled, by this design or from its rows.
- Your own detached jobs, desktop services, and live sessions are not named as left behind.
- Only your own user's processes are read, so memory held by root or another user is never named. The row says
  "Your largest" for that reason.
- No command line leaves the host.

## What you will see

- An Inbox row under Needs you, such as "kmaster swap low: 4% free", then your three largest holders.
- A row saying how many processes finished sessions left behind, their total size and the three largest.
- Each is told once on Telegram and push, can be snoozed, and clears by itself. A swap row can stand until its
  holders exit or the host restarts.
- The disk command in the terminal lists each left-behind process, so you can check one before acting on it.
