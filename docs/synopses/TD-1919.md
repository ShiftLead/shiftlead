# TD-1919: The Focus terminal holds its pane at one grid, and the person resizes it on demand

## What it does

Every session's terminal is fixed at one size, its grid of columns and rows, from its start. Opening a Focus page, a
phone's turn or keyboard, or another window no longer resizes it. The page draws the terminal at the session's own grid:
a smaller window scrolls across, by a sideways wheel or Shift+wheel, which no longer scrolls the history, and shows the
rows down to the prompt, the bottom rows where Claude Code's input box sits once the screen is full, and a larger one
leaves empty room. Scrolling in a smaller window first moves the view up the session's own screen, then into its
history, a page one row short of what you see, so no line is skipped. A jump to an earlier prompt lands it at the top.
On a new monitor or window, one press, Fit to window, sets the grid to the window in view.

## The key choices, and why

- Resizing a session while Claude Code is drawing is what cut off, dropped and repeated lines in the history. Measured
  on a real session: a width change repeated 5 to 13 paragraphs and a height change lost 3 to 4. With the size fixed,
  every paragraph was there once in every run.
- Fit to window waits until the session is idle with nothing of its own running, then resizes. It never interrupts the
  turn or stops the tool: each of those ways was measured and lost or repeated lines.  Even at idle, a fit to a smaller
  window can repeat or drop the last few paragraphs. A shell fits at once. If the session stays busy for five minutes,
  the fit gives up with a note. A second press cancels it.
- A new session starts at your grid, 120 by 40 until you set your own. A fit on the desktop's own Focus page becomes
  that grid for later sessions, written only once the session confirms the size it took. A phone's fit, or a popped-out
  window's, changes that one session only. Another window open on the same session follows within a few seconds,
  scrolling or with empty room, until it fits too.
- A phone measures afresh at every press: it puts the keyboard and sheets down, waits up to a second for the keyboard to
  start falling and up to another for the screen to settle, then measures, so a fit never shrinks the session to a dozen
  rows. If the keyboard will not go down, it uses its last good measurement, or with none sends nothing and says to put
  the keyboard down and press again. Turning the phone, or the page switching between its phone and desktop layouts,
  cancels a waiting fit. A tablet's wide page presses the same way, since its fit becomes your grid, and its layout
  holds still through the press so the keyboard falling cannot flip it. A window too small or too large for a grid is
  refused with a note. A pinch still changes how many columns you see, without a new measurement.
- A read-only Focus can fit, since fitting types nothing, so a team's unattended session needs no Take over.

## What it rules out

- Any page, phone or reload resizing a session by itself.
- The terminal mark *resized by another client*, which no longer happens. *fitting when idle…* takes its place.
- Fitting while the tool is drawing, by interrupting it or by pausing it.

## What you will see

- A Focus terminal that keeps its size, with a scrollbar when the window is narrower than the session.
- Fit to window in the more menu and the phone's sheet; its tooltip names both sizes; it reads Cancel fit while waiting.
- Two builds: the first fixes each session's size, draws the page at it, so no narrow window wraps its lines, and
  gives the desktop a plain Fit to window that resizes at once. Until the second adds the wait for idle, the phone and
  tablet measuring and the marks, a phone or a popped-out window has no Fit to window at all, and a tablet measures its
  box with the keyboard up.
