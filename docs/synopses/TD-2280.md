# TD-2280: A forgotten login's records lose their transcript, though the design says they keep it

## What it does

When you forget a login on the Tools page, the sessions that ran on it stop reading anything from its folder:
their transcript, conversations, prompts, links and spend show nothing, and none of them can be resumed or
relaunched. Adding the same folder back as a login gives the name and account back, and every one of those
sessions reads again, whole. The tools design used to promise their transcripts stayed readable meanwhile; nothing
ever built that, and this design withdraws the promise.

Two small builds come with it. One makes a forgotten login named default behave like any other forgotten login,
instead of quietly falling back to the tool's own home folder. The other tells you what is going on: the
Transcript page says the session's login is forgotten and how to get it back, and Forget's confirm says how many
ended sessions ran on the login and will read nothing until its folder is added again.

## The key choices, and why

- No special read of a forgotten folder. Every launch, usage, billing, gate and reserve path reaches a login
  through the same reader, so a read for transcripts alone would have to be fenced off from each of them, and the
  earlier attempt widened four times in review.
- Forgetting is your own act and taking the login back restores everything, so the loss is only for the time the
  login is forgotten. A closed session is itself forgotten a day after it closes; an exited one waits.
- A login's name is unique on its host across tools, so a forgotten default holds that name for every tool: a new
  session with no login named is refused, in words that say why, rather than run in the tool's own home.
- In the gate, such a session shows as a row of its own with no reading until the take-back. It never mixes
  into another login's account.

## What it rules out

- Reading a forgotten login's past sessions without taking the login back.
- A forgotten login named default ever resolving to the tool's own home, so a session can never read a folder
  that was not its login's.

## What you will see

- On the Transcript page of such a session: its login is forgotten on that host, and adding its folder again
  gives it back, in place of the misleading "the tool's file is not on the host".
- On Forget this login, where ended sessions ran on it: how many, and that they read nothing until the folder is
  added again; the same line after the command-line forget, and in the Forget help.
