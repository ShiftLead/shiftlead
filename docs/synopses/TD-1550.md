# TD-1550: Integrate xAI's Grok Build CLI as an adapter, and run Grok as a fact-checker behind Gemini

## What it does

- Grok Build, xAI's open-source coding-agent command line, becomes a third tool ShiftLead can run, beside Claude
  Code and Gemini CLI: its sessions report working, idle and needs-you, take mail by the doorbell, and close like
  any other, on your SuperGrok sign-in. Its version is pinned, with its own updater off.
- Its first job is a second fact-checker on every doc-only PR, with its own trial comment and its own tally.
- Every fact was read from its user guide and the program installed here, never from a run; the build reads
  each again against the real tool and corrects the design where they differ.

## The key choices, and why

- "Behind Gemini" is read as beside it: a seat of its own, asked on every doc-only PR as Gemini's is, which
  spends two of the hour's six seat starts on a PR. It is added only after the live walk and a trial run pass.
- It is fed by the tool's own hooks, which copy Claude Code's events; a Grok prompt is answered in its terminal.
- Each Grok profile has a home of its own; yours is never touched, and two homes on one login are one account.
  Every launch clears any xAI API key, so an expired sign-in never falls onto a bill (a key-billed profile waits
  for an entry of its own), and trusts the session's folder without writing it down.
- Your Claude Code and Cursor hooks and MCP servers are off inside a Grok session; the kill and CI-watch guards
  apply as on the other tools.
- Your redesign: the repo's start hooks run before the session starts, and what they print goes into the first
  prompt after the brief, marked as information, cleaned of terminal codes and cut at 16 KiB. Until it is in,
  the session takes nothing, for as long as that takes: any send is refused, yours among them (your keys still
  reach its dialogs); a wrap-up or a stop time stops it, since it never began, though the wrap-up still says
  sent and a second press says exited; ShiftLead's own lines wait.
- The cost: a hung hook holds that start, others in its folder or repo, and the home's tick, up to a minute.
- Grok's own Claude-hooks switch was weighed and not taken: it would show the hooks' output on screen but never
  give it to the model, would also run your personal Claude Code hooks, and has no project-only form.

## What it rules out

- The tool's headless mode, its own worktrees and its JSON-RPC mode: a session is the interactive terminal.
- Moving Grok's brief to where its prompt cache reads, until the walk has recorded how Grok caches.
- Rate-limit reports for now, so a Grok seat at its limit is not routed around: its asks expire.
- A role that needs a start context; and hook text for a launch with no first prompt, or after a clear.
- Merging the adapter if the walk finds no steady composer row, hooks blind to the session's environment, or a
  moved home that leaves the hooks and the sign-in behind.
- Until agent-cadence's TD-252 lands, the one-agent-per-directory check does not see a Grok session.

## What you will see

- One decision. The redesign's four rounds ended at their limit, each finding something new in one piece, how a
  session wrapped up before its brief is stopped; the reviewers judged that piece over-built, not your shape,
  and it is now a plain stop. A fix to it for a team member on another machine, and two from the closing check,
  no review has read, so the recommendation is one more round. Go needs Grok Build, already installed here,
  confirmed as yours and two homes signed in on SuperGrok. Grok's builds land after TD-1577's; xAI's terms page
  could not be read, so whether a subscription may be driven this way is yours to weigh. Or hold, or not now.
