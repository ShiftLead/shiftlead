# TD-1550: Integrate xAI's Grok Build CLI as an adapter, and run Grok as a fact-checker behind Gemini

## What it does

- Grok Build, xAI's open-source coding-agent command line, becomes a third tool ShiftLead can run, beside Claude
  Code and Gemini CLI: its sessions report working, idle and needs-you, take mail by the doorbell, and close like
  any other, on your SuperGrok sign-in. Its version is pinned, with its own updater off.
- Its first job is a second fact-checker on every doc-only PR, with its own trial comment and its own tally.
- Every fact about the tool was read from its user guide and the downloaded program, never from a run; the build
  reads each again against the tool it installs and corrects the design where they differ.

## The key choices, and why

- "Behind Gemini" is read as beside it: a seat of its own, asked on every doc-only PR as the Gemini seat is.
  Two seats spend two of the hour's six seat starts on a PR, which is accepted.
- The seat is added only after the adapter's live walk and an unattended trial run have passed.
- It is fed by the tool's own hooks, which copy Claude Code's events; a Grok prompt is answered in its terminal.
- Each Grok profile has a home of its own; your own Grok home is never touched. Every launch clears any xAI API
  key, so an expired sign-in never falls onto a bill, and trusts the session's folder without writing it down.
- Your redesign: the repo's start hooks run before the Grok session starts, and what they print goes into the
  first prompt after the brief, marked as information rather than instructions, cleaned of terminal codes and
  cut at 16 KiB. Until that prompt is in, the session takes nothing: any send is refused, your own Send among
  them (your keys still reach its dialogs); a wrap-up or a stop time stops it, since it never began; ShiftLead's
  own lines wait. A launch with no first prompt gets no hook text.
- The cost: a hung hook holds that start, and any other start in the same folder, and the home's tick, for up
  to a minute; a normal run takes about three seconds.
- Grok's own Claude-hooks switch was weighed and not taken: it would show the hooks' output on screen but never
  give it to the model, would also run your personal Claude Code hooks, and has no project-only form.

## What it rules out

- The tool's headless mode, its own worktrees and its JSON-RPC mode: a session is the interactive terminal.
- Moving Grok's brief to where its prompt cache reads, until the walk has recorded how Grok caches.
- Rate-limit reports for now, so a Grok seat at its limit is not routed around: its asks expire.
- A role that needs a start context, which a Grok session cannot take.
- A few stops read oddly and are accepted: a wrap-up on a session not yet briefed says it was sent though the
  session was stopped, and pressing it again says the session has exited.
- Until agent-cadence's TD-252 lands, the one-agent-per-directory check does not see a Grok session.

## What you will see

- One decision. The redesign's four rounds ended at their limit. Each found something new in one piece, how a
  session wrapped up before its brief is stopped; the reviewers judged that piece over-built, not your shape,
  and it is now a plain stop. One fix to it, for a team member on another machine, no review has read, so the
  recommendation is one more round. Go also needs Grok Build installed (its script checks no checksum and edits
  your shell's startup file) and two homes signed in on SuperGrok; or hold it with a note; or not now. Grok's
  builds land after TD-1577's, and xAI's terms page could not be read, so whether a subscription may be driven
  this way is yours to weigh.
