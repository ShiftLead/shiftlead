# TD-1721: Who reviews a design, and what the designer carries

## What it does

It cuts what a design costs where the money goes. The reviewers cost twice what the designer does, and a Fable
review costs about seven times a Sonnet one. So Fable reads a design once, in its first round, and every later
round is reviewed by Sonnet alone. The designer also starts each design in a fresh run instead of carrying the
last design's context into every turn.

## The key choices, and why

- Fable in round 1 alone: Fable finds the most that only a design review catches in round 1, and less after.
  A design's reviewers cost about $10 instead of $21.
- The other two choices: Fable then Sonnet in every round as today, about $21; or Sonnet alone in every round,
  about $6. You choose.
- The designer's context bound drops from 300k to 150k. It only ever ends a run between entries, so it never cuts
  a design in its rounds; it means the next design starts fresh, about a fifth off the designer's spend. It is set
  in your org file, so it is yours to change.
- No second seat applies the fixes: the cost model showed it would save about 2%.

## What it rules out

- Ending the designer's run between a design's rounds: it would save more, but the next run would not know why
  each fix was made.
- Changing the replays: they keep Fable then Sonnet, testing the checklist and the gate alone.

## What you will see

- Design review records with one Fable review, in round 1.
- The trial's report showing the designs reviewed this way apart from the rest, with the escapes that would show
  what a round without Fable missed.
- The designer restarting after each design.
