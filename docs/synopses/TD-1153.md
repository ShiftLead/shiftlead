# TD-1153: The team page

## What it does

A team gets a page of its own. It shows the flow the team runs, drawn as a route map with the entries in each
stage, the logins the team uses in the order they are tried, how many sessions may run at once, and when the
team starts and stops. You make, change and delete a team there, and everything saves at once.

## The key choices, and why

- A team is a repo, a flow and a list of logins. The new flows start a fresh session for each entry in each
  stage, so a team has no members to name and no briefs of its own.
- All of a team is a setting. Nothing about it lives in the repo's file any more, so no team change needs a
  pull request. A flow kept in a repo still changes by pull request.
- One number per stage, at most this many at once. A limit for the whole team is yours to set and is off
  until you do. There is no range, because no session is kept idle between entries.
- A team's name is fixed once made, and it carries a label you can change, as a login does.
- A session starts on the first login in the list that offers its stage's model and is usable right now. When
  a login reaches its reserve or a limit, its sessions move down the list. A metered login is marked as
  spending money.
- A flow names models, never tools. A new subscription joins in three steps: sign in on Tools, add it to the
  team's list, and run a flow that names one of its models. The page says which step is missing.
- A flow switch shows every entry in flight and what happens to it, then takes effect at once. An entry in a
  stage the new flow also has carries on. One in a stage it lacks has its session ended and comes to you.
- Balance becomes Holds new work: over its line the team takes no new entry, and work in a stage carries on.
- Tools, Flows, Briefs and Teams sit under the gear with Settings, on one rail. None is a top bar tab.
- What it costs: a flow switch can end sessions mid-stage, and their unpushed work is lost. A team on a node
  shows its login names but not their state, since the home does not read a node's logins yet. Most of the
  page does nothing until the one cut-over, and its later builds are seen live only after it.

## What it rules out

- A range of sessions per stage, members kept for one run, and sizing a team from its workload.
- Editing a team in a file, a change tray, and a team change that merges by pull request.
- An add to a team button on the Tools page. Adding a login is the team page's alone.
- Changing a team's name, or moving it to another repo or host. Change its label, or make a new team.

## What you will see

Under the gear, a rail with Settings, Tools, Flows, Briefs and Teams. On Teams, a tab per team. Each page shows
whether the team runs, its flow as lines of stops with the entries waiting at each, the ones waiting on you
drawn dashed, its logins as a numbered list with each one's gauge, and its timetable. Before the cut-over the
page shows only the head and the timetable.
