# TD-1153: The team page

## What it does

A team gets a page of its own. It shows the flow the team runs, drawn as a route map with the entries in each
stage, the logins the team uses in the order they are tried, how many sessions may run at once, and when the
team starts and stops. You make and delete a team there. Its logins, numbers and timetable save as you change
them, a flow switch takes a press, and its name, repo and host are fixed once made.

## The key choices, and why

- A team is a repo, a flow and a list of logins. The new flows start a fresh session for each entry in each
  stage, so a team has no members to name and no briefs of its own.
- A team is settings, not a file in the repo. One thing stays a pull request: a repo's second team is declared
  in that repo's ledger. A flow kept in a repo still changes by pull request too.
- One number per stage, at most this many at once, starting at 1. A limit for the whole team is off until you
  set it. There is no range, because no session is kept idle between entries.
- A team's name is fixed, and it carries a label you can change, as a login does.
- A session starts on the first login in the list that offers its stage's model and is usable right now. When
  a login reaches its reserve or a limit, its sessions move down the list. A metered login is marked.
- A flow names models, never tools. A new subscription joins in three steps: sign in on Tools, add it to the
  team's list, and run a flow that names one of its models. The page says which step is missing.
- A flow switch shows every entry in flight and what happens to it, then takes effect at once. An entry in a
  stage the new flow also has carries on. One in a stage it lacks has its session ended and comes to you.
- Where a repo has several teams, one of them looks after the repo: tagging, board answers, pull requests
  nobody holds and the sweep. While that team is stopped, those four wait.
- Balance becomes Holds new work. Tools, Flows, Briefs and Teams sit under the gear with Settings, on one rail.
- What it costs: a switch can end sessions mid-stage, and their unpushed work is lost. Before the switch-over
  you pick each team's new flow yourself. Today's scale-up setting is dropped. Deleting a team needs every
  entry in flight finished, answered or retagged. A team on a node shows its login names but not their state.
  Most of the page does nothing until the one cut-over.
  After the cut-over every team is stopped, and runs only once you press Start.

## What it rules out

- A range of sessions per stage, members kept for one run, and sizing a team from its workload.
- Editing a team in a file, and a change tray.
- An add to a team button on the Tools page. Adding a login is the team page's alone.
- Changing a team's name, or moving it to another repo or host. Change its label, or make a new team.
- The when work appears setting. A started team takes work the moment it appears, so the setting goes.

## What you will see

Under the gear, a rail with Settings, Tools, Flows, Briefs and Teams. On Teams, a tab per team. Each page shows
whether the team runs, its flow as lines of stops with the entries waiting at each, the ones waiting on you
drawn dashed, its logins as a numbered list with each one's gauge, and its timetable. Before the cut-over the
page shows only the head and the timetable.
