# TD-2114: A session offered a live check is told to build, because the standard build brief has no words for a check

## What it does

A live check is a look at the running system to see whether a merged change really works. Once flows run, such a
check reaches a build session, whose brief says to build. This design tells that session, whatever brief its stage
uses, that it writes no code, reads the live system and never presses anything in it. It also gives each of the
check's four endings a way to finish. When the check holds, the entry is archived along the flow's normal route.
When it fails, must wait for something, or needs your judgement, the session records that and the entry waits.

## The key choices, and why

- The rules go in the instructions every stage session gets, not in the build brief, because a brief of your own
  can replace the standard one.
- One new ending, "the check waits", covers the three cases that leave the entry open. No flow file changes.
- That ending merges its small ledger change without a review stage, after the session's own fact-check, because
  the work is a note and a blocker. If a reviewer has already read the pull request, it may not skip them, and
  the entry comes to you instead.
- The ending is refused unless the entry is really blocked, so a check cannot bounce straight back.
- Once merged, the entry's flow record closes. When the blocker clears, a fresh session checks again.

## What it rules out

- A live check session pressing anything on the live system.
- A waiting check asking you to archive it, or coming to you merely because it must wait.
- Any team starting to offer live checks before this is built: the team start work waits on it.

## What you will see

Nothing new on a page. A failed check shows as an entry blocked by its fix, a waiting one by its date or event, and
one needing you as an ordinary question with your look attached. After it clears, the check runs again by itself.
A merge refused for a cause the session can cure goes back to it up to three times, then to you; one only you can
cure comes straight to you. You may merge it by hand.
