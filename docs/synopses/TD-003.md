# TD-003: Phone layout: Focus as one screen — terminal, strip, key bar, input bar; the rest in sheets

## What it does

On a phone, Focus becomes one screen that never scrolls, and the terminal takes nearly all of it. Above it is
one thin strip: back, the session's name and state (a press opens a switcher of every session), a count of
other sessions that need you, and a ⋯ button. Below it are two bars: a key bar (esc, tab, shift-tab, ctrl, an
arrow pad, enter and a digit page) and an input bar that opens the composer as a sheet, with a ⌨ button to type
straight into the terminal. Everything else on Focus, every act and side-panel card, lives in sheets behind ⋯.

## The key choices, and why

- Your pick, option A of the mockup: the strip and bars sit beside the terminal and never cover it, rather
  than a floating dock over its last rows.
- Questions and permissions stay in the terminal, answered with the key bar, because redrawing them as buttons
  has proved troublesome. The phone draws no Allow / Deny; an unattended session's are answered from the Inbox.
- A tap on the terminal does not raise the keyboard; only ⌨ does, so reading never swaps the bars for a
  keyboard. The composer is the way to write a prompt, since it takes autocorrect, paste and dictation.
- The phone that types gets its own columns (about 50 upright); the desktop gets its own back when it types.
  The grid changes only for the keyboard, a turn of the phone and a pinch, and never while a sheet is open.
- A pinch sets the font between 9 and 16 px for this phone's browser alone, with a note of the columns it gives.
- A swipe along the strip moves to the next or previous session; never from the screen's edge, which is the
  browser's back and forward.
- A link in the terminal opens on a press held for half a second, so a tap or a scroll never opens one.
- The phone layout applies to a phone upright or on its side; a tablet wider than 720 px keeps the desktop
  page with the key bar under the terminal. The layout never flips while the keyboard is up.
- The UI can be added to the home screen as an app with no browser bars, and its connections come back by
  themselves after the phone suspends the page.

## What it rules out

- Nothing is dropped from Focus, only moved into the ⋯ sheet; pop-out, the VS Code link and the tmux command
  are not drawn, since a phone has no windows or editor for them.
- No smaller font to keep a desktop's 120 columns: it would be unreadable.
- No copy from the terminal on a phone (the Transcript is for that), and no sharing into ShiftLead from
  another app.
- The git panel's full views and the New-session form stay desktop-width.
- Push alerts when a session needs you are not part of this design (TD-1302 adds them).

## What you will see

Open a session on your phone: a strip at the top, the terminal filling the screen, the key bar and the
Compose bar at the bottom. Answer a numbered question with the digit page, read the cards behind ⋯, and
switch sessions from the strip. It is built in three parts: TD-1299 (the screen), TD-1300 (the key bar) and
TD-1301 (pinch and swipe).
