# TD-1671: A scaled seat's pair and thread bounds, and whether they count over the seat

## What it does

A seat that has scaled out into extras is one seat to the rest of the org. Until now only the "one open question
per sender" rule counted it that way. The limit on how many messages two sessions may trade without replying to
anything, three hundred a day, counted each extra on its own. So a session could send a seat with two extras
three times that limit. The design makes the seat one party to that limit: every message between a session and
any of the seat's records counts toward one shared total. Where both ends are scaled seats, all their records
share one total.

## The key choices, and why

- The count is added up when a send is checked. The tallies stay where each message wrote them, so nothing that
  already writes them changes.
- Mail inside one seat is counted as it is today, pair by pair. Adding it up over the seat would count each
  message twice.
- The limit on one conversation's length needs no change between a session and a seat. The session holds every
  message of the conversation, and its own count stops it.
- When a send is refused, the message names the seat rather than the extra that took the mail.

## What it rules out

- Spreading messages over a seat's extras to get past the daily limit.
- Changing how either limit is written, or the numbers themselves.
- A perfect count in rare cases, each small and accepted: mail inside one seat, an extra removed and
  re-created within the day, one message sent to two of a seat's records, two same-named seats in different
  teams, and a conversation between two scaled seats or with you, which keeps its length count per record.

## What you will see

Nothing day to day. A runaway exchange with a scaled seat stops at the same limit as with any session, and the
refusal names the seat. Two busy scaled seats share one daily total, so if they ever hit it doing real work,
the number is the thing to raise.
