# TD-1590: A decided end-of-rounds line goes to the grinders, and a dropped work order re-rings after none

## What it does

When you answer a design's end-of-rounds item (Go, One more round, or Hold with a note), the answer now goes
to that design's own designer, not to the grinders. Today any grinder can take it, and a grinder can only pass it
on or drop it; in ledgerboard one dropped it while the designer sat waiting. The answer counts as the designer's
work, taken before any other entry.

Separately, a session that drops a board line or a red PR itself is no longer rung for it again after every
time it says it is out of work. It hears of it again only if you answer the line again, or if another session
takes it and drops it.

## The key choices, and why

- The answer goes to the designer who last worked on that design; with none, to any designer of the repo.
- A designer whose team has wound down still counts: the team is started for your answer as for any work.
- Where the repo has no designer at all, the answer goes to the grinders as it does today, and a grinder that
  takes it carries One more round or Hold on as a new design entry rather than dropping it.
- A designer that drops your answer is passed over for it until you answer again; the other designers, or the
  grinders where there are none, get it instead.
- Only a session's own drop counts; your drop of its claim, or a lead's, does not silence anything.
- The techlead agreed to this default over the alternative: keep the grinders and only tell them to pass it on.

## What it rules out

- A grinder dropping a design's answer as not its own.
- A line naming two designs' items at once is not routed; it stays with the grinders.
- A designer busy on other work is not interrupted; it takes your answer at its next pick, and other idle
  designers do not take it meanwhile.
- Where the repo's teams start only when you say so, the answer waits on that start, not on the grinders.

## What you will see

The Repo page shows such a line in the Design column, and the Inbox says which team's designers it waits on,
and which designer where one is named. A designer with nothing to do hears of your answer at once; one at work
takes it next, without waiting for a grinder's hand-off.
