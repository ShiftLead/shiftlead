# TD-1871: Take the design checklist and its gate out of the designer's brief, as the trial's Stop says

## What it does

You answered Stop on the trial's replays, so the new way of preparing a design ends. The designer no longer drafts
against the fifteen-question checklist, writes a file of the design's claims, or runs a check that every reviewer
must see pass before reading. A design is reviewed as before the trial: the fact-check and review rounds alone.

## The key choices, and why

- The design text says only what holds now; the old shape's full wording stays in the design's history.
- The two scripts the trial built stay in the repo as tools nothing requires, since keeping them costs nothing and
  a later trial could use them. Running one decides nothing. The checks the claims script made go back to the
  reviewers.
- The trial's report and its replay results stay as the record of why it stopped: on the hard situations the
  replays left 5 of 36 known problems unhandled where the originals had left none, over the trial's limit.
- The rule on who reviews a design's rounds (Fable in the first round, Sonnet in every round) and the designer's
  context limit were decided apart from the shape, and stay. The extra Fable week the plan kept for shiftlead once
  the shape passed falls back to its mid-December rule, yours to decide again.
- Nothing goes to agent-cadence, since the shape was only ever to go there if it passed.

## What it rules out

- No second half of the trial: no real design ever ran under the shape, so the ten real designs are not read.
- No partial keep: the checklist does not stay as advice in the brief, since the replays drafted with it and came out
  worse on the hard situations.

## What you will see

One small build edits the designer brief, read by the techlead before it merges. The board's line asking you to
restart the team keeps its ask but no longer wants the time you did it. After it, a design PR has no
prep file and no pass line, and its body lists the rounds as before the trial.
