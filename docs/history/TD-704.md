## §4.9c

- 2026-11-12 (TD-704, designed with Paul): a *TDs in motion* row's chip is the claim's stage in the team's current flow — its name, its order and a new optional stage key `color` (a palette name; default by name, then the first free palette name) — replacing the fixed *add · design · grind · review*; *grind* reads the flow's *build*, and *add* falls out as whatever stage a hunter holds (*find*). Paul chose, of three readings of a PR in a flow with two review stages, the reader that has its newest unanswered ask over the first review stage after the holder's or one review stage per flow: *accuracy is key here, the user needs to be able to trust the ui*. A PR nobody has asked, or answered *findings* or *pass*, is its author's and reads the holder's stage; an ask to the person reads a grey *you*; a stage that does not exist reads a role's name in grey. Round 1 (Sonnet) made the reading exact: a reader *has* a PR while its latest ask's copy is open (not closed or expired); an answer without a verdict, or a close unanswered, gives the PR back to its author; an interactive asker's PR passed by its last reader reads *you*; while Apply waits on a switch of flow every chip is grey by role, since no record names the flow a member runs. A claim's stage is now its holder's role, not the entry's `Kind:` — a grinder holding a design-first entry reads *build*, where the old *design* phase read the kind (TD-197, TD-228).
