## §4.9c

- 2026-11-12 (TD-704, designed with Paul): a *TDs in motion* row's chip is the claim's stage in the team's current flow — its name, its order and a new optional stage key `color` (a palette name; default by name, then the first free palette name) — replacing the fixed *add · design · grind · review*; *grind* reads the flow's *build*, and *add* falls out as whatever stage a hunter holds (*find*). Paul chose, of three readings of a PR in a flow with two review stages, the reader that has its newest unanswered ask over the first review stage after the holder's or one review stage per flow: *accuracy is key here, the user needs to be able to trust the ui*. A PR nobody has asked, or answered *findings* or *pass*, is its author's and reads the holder's stage; an ask to the person reads a grey *you*; a stage that does not exist reads a role's name in grey. Round 1 (Sonnet) made the reading exact: a reader *has* a PR while its latest ask's copy is open (not closed or expired); an answer without a verdict, or a close unanswered, gives the PR back to its author; while Apply waits on a switch of flow every chip is grey by role, since no record names the flow a member runs. Round 2 (Fable): an ask to the person stays *you* once answered, until a newer ask or the PR's close (§4.9b: the PR is the person's until their merge or word); a seat's pass-up reads *you*; a closed PR never reads *you*; the special *you* for an interactive asker's passed PR was dropped, since that row already carries the person glyph; `pr_orders.reads` is recomputed from the mail each pass, and the Repo page's standing reads it instead of the seats' mail; two holders reading different chips are a row each. Round 3 (Sonnet): the rules became one ordered list, the first that applies — the person, a seat with an open ask, the holder's stage, grey by role — so a team with no flow or an unreadable flow loses stage names but keeps *you* and the reading seat's role; the person's answered ask is *you* unless they pressed *Ask the reader again* (the pressed index is kept on the reads); any answer of a seat gives the PR back; only a member whose own lane or role the pending Apply changes reads grey; a claim's PR is looked up in its own record's repo's reading; the standing's words, the person's included, are written out in *What is shown*. Round 4 (Fable): Paul kept *you* after his answer (over *you* only while the ask is open) and chose *you* for a design waiting on his `steer` (over *design*), keeping TD-814's point that such a design is not being written; *Ask the reader again* is the walk's `again`, true only on the take-up question, and a newer ask to a seat wins over an answered person entry; a PR no longer open reads its holder's stage, marked, with no reads kept past the close (the reader-merge race made a merged PR's chip depend on timing); rule 5 is put in `teamrun` *Difference* terms; an ask counts for its envelope's `repo`; a holder's stage is a worker stage. Round 5 (Sonnet): the rules were reordered so the first that applies is the answer — a closed PR first, then the person, a seat, the holder, grey — with the unread-stage-name gate (an unreadable or node flow, a pending Apply that moves the member) stated before the list instead of overriding it from below; a seat's pass-up to the director reads the director's role, not *you*; an open `ask` to the person about the reference reads *you* as a `steer` does, since §6 rule 5's *waiting* reading counts both. A claim's stage is now its holder's role, not the entry's `Kind:` — a grinder holding a design-first entry reads *build*, where the old *design* phase read the kind (TD-197, TD-228).
