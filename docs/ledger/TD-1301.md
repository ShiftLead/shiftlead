**Why:** TD-003's design: §4.5a *a pinch* and *the strip*'s swipe; §4.5 *What the terminal's grid does*.

**Ask:** On your phone, does a pinch change the columns once and survive a reload, and does a swipe on the strip walk the records?

**Fix:**
1. **A pinch** on the terminal: the four terminal elements `touch-action: none` under the narrow query, in place of §4.6's `pinch-zoom`, and the two-finger `touchmove`'s default prevented by the pinch handler (iOS Safari ignores `touch-action` for page zoom); a second finger ends a one-finger scroll and starts the pinch (the TD-986 touch handler otherwise unchanged); font size 9–16 px, kept in `localStorage` in place of `person.terminal.size` on this browser's narrow screen alone (its desktop page keeps the setting: `AO.setTermLook` is shared by both layouts, so the kept size is read only while the narrow query holds), `AO.setTermLook` reading it first so a Settings broadcast (`AO.termChan`) leaves it; one grid sent when the fingers lift; a passing note *<n> px · <cols> columns*.
2. **The strip's swipe**: 40 px, mostly sideways, opens the next or previous record in the switcher's order; nothing from the screen's edge.
3. **Help**: §4.5a help entries for *a pinch* and the strip's swipe, in `help.py` and the design's list in the same PR.
4. Tests: the stored size wins over the setting and over a broadcast on this browser only and is clamped; the swipe's order is the switcher's.

**Done when** a pinch on a phone changes the columns once, with its note, and survives a reload, and the swipe walks the records in the switcher's order.
