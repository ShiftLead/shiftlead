## TD-846: Compliance before shiftlead is sold: SOC 2 and ISO 27001 wait for a hosted or team edition; the EU Cyber Resilience Act and the supply-chain basics a buyer asks for come first

**Priority:** Low
**Added:** 2026-10-17
**Owner:** paul
**Kind:** decision
**Status:** Open
**Blocked by:** decision (paul)

**Why:**
- Paul asked (2026-10-17, the `security` session) whether SOC 2, ISO or other compliance applies to shiftlead.
- SOC 2 and ISO 27001 certify an organisation that operates a service and holds customer data. shiftlead today is MIT, self-hosted, one person per home (design §1, §2), with nothing hosted, so there is nothing for an auditor to examine. They begin to matter on options 2, 3 and 5 of the monetisation doc:
  - **A hosted or relay part** (option 3's managed host, option 5's ciphertext relay; anything that carries sessions, transcripts or keys): SOC 2 Type II (US buyers) or ISO 27001 (EU buyers), and GDPR processor duties with a DPA.
  - **A paid team edition sold to companies:** their procurement sends security questionnaires even for self-hosted software. A SOC 2 report helps and is not required. The product features they need are the ones option 2 lists: identity-provider sign-in, roles, an audit log of who typed into which session.
- What applies to self-hosted software regardless, and is cheap before it is dear:
  - **The EU Cyber Resilience Act.** It covers software with digital elements placed on the EU market. Non-commercial open source is largely exempt; a paid edition is in scope. Vulnerability reporting duties apply from September 2026, the rest from December 2027: a vulnerability disclosure policy, an SBOM, security updates for the support period, secure-by-default settings.
  - **Supply-chain basics a questionnaire asks for:** a `SECURITY.md`, an SBOM of the wheel, signed release artifacts, dependency and secret scanning in CI.
  - **Sensitive data shiftlead keeps:** run logs, mail and pane scrollback under `~/.shiftlead` can hold secrets and personal data. Their retention, redaction and file modes matter more to a real user than a certificate (TD-131 holds retention for mail and records).
  - **Each AI provider's terms for unattended use:** whether `ao new --unattended` on a subscription, rather than an API key under that provider's commercial terms, fits its usage terms. Not read for Anthropic yet. Since the Gemini (2026-10-16) and Grok (2026-11-07) adapters were designed, the question covers every adapter (`src/shiftlead/adapters/`: Claude Code, Gemini, Grok). A Grok profile runs on a SuperGrok subscription only, and xAI's terms page could not be read (HTTP 403, 2026-11-11; design §4.3, TD-1550's end-of-rounds item). Read each provider's terms before shiftlead is offered to others.
    - **First read, 2026-11-15** (Paul's `security` session; a Grok-written summary of the four providers was checked against the pages themselves, and where they differ the pages win). Anthropic, Google and the OpenAI sign-in page were read; OpenAI's Terms of Use (HTTP 403) and xAI's live terms were not:
      - **Anthropic: the unmodified `claude` binary, signed in by the person, is allowed.** [Legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance) (Authentication and credential use): OAuth is "intended exclusively for purchasers of Claude Free, Pro, Max, Ultra, Team, and Enterprise" plans, and developers "including those using the Agent SDK, should use API key authentication". Anthropic "does not permit third-party developers … to route requests through Free, Pro, Max, or Ultra plan credentials on behalf of their users", and developers "may not collect, store, or intermediate Claude.ai credentials or session tokens". Nothing "prevents an end user from signing in to the unmodified Claude Code binary with their own Claude subscription", including on a platform that hosts it. shiftlead runs that binary in tmux and never reads its credentials, so it sits on the allowed side, **provided it stays that way**: a shiftlead that held a token, or called the Agent SDK on a subscription, would not.
      - **Anthropic: two open risks.** (1) The same page says advertised Pro and Max limits "assume ordinary, individual usage of Claude Code and the Agent SDK": a lead running many unattended workers is not obviously ordinary, and the terms do not say what is. (2) The [Consumer Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms) (effective 2025-10-08, §3) bar access "through automated or non-human means, whether through a bot, script, or otherwise" except "via an Anthropic API Key or where we otherwise explicitly permit it"; whether `tmux send-keys` into an interactive `claude` is that, no page says. The [Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup) (2026-11-12) makes the person responsible for agents they deploy "and actions those agents take". Selling shiftlead is a separate line: running Claude Code in a product needs the Commercial Terms, and the customer may not "pay for, resell, or intermediate Claude usage on their end users' behalf".
      - **Google: personal-account OAuth with another tool is forbidden.** The Gemini CLI [FAQ](https://geminicli.com/docs/resources/faq/): using third-party software "to harvest or piggyback on Gemini CLI's OAuth authentication" is "a direct violation of our applicable terms", "grounds for immediate suspension or termination of your account"; the supported route for third-party agents is a Vertex AI or AI Studio API key. The FAQ says nothing about running the official CLI headless, or from a program that never touches its token, which is what the Gemini adapter does. Not answered; forum threads report 403 account disables (anecdote). The [Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy](https://policies.google.com/terms/generative-ai/use-policy) (2024-12-17) bars only unsupervised automated decisions that harm individual rights.
      - **OpenAI: no wrapper clause found.** [Codex auth](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/auth) recommends an API key "for programmatic Codex CLI workflows, such as CI/CD jobs", treats `~/.codex/auth.json` "like a password", and says nothing on third-party tools. The Terms of Use bar programmatic extraction of output (secondhand, unread).
      - **xAI: unread.** The Grok summary quotes the Acceptable Use Policy as barring "unauthorized automated or non-human means, whether through a bot, script, or otherwise"; that is the same shape as Anthropic's clause and the one most likely to catch a SuperGrok profile driven by a script. Not checked against the current page, and Grok Bot's terms are for xAI's own product.
      - **What this changes:** nothing before a paid option; at a paid option the Claude and Gemini paths need an API-key route (Commercial Terms, Vertex or AI Studio) offered beside the subscription one, and the page of third parties must say whose credentials each profile uses. Still to read: OpenAI's Terms of Use, xAI's current terms and AUP, and a written answer from each vendor for the driven-CLI case (Anthropic's page invites a sales contact).
  - **The EU AI Act:** orchestrating general-purpose models for coding is very unlikely to be high-risk; at most transparency duties, only if sold in the EU.
- What changed in the design between 2026-10-17 and 2026-11-14, read again at Paul's request:
  - **Third parties now carry data off the home.** Each is a sub-processor a paid edition would list, with a data-flow statement:
    - Telegram notifications (§4.10 *Told on Telegram when nobody is looking*) go through Telegram in plain text. By design they carry one line built from structured fields (who, what kind, an entry id) and never what a session wrote.
    - Web Push goes through the browser's push service (Apple's, Google's or Mozilla's), with the payload encrypted to the device (RFC 8291), so the push service sees ciphertext only.
    - Tailscale is the first remote route offered (§4.5b).
    - Doppler holds the push key (`VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY`) and Telegram's secrets.
  - **The home runs code on other machines.** Container nodes are built from generated Dockerfiles, and machine nodes are reached over ssh (§4.4a). A container node is provisioned from the wheel in `~/.shiftlead/wheels/` and carries the vendored `agent-cadence` (`src/sessionorc/agent_cadence.py`, TD-1014). An SBOM must cover the base images and the vendored package as well as the Python dependencies.
  - **Access control is now designed, for one person.** §4.8a classifies every connection to the host agent's socket by its peer credentials (`SO_PEERCRED`) and tells the person from an agent, with person-only acts that no session gains by crossing a channel. The home's `agent.sock` is never mounted into a container (§4.4a). This is what a questionnaire's access-control section asks for. Multi-user access control is still a non-goal (§2), and the relay is still unbuilt (§4.5b).
  - **Retention is partly done.** Mail keeps a closed entry for `MAIL_RETENTION`, 12 hours (§4.10). Anything longer is TD-131's question. A rule for run logs and pane scrollback was not checked on this read.
  - **Unchanged:** the monetisation decision, the MIT licence at version 0.0.1 with no release, no `SECURITY.md`, and `.github/workflows/ci.yml` as the only workflow (no signing, SBOM or scanning).
- The regulations' dates and scopes above are as known on 2026-10-17 and were not re-read against the regulations' text; check them when the entry is picked up.

**Location:** `docs/decisions/2026-10-13-monetisation.md`; the repo root (`SECURITY.md`, absent); `.github/workflows/` (release and scanning); the wheel `ao service install` writes to `~/.shiftlead/wheels/`

**Ask:** Once the monetisation decision names a paid option, should shiftlead start the compliance work it brings: the EU Cyber Resilience Act and the supply-chain basics first, and SOC 2 or ISO 27001 only for a hosted or team edition?

**Fix:** when the monetisation decision names a paid option:
1. Read the CRA's obligations for that option (manufacturer of a commercial product, or open-source steward) and each adapter's provider's current terms for unattended use, and write the answer here.
2. Land the basics as build entries: `SECURITY.md` with a disclosure address, an SBOM (wheel, container images, vendored `agent-cadence`) and a signature on the release wheel, dependency and secret scanning in CI, a retention and file-mode rule for `~/.shiftlead`'s logs, and a page that lists the third parties data reaches (Telegram, the push services, Tailscale, Doppler, each AI provider) and what each one sees.
3. Only for a hosted part or a team edition: choose SOC 2 Type II or ISO 27001 by the first buyers' region, and file the controls work (access control, audit log, change management; the cadence's branch, review and merge loop is already most of the last).

**Done when** the paid option's obligations are written here, and the basics are built or each is archived with its reason.
