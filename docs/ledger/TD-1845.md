**Fix:** A design round on the text a person reads, and the checks that hold it. It covers: a board item's line, `Context:`, question, `Recommended:` and answers; an `ask` or `steer` to the person; a ledger entry's `**Ask:**` and `**Answers:**`; and a seat-to-seat message the Queue shows the person. Paul's rules, as corrected by him on 2026-11-14:

1. **The question is one plain sentence**: at most about 25 words, ending in `?`, with no code in it (no backticks, paths, `name()` calls or file names).
2. **Every answer says who acts**: *I'll …* or *you …* for the person, or *a session …*. An answer like *Pinned, run it again* is refused.
3. **Orientation first.** The context opens by saying which project and which larger piece of work this is in (the umbrella entry's title in plain words), what the named thing is for, and what is being decided and why it matters. A term the session coined is explained or left out. The mechanical part: the context's first sentence names the repo and the larger piece of work, and the writer refuses one that does not.
4. **Context is as long as it needs, written for a person**: plain words, explained rather than referenced; ids, files, functions and finding numbers go behind the link (the synopsis or **Open**). No length cap (Paul's correction of his first note).
5. **A seat-to-seat message shown on the Queue** leads with a plain one-line summary for the person (the recipient's recommendation, where there is one), with the original folded under *details*.

What the design settles:
- Each rule as a check in code, never a model's judgment: where it lives (beside `render.one_sentence` in `shiftlead.ui.render`, and the board and ledger writers' checks), what it matches, and its known false positives.
- **Refuse or warn.** Paul's words say *refused*; §4.10 today says *the home warns, never refuses* and *mail is never lost to a style rule*. The design says which rules refuse at which writer (a board or ledger helper can refuse and be retried; mail from a session in a bound may need to land anyway) and changes §4.10's sentence in the same PR.
- Rule 5's summary line: who writes it (the recipient when it passes the message up, as `--recommend` is today) and how the Queue draws it.
- What each preset brief (techlead, manager, grinder, designer) says, so writers meet the checks before they are refused.

ShiftLead's part is `ao msg` to the person and `--pass-up`, the Queue's display of seat messages, and ShiftLead's own board and ledger writers. The board-item rule (cadence.md §3) is agent-cadence's, which every repo's writers follow: file it on agent-cadence's ledger with `ao td add --repo agent-cadence` when this design is written, and name it here. The board's grammar may be ledgerboard's (its item-line check); the design says so where it is.

**Done when:** The design names each check and where it runs, and says which refuse and which warn; Paul has agreed it; its builds have landed in ShiftLead, and agent-cadence's board-item rule is filed there; and a fresh `ask` to the person with code in its question, or an answer that names no one who acts, is caught by the writer before it reaches the Queue.
