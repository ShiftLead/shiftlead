## TD-1832: The Queue draws one answer set, the same way and in the same place, on every item kind, and never strands a press

**Priority:** Medium
**Type:** feature
**Added:** 2026-11-14 (paul, through ao-led-tds-ledger's notes m-1794a53339e7 and m-4940371d08f3; drafted by ao-shiftlead-techlead-ao-1)
**Owner:** designer
**Kind:** design-first
**Status:** Open
**Id take:** d66e99d7414a43f29f131c6c46739aa8
**Part of:** TD-1801

**Why:** Paul's direction, 2026-11-13, sent through his session `ao-led-tds-ledger` (m-1794a53339e7): the Queue should have one set of answer controls for its items, always drawn the same way and in the same place, to avoid confusion. Today they vary by kind. A ledger gate card shows **Lift the hold** / **Answer…** / **Start session**. A seat's ask shows its answer chips; after an answer it shows *outcome · blocked*, the outcome's text, then **Answer…** alone, with **Open** / **Dismiss** on a separate line at the right. A board decide shows its own answers (*Go* / *One more round* / *Hold*). Paul finds it hard to tell what each card is asking and which button answers it.

A second case, from TD-1796 (Paul, 2026-11-14, m-4940371d08f3): Paul pressed *Lift it* on grinder-ao-2's ask (m-eec2a2799dc3). The grinder could not apply it, and the Queue then showed the ledger gate as *answered — waiting on grinder-ao-2* with no buttons, and the ask as *outcome · blocked* with only **Answer…**. Paul had to type the ledger command himself (#3818). A person's press must never be left stranded like that. TD-1829 (design #3820) makes the press on a gated entry lift the gate itself. This entry covers what the card draws after any press that did not finish.

TD-1801's design (#3771) and its first build (TD-1808, #3803) brought the controls back onto the Queue. Its mockups were agreed before this direction, so this is a design change of its own, made after them.

**Location:** docs/design/4.5-ui.md (screen 12: An item is answered where it is read, Three verbs); docs/design/4.5a-controls.md (the Queue card rows); src/shiftlead/ui/templates/queue_card.html, queue_row.html; src/shiftlead/ui/static/app.js

**Fix:** A design round, held with Paul, on top of TD-1801's design (and TD-1829's, #3820, once it merges: read it before starting).

1. One answer set for every item kind (a ledger gate, a seat's `ask` or `steer`, a board `decide` or `act`, a holder's state row): the same controls, drawn in the same order and the same place on the item, so a person reads what is asked and which button answers it the same way on every card. The item's own answers (its `--answer`s, the board line's `Answers:`, **Lift the hold**) fill that one set's slots, and **Answer…**, **Open** and **Dismiss** sit in fixed places beside them, never on a line of their own.
2. A press that did not finish (an outcome *blocked*, *answered — waiting on <session>*, a refused write) leaves the item with its answer controls and a plain sentence saying what happened and what the person can do. It is never left with no buttons or with **Answer…** alone.
3. Paul's constraints, as for TD-1801: the designing session uses the frontend-design skill; Paul signs off on the mockups (light and dark, every item kind side by side) before any review round, his agreement recorded; all text reads for a person.
4. The design changes §4.5 screen 12 and §4.5a's Queue card rows, and files its builds, each `Part of:` this entry. Reconcile with TD-1809 (the Queue's words), which is not yet built.

**Done when:** Paul has agreed mockups that draw every item kind with the one answer set; the design's rounds have ended; its builds have landed; and Paul has looked at the live Queue and said every card's answer controls read the same.
