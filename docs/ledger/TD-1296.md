## TD-1296: Remove ShiftLead's Hold (Hold…, Release, `ao td reserve --held` and the settle clock's hold records) in favour of the ledger's `decision (<person>)` gate, which every machine reads

**Priority:** medium
**Type:** debt
**Added:** 2026-10-31 (ao-led-tds-ledger, through `ao td add --repo`, from Paul's decision in ledgerboard's target design; drafted by ao-shiftlead-techlead-ao-1)
**Owner:** designer
**Kind:** design-first
**Status:** Designed 2026-10-31 (the designer; PR open, its review rounds running): build TD-1297
**Location:** design §4.4 *Hold* and *An entry can land held* (~239–306, and `ao td reserve --held` ~1628, ~1718), §4.5 screen 6 (*held entry* rows) and screen 12 (**Hold…** / **Release**, *held (person)*), §4.5a rows *Repo page: Hold / Release*, *Inbox row: held entry* and the help line for **Hold…**, §4.7 `ao td reserve --held`; `src/sessionorc/settle.py` (`hold`, `prehold` and the `held` records the clock keeps beside settling); `src/sessionorc/agent_ids.py` (`rpc_td_hold`, `_clear_hold`, the `--held` prehold in reserve); `src/sessionorc/queue.py` (the *held (person)* status, ~708, and the designer-owner special cases: the `not in (PERSON, DESIGNER)` test ~358 and `f"decision ({DESIGNER})"` ~704); `src/sessionorc/ledger.py`, `src/shiftlead/cli.py` and the UI templates that draw Hold and Release; ledgerboard's `docs/plans/target-design.md` §0 (Paul's note on draft 2, *Remove Hold from ShiftLead*) and §3

**Why:** Paul decided 2026-10-31, in ledgerboard's target design (§0, his note on draft 2; §3 *Removed in draft 3*), that ShiftLead's Hold goes. Hold was added so an entry being designed in an interactive session is not picked while he keeps changing it. That is a decision he owes (*go*), and it can already be written as `Blocked by: decision (<person>)` with an `**Ask:**`, which holds for as long as he needs (his cadence layer's §2.4 rule already says so). A hold, though, lives in one home's settle clock, not in the ledger, so agent-cadence, and ShiftLead on another machine, pick a held entry anyway. Two ways of saying "not yet" also cost a design, a clock record, two controls and an Inbox row each. The short window between a PR adding a decision to an existing entry and its merge, in which a session could still pick it, is reported accepted by the asking session; the target design itself still marks it open (§3, §8.1). Ledgerboard's later remodel (v2.4, the gates) has no `hold`, so the two must not exist at once.

**Fix:** design first, for the long-term shape rather than how Hold is used today:
1. **A new design entry filed by an interactive session** starts with `**Blocked by:** decision (<person>)` and an `**Ask:**`. `<person>` is the human driving that session, read from its record, never a hard-coded name, so the rule works for any user. It replaces `ao td reserve --held`.
2. **Remove Hold…, Release and prehold** from the design (§4.4, §4.5, §4.5a, §4.7), the CLI, the RPCs and the UI. Before ShiftLead's ledger moves to ledgerboard v2.4, turn each hold still recorded into `decision (<person>)` with an Ask, by PR, so holds and gates never coexist.
3. **The settle clock keeps only settling**, its minutes-long cooldown on a new or changed entry, which stays ShiftLead's scheduling choice.
4. **Drop the `decision (designer)` special cases in `queue.py`**: design-first routing is `Kind`'s job, and ledgerboard's migration removes the role form.

Not blocked: decision gates work under the ledgerboard ShiftLead runs now. Done when the design says the above, its builds are filed `Part of: TD-1296`, and no hold remains on the live home.
