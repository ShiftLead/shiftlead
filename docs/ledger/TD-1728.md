## TD-1728: The pair bound counts over a scaled seat's records

**Priority:** Low
**Type:** debt
**Added:** 2026-11-12
**Owner:** grinder
**Kind:** build
**Status:** Open
**Id take:** c3eb1364ef424a4cb236d5cf2d37e52c
**Part of:** TD-1671
**Location:** design §4.9 Mail to a scaled seat, §4.10 A bounded exchange; src/sessionorc/agent_mail.py _check_bounds

**Why:** TD-1671's design (§4.9 *Mail to a scaled seat*, §4.10 *A bounded exchange*) counts the pair bound between a session and a scaled seat over the seat's records: a sender whose reply-less asks fill extras spends one budget with the seat, not one per extra. Today `agent_mail._check_bounds` reads only the pair tally on the record the send picked (`_pair(me, sid)`, `_pair(records[sid], sender)`), so a sender writing to a seat with extras takes up to the seat's `max` records' worth of `PAIR_BOUND` from it.

**Fix:** In `agent_mail._check_bounds`'s pair branch (`src/sessionorc/agent_mail.py`), for each named addressee `sid` other than the person, in `named`'s order as today:

- **One seat or two.** Where `sid != sender` and `sender in mail.seat_records(records, sid)`, the two are records of one seat (the seat's own mail to itself): read their two tallies alone — `sid`'s `pair:<sender>` and the sender's `pair:<sid>` — as today, the sum over the seat counting each send twice. Otherwise let `A = mail.seat_records(records, sender)` and `B = mail.seat_records(records, sid)` (a record that is no seat is a seat of one, `[itself]`).
- **The sums.** The addressee's side: the sum, over every `b` in `B` and `a` in `A`, of `b`'s `pair:a` tally's count inside `PAIR_WINDOW`; the sender's side: over every `a` in `A` and `b` in `B`, `a`'s `pair:b`. Compute both on every check and refuse when either is at `PAIR_BOUND`, as one record's tally refuses now. The keys are read as they stand: a resume (`agent._move_mail`) has already moved a superseded record's tallies to its successor and renamed `pair:<old>` to `pair:<new>` on every other record, so a superseded record still in `seat_records` adds nothing.
- **The read creates nothing.** Read each tally through a helper beside `_pair`, `_pair_peek(r, other, now) -> Tally | None`: `t = r.threads.get(f"pair:{other}")`; `None` when absent; else `t.at` pruned to `_parse(x) >= now - PAIR_WINDOW` and `t.count = len(t.at)`, as `_pair` does — the same in-place prune, no new key and no save of its own. The same-seat branch reads through it too. `_pair` and its `setdefault` stay for the write after the landing.
- **On a refusal only**, for the refusing addressee alone (the loop raises at the first, as today): `bound_hit = True` on every tally the two sums read whose count is above zero (in the same-seat branch, on the two tallies where each exists), each of those records saved, then raise.
- **The refusal string** keeps today's template, `"{x} and {y} have exchanged {limit} messages replying to nothing inside {PAIR_WINDOW}: the send is refused — write the user_attention.md line yourself (design §4.10)"`, the numeral `{limit}` (the sum may pass it). `x` and `y` name each end: a seat record (`seat` set — an own record or an extra, resumed or not) by the seat's name, any other record by its id, as today. The seat's name is the expression `seat_records` already computes (`scale["line"]` for an extra, else the record's `name`): lift it into `mail.seat_name(r)` and use it in both, so the two never diverge.
- The tallies are still written where they are now (the sender's `pair:<sid>` and `sid`'s `pair:<sender>`, after the landing); only the read sums. The thread branch is unchanged (§4.9: the thread bound needs no sum between a session and a scaled seat, and stays per record otherwise).

Tests beside the seat suite (`tests/test_td_max_*.py`): a sender's reply-less asks spread over a seat's own record and an extra are refused once their sum reaches `PAIR_BOUND`, though neither record's own tally is at it; the refusal names a seat record by the seat's name, with or without extras, and a record that is no seat by its id as today; `bound_hit` lands on each read tally with a count and a record with none gains no `pair:` key; an extra writing to the sender counts in the same budget; mail between two records of one seat counts once, on their own tallies; a resumed extra and a resumed sender keep their count; a record that is no seat behaves as today.

**Done when:** `_check_bounds` counts a pair's reply-less entries over both ends' seat records as §4.9 *Mail to a scaled seat* says, and the tests above pass.
