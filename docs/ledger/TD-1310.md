**Why:** Paul (2026-11-02, with a screenshot of the live page, **ao-grind** picked): "lets change the queue progress bar to adjust to the current set of filters. Lets also move the bar itself down so its top aligns to the height of the top of the "Needs you" box. Would it make sense to have the progress bar include the "needs you counts"?" With one team picked the bar and its legend still read *3 in progress · 60 waiting · 167 up next · 230 cards*, the whole Queue's, while the groups below show that team's cards with *(n of m)* shares, so the bar describes cards the page is not showing. TD-1293's design chose this on purpose (the summary replaced a sentence that counted the whole Queue); the live page shows that it reads wrong. The **Needs you** number (*159*) and *Decide 158 · Do 43* are the whole Queue's too (`q.n`, drawn once by the server and redrawn by `qDrawCounts` from `#qcounts`, never by the picks). The bar also sits higher than the switch beside it: `.qhline` centres its items, and the 6 px bar sits at the top of the `.qsum` column above its legend, so the bar's top is above the switch's border.

Paul asked how *159*, *Decide 158* and *Do 43* relate. They count different things. **Needs you** counts **cards** whose status is *needs you* (`_status` in `src/sessionorc/queue.py`: a row with `Owner: paul` or `decision (paul)`; or `_mail_status`: any Decide or Do item attached that is not a running `steer`). *Decide* and *Do* count **items** on those cards, not cards (`want_of`: Decide is an `ask`, a `steer`, a `decide` or `look` board line, a `decision (paul)` entry; Do is an `act` board line, an `Owner: paul` entry, a blocked outcome; a red PR is left out of the header). Those are Inbox mail and board lines attached to cards, plus the person's ledger rows. One card can carry several items, and one item can make a card *needs you* while the card counts once, so the numbers neither add up nor bound each other. The page does not say so beyond the *Decide · Do* hover.

**Fix:** design first, as a UI round with Paul (agree the mockups before model review rounds):
- The summary counts the cards the picks (teams, repos, **Needs you**, find) leave shown: the bar's segments, the legend and *n cards* (e.g. *of 230* while a pick is on, as the group shares do). The browser recounts them on a pick, as it recounts the shares (TD-829). **Needs you n** follows the team and find picks too; the switch's own number ignores the switch's state.
- The bar drops so its top lines up with the top of the **Needs you** switch's border, and the legend stays under it, at desktop width and in the narrow layout (C only; moot under D).
- The bar shows the needs-you share as a thin needs-colour mark inside each segment, as wide as that group's needs-you cards (Paul, 2026-11-02, recommended for the round). It is not a fourth segment: *needs you* cuts across the three groups (a needs-you card can be in progress, waiting or up next), so a segment would count those cards twice. The round settles the mark's shape (a sliver along the top or bottom edge, or a hatch) and its hover.
- The heading line's *Decide · Do* is dropped, leaving the **Needs you n** switch alone (Paul, 2026-11-02: *I wonder if the "needs you" count is enough and we drop decide vs do — I am not sure having those separate adds much*; a leaning for the round to confirm). This also removes the cards-versus-items puzzle. Only the page header's count goes: a card's own Decide and Do (its header and pill, §4.5 screen 12 *Three wants*, TD-909) stay. The round checks what else reads `n.decide` and `n.do` (`#qcounts`, `qDrawCounts`, tests) and words the switch's hover so that it says it counts cards.
- **Where the bar sits** (Paul, 2026-11-02, after answering the asks with B, then C, relayed by ao-shiftlead-queue-top: *put the filters above the bar, so it is plain that they set its counts*): mockup D (`docs/mockups/reviews/2026-11-02-td1310-d-{1600,400}.png`) draws one control row, **Queue**, the team and repo chips, the switch and find, wrapping as the chips do, then a rule, then the bar and its legend on their own line across the width, directly above the groups. It reverses part of TD-1293's agreed layout (the summary on the heading line, the chips on a row under it), and it makes the alignment with the switch moot. Paul picks C or D.
- §4.5 screen 12 and the §4.5a row say what the build draws. The build entry covers the template, `qDrawCounts`, the CSS, the mockup and the tests that hold the counts.
