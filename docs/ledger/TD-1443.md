**Why:** Asked by Paul through the person's session `ao-led-tds-ledger` (m-f4de57b098b7): a seat whose model is limited or hung must never block progress.

Evidence, 2026-11-06. `fact-check-ao-1` (Gemini, unattended) sat for about a day on Gemini CLI's *Usage limit reached… Keep trying / Stop* dialog, marked limited. Overnight, `ao td add` routed an ask to it as the question seat (m-e5f321db6cd6), and that ask is still unanswered. After Paul pressed **Keep trying**, its footer read *still on it… 36h 21m*: one request hung for a day and a half. Its brief's *usage limit → summary and exit* never fired, because the CLI's own retry loop caught the limit first. The seat was also 69 commits behind main and on an outdated Gemini CLI (0.66.0, current 0.67.0).

The routing, read from the code: `_question_seat` (`src/sessionorc/agent_mail.py:205`) skips only a `closed` or superseded record. A limited, hung or exited seat still receives asks.

Addendum from Paul (m-5bea108c7323, 2026-11-06, 11:15 MST). After **Keep trying**, `fact-check-ao-1` hit *Usage limit reached for gemini-3.8-flash* again, and every further Keep trying re-pops the dialog. The Gemini CLI signs in with a Gemini API key (`security.auth.selectedType = gemini-api-key`). So this is a per-model API quota, the free tier's daily one, which resets at midnight Pacific, and not a short rate limit. Retrying spends the quota and never clears it within the day. The seat's `/model` is *Auto*, which may keep choosing the capped model.

**Fix:** Design first. Evaluate these approaches for fit with this issue and with ShiftLead in general, along with any others the designer finds:
- **(A) Route around.** Routing skips a seat that is limited, hung or exited. Its waiting asks are re-routed, to the techlead or to a subagent that answers them.
- **(B) Fallback profile, per seat and opt-in.** When the seat is limited, it relaunches on another profile (for example Sonnet) until the reset, then switches back. Off for a seat whose point is testing a model, as `fact-check` is for Gemini. Its manual form exists today: **Switch profile** on a limited session (§4.2).
- **(C) A seat as an ordered pool (Paul's idea).** A seat holds members in priority order, for example 1 Gemini, 2 Sonnet. Work goes to the first member able to take it: not busy, not limited, not otherwise unable.

Also in scope: tell an hour-scale rate limit apart from a day-scale quota. On a quota, the seat goes straight to its next member or its fallback rather than waiting in the retry dialog, and a model choice such as *Auto* that keeps picking the capped model is part of the reading. Then a hung request is a state the home acts on, not *working*. A detector exists: `working` reads `stalled?` after `STALL_AFTER` (20 minutes) with no output (`src/sessionorc/agent_tick.py:5079`, `agent_common.py:51`). Find out why it did not mark `fact-check-ao-1`, likely because the CLI's retry loop kept producing output or hook events, and build on `stalled?` rather than adding a new state. A request is hung when a long *still on it* runs with no output, or a tool's own retry loop holds the limit dialog. Say how each adapter reports it (§4.3), since Gemini CLI's retry hides the limit from the brief. Note the seat's lag behind main and its outdated CLI as separate checks to consider: a seat's checkout and its tool version.

**Done when:** A design round, with Paul, picks among (A), (B), (C) and others and says what reads a request as hung. Its builds carry `Part of: TD-1443`. A test: an ask routed while the question seat is limited reaches an able member, or the techlead. Another test: a request held in a tool's retry loop past the hung bound reads `stalled?` (or what the design names), not *working*.
