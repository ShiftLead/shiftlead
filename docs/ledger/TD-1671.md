## TD-1671: A scaled seat's pair and thread bounds are tallied per record, and the design does not say whether they count over the seat

**Priority:** Low
**Type:** debt
**Added:** 2026-11-11
**Owner:** designer
**Kind:** design-first
**Status:** Open — in design: build TD-1728. Class: in-repo (two section files and one function in this repo, nothing another repo runs); cap `design-rounds` (4). The pair bound counts over a scaled seat's records; the thread bound needs no sum. Rounds running; record docs/reviews/TD-1671.md.
**Id take:** a8a4df28de0a42e78e6d0c68ed39e5ef
**Location:** design §4.9 Mail to a scaled seat, §4.10 A bounded exchange; src/sessionorc/agent_mail.py _check_bounds, _pair

**Why:** A scaled seat's mail goes to one of its records (§4.9 *Mail to a scaled seat*, TD-1547), and *one open question per sender per seat* is counted over the seat's records — but the pair bound (`PAIR_BOUND` replying-to-nothing entries per pair inside `PAIR_WINDOW`) and the thread bound (`THREAD_BOUND`) are tallied on whichever record took each send (`agent_mail._check_bounds`, `_pair`, the tallies in `Session.threads`). A sender writing to a seat with extras spreads its pair tally over them, so the seat as a whole takes up to `max` times the pair bound from one sender; a thread a record holds is counted on that record alone. §4.9 says only that *each record's mailbox depth is its own*; it is silent on these two bounds. Met in TD-1547's slice 4 review; left out of TD-1547's Fix, which names only the open-question count.

**Design PR:** #3570

**Fix:** Decide in §4.9 *Mail to a scaled seat* (and §4.10 *A bounded exchange*) whether a sender's pair tally and a thread's tally with a scaled seat are counted over the seat's records (`mail.seat_records`) or per record as now, and say it; if over the seat, a build entry `Part of:` this one makes `_check_bounds` sum the seat's records' `pair:<sender>` tallies and read the thread's tally on any of them.

**Done when:** §4.9 says how the pair and thread bounds count with a scaled seat, and the code does what it says (a build, if it changes anything).
