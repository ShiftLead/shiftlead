**Why:** TD-1491's design (§4.7 *Feedback*) gives the `/shiftlead-feedback` procedure a deterministic extractor that condenses a seat's transcripts over a window into text an agent reads. It reads through each tool's adapter, never a tool's files from core. The 2026-11-06 review the procedure replaces read Claude Code's and Gemini's files by hand.

**Location:** design §4.7 *Feedback*, §4.3 `conversations`, `marks_errors`, `read_transcript`, §4.4a NODE_READS, §4.8a; src/sessionorc/adapters.py (TranscriptEntry, Conversation); src/shiftlead/adapters/claude_code, gemini; src/sessionorc/agent_common.py (NODE_READS, ACT_TIMEOUT); src/sessionorc/agent_remote.py (_route_read); src/sessionorc/identity.py (READS); the RPC handlers; src/shiftlead/cli.py; tests/test_transcript.py, tests/test_identity.py

**Ask:** Held with its design TD-1491 until Paul answers its end-of-rounds item (Go, One more round or Hold) on docs/user_attention.md.

**Fix:** Build §4.7 *Feedback*'s extractor and §4.3's additions:

- **§4.3**: `TranscriptEntry.error` on a `tool` entry, which `to_dict` carries only when True. The Claude Code adapter sets it in `read_transcript` from the tool result's `is_error`. The class attribute `marks_errors` is True for Claude Code, and False where unset; Gemini CLI's is TD-1719. Add the optional `conversations(cwd, profile) -> list[Conversation] | None`, where `Conversation` is `(id, first_at, last_at, size)`, oldest first, read from each file's head and tail only. Claude Code reads the profile's `projects/<munged cwd>/*.jsonl`. Gemini CLI reads the top-level chats of the projects whose `.project_root` names `cwd`. It joins `OPTIONAL_METHODS`, so each adapter declares it in `implements` and the conformance suite (TD-112) tests it; a file with no timestamped entry is left out.
- **RPCs**: add `conversations {id, since, until}` and `transcript_digest {id, conversation, profile, since, until, zone, skip}`, both added to `NODE_READS` and to `identity.READS` (never gated).
  - `conversations` asks every profile the adapter's `profiles()` lists (the record's own alone where it has none; the record's own asked whether or not `profiles()` lists it; a profile answering None read as none, the record refused only when its own answers None, *<id>'s profile <name> is unknown to the <adapter> adapter*). Both RPCs read their files through `asyncio.to_thread`, as `rpc_transcript` does. It keeps one entry per `id`, preferring the record's own profile. It refuses a shell with *a shell has no transcript*, and answers `{marks_errors, conversations}`. Both are bounded by one constant, `DIGEST_TIMEOUT = 300` s in `agent_common.py` beside `ACT_TIMEOUT`, by RPC name: the handler wraps a record of its own host's read in `asyncio.wait_for(DIGEST_TIMEOUT)`, the CLI calls them with `_timeout=DIGEST_TIMEOUT + CALL_TIMEOUT`, and the home's `_route_read` forwards them to a node's record with it, not `ACT_TIMEOUT`, the node serving them as `rpc_conversations` and `rpc_transcript_digest` through `_read`. A node forwards no read: at a node, another host's record is *no session*. Agent adapters without the method are refused, by its `implements`, *the <adapter> adapter lists no conversations (§4.3)*.
  - `transcript_digest` takes `profile` as required. It pages `read_transcript` back by `before`, 100 turns a page, until it holds a dated entry older than `since`.
  - Each line form is as §4.7 lists them, rendered in the caller's `zone: {name, offset}` (the offset where the name is null or unresolvable), with `since` inclusive, `until` exclusive and the first `skip` entries at `since` dropped:
    - day lines;
    - `HH:MM` leads and a kind mark (`>` a prompt, `<` the assistant's text, `✗`, `·`, `—`), with continuation lines indented; the CLI finds a prompt by its mark;
    - a prompt cut to three lines;
    - whole text;
    - a `✗` line's `<call>` cut to 120 characters, its result kept to three lines or 300 characters, and `→ (no result)`;
    - collapsed calls, led by the run's first call's `HH:MM`;
    - the subagent line, never counted in a run, and a lone `sidechain` entry's;
    - compaction;
    - no thought.
  - An undated entry, of any kind, takes the instant before it, and an entry dated earlier than the entry before it takes that entry's instant: each entry's instant is the latest of its own and those before it, from the oldest entry the read holds, and the window, `skip`, `next`, day lines and `HH:MM` all use it (a step back past where a read stops, or an instant differing between calls because their reads stop at different entries, is an accepted rare repeat or loss). A reply that would pass 4 MiB as JSON ends at the last prompt before that point, else at the last entry before it. Every entry is written to 64 KiB at most, in bytes as written (lead, indents and the *(cut)* included), cut at a character boundary, then *(cut)*. A reply that is cut always holds at least one entry (it may write no line); a window with no entry, or none that writes a line, answers `{text: "", lines: 0, next: null}`. That prompt or entry opens the next reply. `next` is then `{at, skip}`, `skip` counting the window's entries at that instant consumed, written or not, from the instant's first entry (so including the `skip` given when `at` is the call's `since`); otherwise it is null. `next.at` keeps the entry's own precision (RFC 3339 with its fraction), and the CLI's `since` equals it on that value. A day line is written only before an entry's line, so a reply that writes a line opens with its first written entry's day line; the CLI always writes the day line after a conversation header (a continued part's included) and drops a reply's opening day line only when it equals the last written under the same header.
- **CLI**: the `ao feedback` parser, whose bare form prints usage and exits 2, and `ao feedback digest <seat>… --since <when> [--until <when>] --out <dir> [--part-bytes N] (bytes, default 250000, under 65536 refused; a part always holds at least one entry) [--skip <conversation>]… [--list]`.
  - `<when>` is a local day or an instant. The CLI resolves `--since` and `--until` (a local day or an RFC 3339 instant; `--until` now) once to UTC instants, sends `zone` (`TZ`, else `/etc/localtime`, and the offset in minutes), and writes RPC instants (UTC) local; and waits `DIGEST_TIMEOUT + CALL_TIMEOUT` for each call.
  - `--skip` is repeated, once per conversation, matched by id in every record.
  - The calling session's own conversation, its `SHIFTLEAD_SESSION` record's `adapter_id`, is always skipped where there is one. A conversation an earlier record's `conversations` call returned is listed only under that one, unless that record failed: a failed record lists none, so a later record that shares its conversations lists them, all but its `failed` one, which no later record reads again.
  - The CLI calls again with `next`'s `since` and `skip` until `next` is null.
  - It writes `<dir>/<record id>.txt` (the id as `ao status --json` prints it), or `<record id>.1.txt` and on, split at a conversation header, else the last prompt before the bound, else the last entry before it, never inside an entry. Each conversation goes under its `## <id> · <profile> · <first_at> → <last_at>` header (each time `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM` in the run's zone, as in the `--list` table, the conversation's span, not the window's), written only before its first written line; a part that starts inside a conversation repeats the header with *(continued)* and its day line.
  - It prints the `--json` index §4.7 gives, `{since, until, records: [...]}`. A conversation's `lines` counts every line written for it in all its parts, day lines included, its header lines (the first and each *(continued)* one) left out.
  - A record with none in the window gets no file. A conversation that writes no line in the window (no entry there, or thoughts only) is left out of the file and the index; a record all of whose conversations are left out is listed with none, `parts: []` and no file. A record whose host does not answer, or whose read is refused or passes its bound, on any of its conversations, is listed with its `error`, `failed` (the conversation whose read failed, null when the `conversations` call itself failed; null on a record read whole), `conversations: []`, `marks_errors` null and no file, a part of it already written removed; its read ends at that first failure, and the others go on; the run exits 1. Every record with no file has `parts: []` and `bytes` 0. Before it reads a record the CLI removes that record's own `<record id>.txt` and `<record id>.<n>.txt` from `<dir>`.
  - `--list` needs no `--out` and prints one row per conversation (record, id, profile, span as `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM` in the run's zone, size), or each record's `conversations` answer under `--json`, both with `--skip`, the caller's own and an earlier record's left out; the JSON is the index's shape with `size` in place of `lines` and no `parts` or `bytes`. It lists by span, as `conversations` answers, so a conversation that writes no line in the window may be listed and then left out of the digest. A record whose `conversations` call fails is listed with its `error`, `failed` null and none, a later record lists the ones it shares, and `--list` exits 1.
  - It writes nothing outside `<dir>`.
