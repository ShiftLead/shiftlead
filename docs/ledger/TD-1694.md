## TD-1694: A blocked outcome that names the sender's own open question is refused, and every blocked outcome is stamped with what it waits on

**Priority:** medium
**Type:** debt
**Added:** 2026-11-11
**Owner:** grinder
**Kind:** build
**Status:** Open
**Id take:** b9805dbd226d44c0820a06cff27c94f3
**Part of:** TD-1556
**Location:** design §4.10 *Outcomes*, *Told on Telegram*; src/sessionorc/agent_mail.py (`_msg`, the outcome path), src/sessionorc/agent_notify.py, src/shiftlead/briefs/{designer,grinder,hunter,manager}.md, src/shiftlead/skill.md, tests

**Why:** TD-1556's sender's half. On TD-1529 a designer reported `--outcome blocked` naming its own fresh `ask` (m-998693d16e2a) instead of asking on the answered steer's thread, and the person saw one need as two Decide cards. Design §4.10 *Outcomes* now refuses that at the send, says it when the caller holds an open question the line does not name, and stamps a `blocked` outcome with the open items it waits on, which TD-1695 draws.

**Fix:** Design §4.10 *Outcomes* (the paragraph *A `blocked` outcome is never a question's stand-in*, and *A `blocked` outcome that waits on another open item is drawn under it, and counted once*) holds the rules; build them:
- **The refusal.** In `src/sessionorc/agent_mail.py`'s `_msg`, where `state` is `blocked` and `settle` (or a handed entry, `_owing_question`'s first branch) is found: read the `m-[0-9a-f]{12}` ids in the cleaned outcome line; any that is an open `ask` or `steer` of `self.person_inbox` whose `from_` is the caller refuses the send with §4.10's words, naming the id and the `--for` id in the cure. Nothing is written on a refusal (the order of `_msg`: validate before land).
- **The warning.** Where none is named but the caller holds such an open question, the outcome lands and the reply's `warnings` gains one line naming them (`m-…, m-…`) with the same cure.
- **The stamp.** The `outcome` written by `_mark` gains `waits_on`: the named ids, in the order named and without repeats, that are open `ask`s or `steer`s of the person inbox at the send (any sender but the caller, whose are refused), else `[]`. Only a `blocked` outcome carries it. `MailEntry.outcome` (`src/sessionorc/models.py`) is a plain dict, so the field round-trips as it is.
- **The briefs and the skill.** `src/shiftlead/briefs/designer.md`, `grinder.md`, `hunter.md`, `manager.md` and `src/shiftlead/skill.md` each carry the outcome rule: say in each that more direction after an answer is a new `ask` on the question's thread (`ao msg person --kind ask --thread <id> …`), never `--outcome blocked` beside a new ask, and that `blocked` is for work that waits on something else only the person can move (grinder.md and hunter.md already say the first half, and skill.md says `--thread <its id>` asks again: add the second to each; designer.md, whose outcome line says neither, and manager.md gain both). `manager.md`'s is what a manager reads of its members, so it says the rule a member follows, not one it sends.
- **Not told while folded.** `src/sessionorc/agent_notify.py` raises a `blocked:<id>` row for every `blocked` outcome, a person-inbox question's and a handed entry's (§4.10 *Told on Telegram*): skip one whose `outcome.waits_on` names an entry of the person inbox that is still open, so the person is told of the item it waits on and not twice; one with an empty or absent `waits_on` is told as today.
- Tests in `tests/` beside the outcome tests (`grep -ln "asked_again" tests/*.py`): refused when the line names the caller's own open ask, and own open steer; lands with the warning when the caller holds one unnamed; lands with `waits_on: [id]` when the line names another sender's open ask, `[]` when it names a closed one or none; the handed entry's outcome takes the same refusal; a `blocked` outcome whose `waits_on` names an open ask raises no `blocked:` notify row, one whose `waits_on` is all closed or empty does.

**Done when:** `ao msg person --outcome blocked "…m-X…" --for <id>` is refused with the thread cure when m-X is the caller's own open question to the person, lands with a `warnings` line when the caller holds one the line does not name, and stamps `outcome.waits_on` with the other senders' open questions it names; the five brief and skill texts say a follow-up is an ask on the thread and never `blocked` plus a new ask; the tests above pass.
