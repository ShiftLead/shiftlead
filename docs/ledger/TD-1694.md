## TD-1694: A blocked outcome that names the sender's own open question is refused, and the person's Reply on a blocked outcome is recorded

**Priority:** medium
**Type:** debt
**Added:** 2026-11-11
**Owner:** grinder
**Kind:** build
**Status:** Open
**Id take:** b9805dbd226d44c0820a06cff27c94f3
**Part of:** TD-1556
**Location:** design §4.10 *Outcomes*; src/sessionorc/agent_mail.py (`_msg`: the outcome path, the person's reply), src/sessionorc/agent_notify.py, src/sessionorc/agent_inbox.py (`rpc_inbox_dismiss`), src/shiftlead/briefs/{designer,grinder,hunter,manager,techlead}.md, src/shiftlead/skill.md, tests

**Why:** TD-1556's sender's half. On TD-1529 a designer reported `--outcome blocked` naming its own fresh `ask` (m-998693d16e2a) instead of asking on the answered steer's thread, and the person saw one need as two Decide cards. Design §4.10 *Outcomes* now refuses that at the send and warns when the caller holds an open question the outcome does not name. It also records the person's Reply on a `blocked` outcome. *Reply answers it and the row leaves* was written and never built: nothing stores the reply, so the row stays.

**Fix:** Design §4.10 *Outcomes* holds the rules, in the paragraphs *A `blocked` outcome is never a question's stand-in* and *`blocked` is not a dead end*. Build them:
- **The refusal.** In `src/sessionorc/agent_mail.py`'s `_msg`, once `state` is `blocked` and `_owing_question` has found the entry (a question, or a handed entry from its first branch):
  - The caller's open questions are the open `ask`s and `steer`s of `self.person_inbox` whose `from_` is the caller or a record on the caller's `superseded_by` chain, read as `rpc_msg_withdraw` reads the sender.
  - Read the `m-[0-9a-f]{12}` ids in the whole `text` as sent, before `_clean` caps it.
  - An id that is one of those questions, or a person-inbox copy closed `rerouted` whose thread's open copy is one of them, refuses with §4.10's first words. The words name that open question's id and the `--for` id as the `--thread` target.
  - Where the open question's `root` is the settled entry's `root` (a seat's `--thread` question about a handed entry), refuse with §4.10's second words (*wait for its answer*).
  - An answered or closed id refuses nothing. Nothing is written on a refusal.
- **The warning.** Where the text names none of them but the caller holds such an open question, the outcome lands. The reply's `warnings` gains one line per open question, with the second words for one on the settled entry's own thread and the first words otherwise.
- **The reply recorded.** A person's `reply` whose `reply_to` is the id of an entry carrying a `blocked` outcome makes `_mark` add `replied: {at, by}` to that outcome on every copy, `by` being the reply's id. A reply to another entry on the thread stamps nothing. A reply to a closed question is accepted today (`closes` is False, and it goes to `from_`), except for an orphaned one, which stays refused, so its way out is Dismiss. `MailEntry.outcome` (`src/sessionorc/models.py`) is a plain dict, so the field round-trips as it is. `src/sessionorc/agent_notify.py`, which raises a `blocked:<id>` row for every `blocked` outcome (a question's and a handed entry's), skips one carrying `replied`.
- **Dismiss tells the asker.** `src/sessionorc/agent_inbox.py`'s `rpc_inbox_dismiss` writes the `dismissed` outcome and sends the asker its `system` note only for an entry that `owes`, plus the handed branch. A question whose outcome is `blocked` owes nothing (`MailEntry.owes`), so its Dismiss tells nobody, though `inbox_row.html` says *is told*. Send it the same `system` note, keeping the `blocked` outcome as it is recorded, which the dismissal already hides.
- **The briefs and the skill.** Each of these carries the outcome rule: `src/shiftlead/briefs/designer.md`, `grinder.md`, `hunter.md`, `manager.md`, `techlead.md` (the handed-entry rule, *`blocked` with what stopped you*) and `src/shiftlead/skill.md`. Say in each that more direction after an answer is a new `ask` on the question's thread (`ao msg person --kind ask --thread <id> …`), never `--outcome blocked` beside a new ask. Also say that `blocked` is for work that waits on something else only the person can move. Per brief:
  - grinder.md and hunter.md already say the first half, and skill.md says `--thread <its id>` asks again: add the second half to each.
  - designer.md, whose outcome line says neither half, and manager.md gain both. `manager.md` is what a manager reads about its members, so it states the rule a member follows.
  - techlead.md says that a question about a handed entry goes on its thread and that the entry's outcome waits for its answer.
- **Tests,** beside the outcome tests (`grep -ln "asked_again" tests/*.py`):
  - refused when the text names the caller's own open ask, its own open steer, or a rerouted copy's id;
  - refused when it names an open question sent by a record the caller superseded;
  - the second words for a handed entry's own-thread question;
  - not refused when it names the caller's answered question, or another sender's open ask;
  - lands with the warning, its words by thread, when the caller holds an open question it does not name;
  - a person's reply with `reply_to` the blocked entry writes `replied`, and one to the outcome note does not;
  - a replied blocked outcome raises no `blocked:` notify row;
  - Dismiss of a question's blocked row sends the asker a `system` note.

**Done when:** Each of these holds, and the tests above pass:
- `ao msg person --outcome blocked "…m-X…" --for <id>` is refused with the thread cure when m-X's thread holds the caller's own open question to the person.
- It is refused with *wait for its answer* when that question is already on the outcome's own thread.
- It lands with a `warnings` line when the caller holds an open question the text does not name.
- The person's Reply on a blocked outcome writes `outcome.replied`, and that outcome is no longer told.
- The four briefs and the skill say a follow-up is an ask on the thread, never `blocked` plus a new ask.
