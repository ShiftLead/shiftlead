## TD-083: A worker that ends its run on purpose parks its team

**Priority:** Medium
**Added:** 2026-09-20 (the anchor session; found by `orchestrator-ao-1` at the end of ao-grind's first run and written up in its summary and on the board, PRs #274 and #276)
**Owner:** anchor
**Kind:** live-check #323
**Status:** Resolved 2026-10-06. **Step 2 (the chip) built 2026-09-20 (`tdgrind-ao-2`, PR #323):** the **restart wanted** mark on the card and the Focus header, shaped like *out of work* and following its rules — fixed words, the `why` on hover because it is a sentence a card cannot hold, never pressable, not a state, and one malformed record costs that card its chip and not the grid. **It draws `early` differently**, which is the one thing the design did not have to say and the field does: a controller does not act on an early one, so a chip that looked the same would promise a restart that is not coming; it reads *restart wanted · early*, in the needs-you tokens, and its hover says why it is for a person. Partly done — **designed 2026-09-20**, **step 1 built 2026-09-21 (PR #317, `tdgrind-ao-1`)**: `ao progress restart --why` sets `restart_wanted: {at, why, early?}` on the session's own record — declared only by the session it is about, refused without a reason, refused while an outcome is owed to the person (the fresh run does not carry the conversation the debt was made in, so nobody is left who can report it), and refusing `none` as `none` refuses it; a later declared claim clears it, since the session went on after all. `RESTART_EARLY` is thirty minutes: a restart asked inside that of the record's own start still stands — the word is the session's — but carries `early: true`, and a controller puts an early one on the board rather than acting on it. `ao status -v` prints it, and it is in the wake digest, so a lead's `ao wait` returns on it: that is what makes it a thing a manager acts on rather than a field nobody reads. Step (2), the chip, is `tdgrind-ao-2`'s and was blocked on this. **Step (3) built 2026-09-21 (the anchor), after the promote that made the verb live**: `docs/briefs/tdgrind-ao-1.md` (and so `tdgrind-ao-2.md`, which follows it), `docs/briefs/orchestrator-ao-1.md` and the presets `grinder.md`, `hunter.md` and `lead.md` — a worker ends a run with one of the three words and only then the summary; a lead restarts a member that wants it from the record's fields (`early` → the board; suspended → left alone; dirty or unpushed → one send, then the board; else `ao close` and the crash rule's `ao new`, one ceiling), counts it as not finished, and the exemption for *a written end-of-run summary* is gone — the twenty-minute send names the three words. **They take effect at the next team start**; the running `ao-grind` sessions were started on the old briefs. **One gap the briefs showed, left open here:** a hunter whose area is *covered* (quiet, not gone) stops with no word — §4.9a gives it none, `none` would be false and `restart` is not it — so under *declared, never inferred* its lead will ask it; whether *covered* needs a word of its own is a design question, not decided. The design takes the first option whole and half of the second: a third declaration, `ao progress restart --why` → `restart_wanted: {at, why}`, acted on by the controller inside the one restart ceiling; and *a summary is not a declaration* in both briefs. Making a submitted `/exit` an exit was **not** chosen — it has the adapter read what a session typed. Build: (1) the RPC, the field, its refusals (no reason; an outcome owed; `none` already declared), the early-declaration guard (`RESTART_EARLY`, recorded as the sub-field `restart_wanted.early`), `ao status -v`, and `wait` waking the lead on it — tdgrind-ao-1; (2) the chip — tdgrind-ao-2; (3) the two briefs and the three preset briefs, which take effect at the next team start. Not the same gap as TD-053's, which is a worker that *declared* itself out of work. **Next:** all three steps built; the *done when* is a live run.
**Location:** `docs/briefs/orchestrator-ao-1.md` (the round's state rules), `docs/briefs/tdgrind-ao-1.md` (*Shutdown*), `src/sessionorc/agent.py` (`_out_of_work`, the `progress` channel), `src/agentorc/adapters/claude_code/` (what a submitted `/exit` is), design §4.8 and §4.9a

**Why:** 2026-09-20, 18:36 UTC: `tdgrind-ao-1` ended its run deliberately after ten merged PRs — its context was long, the ledger still held pickable entries, and it said a restart with fresh context would pick up cleanly. It could not say so in any way its manager can act on. It was not **out of work**, so `ao progress none` would have been a lie and it rightly did not send it. A Claude Code `/exit` does not leave the session (TD-053 records that), so it was never **`exited`**, and the manager's restart rule fires only on `exited`. It was `idle` with a written end-of-run summary, which the brief says is *not* idle-with-open-work. Closing it is reserved for wrap-up. So the manager put a steer to Paul, the steer lapsed at its bound to its default — park — and the team did nothing for ninety minutes with work on the ledger, until a person acted: the anchor session, on Paul's word, ran `ao team stop ao-grind --close` (about 19:57 UTC; the worker settled and was closed), then closed the manager by hand — its own submitted `/exit` had not left either, so its name was still held — and ran `ao team start ao-grind` at about 20:10 UTC. (That sequence is the anchor session's own record of 2026-09-20; the board PRs #274 and #276 record the parking and the wrap-up, not the restart.)

Two things are missing, and the design round chooses between them or takes both:
1. **A worker cannot say *restart me*.** A third declaration beside `done` and `none` — the run is over, the lane is not — that a manager may act on by closing the record and starting it again under the same name and brief, inside the restart ceiling (three in two hours). It is a structured field, never text the manager parses.
2. **A submitted `/exit` is not an exit.** Either the adapter makes it one (the session is closed when the tool has plainly been told to leave and has settled), or a summary without a declaration does not count as an ending and the manager's idle rule applies to it.

A member that declared `none` stayed finished: grinder-ao-2 after #1146 sat idle and was never restarted. An early `restart` went to the person, as designed: grinder-cm-1's of 2026-10-06 at about 04:09 local, read from its pane and from `ao status -v`, where its card read *restart wanted (early: nothing reported done this run)*. An early one writes no restart line to the journal. One restarted run, grinder-ao-1's at 22:38, came back without its brief. That is TD-341 (built) and TD-342 (designing), not this rule.

**Done when** a worker that ends its run with work left is back at work, with fresh context, without a person — and one that is truly finished still is never restarted.
