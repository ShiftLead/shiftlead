## TD-096: Focus hands a person the keyboard of an unattended session, and typing into one lands in the middle of its work

Before: **designed 2026-09-21 (the anchor; design §4.5 screen 2 *Focus watches*, the §4.5a rows *read-only terminal / Take over* and *Hand back*, the composer's Send row, the mode toggle's Focus-header names, §4.6 *A read-only attach*); the build is a free-pick for a worker with the page (`src/agentorc/ui/`: the attach, `pump`, the Focus header and composer) — no `src/sessionorc` change is needed, since Take over is the existing `set_mode` and invariant 5 already refuses a controller's acts on an interactive target.** What the round settled: read-only for **every** unattended session, whatever its state (an idle worker's next prompt may arrive from its controller at any moment, and the interleave is the same harm); Take over changes only the mode — a stop time, a sent wrap-up, `out_of_work` and `restart_wanted` stay written and are not acted on while the person holds it, and Hand back returns them to the policies and the manager as they are; a permission is answered from a read-only Focus (the hook, not the keyboard); on a phone it is the same page; the interactive side is untouched. The Focus header's toggle takes the name of what it does — **Take over** / **Hand back** (or *Switch to unattended* where there is nobody to hand back to); the *more* entries keep their *Switch to…* names. The read-only rule is the server's (the attach drops key frames), not the widget's. **Still to check in the build:** the manager's presets (`src/agentorc/briefs/manager.md`) and this repo's `docs/briefs/manager-ao-1.md` do not yet say in words that an `interactive` member is left alone and is not a crash or a stall — the code refuses the acts, the brief should say why the refusals are expected (the repo's own brief is the anchor's). Proposed by the anchor and accepted by Paul 2026-09-21 (*takeover / hand back: yes*). Paul asked whether, since interacting with an unattended session disrupts its job, the manager should help it recover or **Focus** should be called what it does. The anchor's proposal: neither — **looking is not the disruption, typing is**, and the design already has the structured form of *I am taking this over*: flipping a session to `interactive` (§4.5a, the mode toggle), which puts it out of its controllers' reach on their next call (§9 invariant 5). So: **Focus watches** — on an `unattended` session the terminal opens **read-only**; one control, **Take over**, gives the keyboard and flips the session to `interactive` in the same act, so its manager stops nudging it and nothing else types beside the person; **Hand back** flips it to `unattended`, and its manager's next round picks it up as any member. A quick question without taking over stays **Message…** — mail, which types nothing. A manager never infers that a session was disturbed: it reads the mode, a field. **Next:** what is left is Paul's use of it and the anchor's `manager-ao-1.md` line.
**Location:** design §4.5 screen 2 (Focus: *keyboard passes through*), §4.5a (the Focus header's rows, the mode toggle), §4.6 (the terminal bridge — read-only is the bridge not forwarding input), `src/agentorc/ui/` (Focus), the manager's briefs (a member that turned `interactive` is left alone — check they say so)

**Why:** today Focus passes the keyboard through for every session, so a person who opens a working grinder to look, and types a question, has their words land in the middle of whatever it was composing — and nothing tells its manager, which may nudge or restart it while the person is mid-sentence. The techlead trial made the question concrete (a seat that answers a batch and ends is the worst session to type into), but it holds for every unattended session.

**What the design round has to settle:** whether read-only is the default for every `unattended` session or only while it is `working`; what **Take over** does to a pending wrap-up, a stop time and a `restart_wanted`; whether a permission prompt can still be answered from a read-only Focus (it is answered through the hook, not the keyboard — so yes, but say so); how it reads on a phone; and the interactive session's side — nothing changes there.

**Done when** the design says it, Focus on an unattended session cannot type by accident, taking over and handing back are one press each and are fields a manager reads, and Paul has used it.
