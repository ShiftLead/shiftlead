**Why:** The Queue tells the person a team is stuck when it is the person's own session sitting idle. On 2026-11-11 TD-1529's card read *td-1529 stalled* beside its holder pill and *0/5 done stalled* on its row (Paul, through `ao-led-tds-ledger`, m-cbdbd7f7761e). Its holder is `ao-shiftlead-td-1529`, an interactive session (`unattended: false`) Paul started to go through the td-max design; it has been idle since 2026-11-07 with a clean worktree and nothing ahead of main, and holds a **derived** claim on TD-1529 read from its branch `td-1529` (§4.8 *Reports*: the `tdNNN-*` shape, `reports.BRANCH_RE`).

This is what the design says today, not a slip in the code. §4.4 *A row's state* gives *with you in <name>* only to a row whose entry waits on the person (`decision (paul)`, or `Owner: paul`); *stalled* is any claimed row whose holder reads idle or stuck. Its clause *so an idle interactive holder is never stalled* sits inside the waits-on-the-person case. `queue._state` follows that: once Paul's Go lifted TD-1529's `decision (paul)` (2026-11-11), the row fell to `_state_word`, and `_holder_stuck` reads an idle interactive record as stuck. *Stalled* says a team is stuck, for its techlead (§4.4); a person's own idle session is the person away from the keyboard, not a stuck team, so the word sends the person looking for a fault that is not there.

Two more things Paul raised with it:

- **The word does not take the person to the session.** A row's holder pill already links to the holder's Focus (`queue_row.html` macro `holder`), but the state word beside it (*stalled*, *with you in <name>*, a stopped holder's *needs you*) is plain text, and on a *with you in* row the pill is not drawn at all. Paul's ask: wherever a card shows a holder state word, the word, or its pill, opens that holder's Focus terminal.
- **A design conversation's derived claim outlives the design.** TD-1529's builds (TD-1545 to TD-1548) are filed and Go is given, yet the interactive session that ran the design conversation still holds the design entry by its branch name, so the card reads as if that session still owns the work. A derived claim lasts while the branch is checked out and its PR is not merged (§4.8), and a design entry stays open until its last build lands.
