## TD-1230: manager-ao-1.md sends an observed problem to Paul, and the on-call template sends a seat's steers to the techlead; the repo's part does not say which wins

**Priority:** Low
**Type:** debt
**Added:** 2026-10-29 (grinder-ao-2, from the techlead's read of #2663)
**Owner:** grinder
**Kind:** build
**Status:** Resolved — the observed-problem line sends its steer to the techlead, as the template routes every steer
**Location:** `docs/briefs/manager-ao-1.md` (:9, *A problem you observed*); `src/shiftlead/briefs/manager_on_call.md` (*What needs someone else*)

**Why:** in its read of #2663 (TD-1224), the techlead noted that `manager-ao-1.md:9` sends a problem the seat *observed* that no ledger holds (a member editing another's worktree, say) as a `steer` to Paul. The on-call template it fills says *a `steer`, and an `ask` about the work, go to* the team's techlead, and to the person only where the techlead is `none`. ao-grind has a techlead. The repo's part may mean to override the template, since a member misbehaving is arguably about the member and not about the work, and the template sends a member still stuck after one send to the person too. But it does not say so, so a filled seat reads two rules for one case.

**Resolved:** 2026-10-29 (PR #2672). Design §4.9b *What it may answer* has the techlead answer any `steer` or pass up what is not its to answer, and nothing in §4.8 or §4.10 sends a member's conduct around it, so the repo's part follows the template: `manager-ao-1.md`'s *A problem you observed* now sends its steer to the techlead and cites the template's routing.

**Done when** `manager-ao-1.md`'s observed-problem line names its recipient in agreement with the template, or says that it overrides it.
