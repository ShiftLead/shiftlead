## TD-1845: Text a person reads is plain, opens with where it is, and every answer says who acts: one rule for every writer, checked by code

**Priority:** High
**Type:** feature
**Added:** 2026-11-14
**Owner:** designer
**Kind:** design-first
**Status:** Resolved 2026-11-16
**Id take:** e92b4edb865742f58e6689e16f0886d0

**Why:** Paul, 2026-11-14, through his session `ao-led-tds-ledger` (notes m-3a1cc2da4139, m-f435a39efd3b, m-4ce0991e19a6 and ask m-e42f0f0a2b13): "How do we get our TD descriptions to be more human friendly? I believe I have raised this in other TDs before." He has: TD-1801's "all text on the Queue and its items reads for a person", and TD-1816's issue-form warning ("tough for a human to read"). Each was fixed as a one-off; this entry makes it one rule that every writer follows, checked by code rather than by a model's judgment.

Two cases from 2026-11-14. (1) TD-108: a grinder's steer to the techlead seat reached Paul's Queue as one dense paragraph of file names, function names and plan text numbers. It was written for a seat, not for him: in his words, "probably great for someone who is already tracking what grammar and a line start are - but to me they are gibberish until i know what grammar, what line is being talked about." The techlead's one-line recommendation under it was the clearest part. (2) TD-1648's decide was well formatted, but its answers did not say who acts: did *Pinned, run it again* mean Paul edits `~/.shiftlead/profiles.yml` himself, or that a session does it on his answer?

Paul switches between projects and repos; the sessions are deep inside one problem. Text he reads has to catch him up first. Today the design has the shape (design §4.10 *How a message to a person is written*, TD-127; *An item leads with one sentence*, TD-909) but only warns, and checks nothing about code in the lead, who acts in an answer, or orientation.
