**The anchor's recommendation.**
- **It is a ladder, not a new mechanism.** A worker's question goes to its **controller** first, never straight to the person; each level answers it or passes it up, and the person is the top. Mail already marks who spoke (`[controller]` / `[person]`), so a go-between's answer can never read as Paul's.
- **Start as a function of the lead, with the lead on the stronger profile** — a role's `profile:` is already per role (`org.yml`), so *grinders on Sonnet, lead on Fable* is configuration today. The lead already holds the context the answers need (lanes, members, the ledger). **No rename**: `orchestrator → lead` landed days ago and old records still carry the old badge; nothing keys on a role (§9 invariant 9), so the word costs only churn.
- **Split it out only if the rounds cost too much.** A lead reads status every round; on the strongest model that is the expensive part and the least technical. If it shows, the go-between becomes its own optional role — woken by mail, holding **no `control` grant** (it answers; it acts on no session), which is also the least privilege — and the lead drops back to the cheap profile.
- **What it may answer:** *steering* (a default existed; the worker would have gone on anyway), and a *needed* question **only when the answer is already written down** — the design, the ledger, a brief, a decision of Paul's — **citing where**. Everything else goes up **with a recommendation and suggested answers** (TD-070), so the person's part is one press. **Never**, whatever it believes is obvious: anything destructive, outward-facing, spending, credentials, or a change of scope.
- **Everything it answers for the person is told to the person** — an FYI, *answered for you: the question, the answer, the source* — skimmable and overrulable, with a count on the team's card. The failure this guards against is a confident wrong go-between steering a team all night unseen.

**Paul's decision, 2026-09-19 — one exception to the recommendation above.** *The current lead role does not need a premier model — handling the lifecycle of an agent is something Sonnet could handle. Acting as the go-between needs a model that is higher trust, like Fable, and we don't want to waste its tokens doing grunt work.* So the go-between is **its own role from the start**, not a function of the lead, and the *split it out only if* bullet above is overtaken: lead and grinders on the cheap profile, the go-between on the strong one, woken by mail and otherwise costing nothing. The anchor agrees, and the rest of the recommendation stands (the ladder, what it may answer, the never-list, *answered for you*). What the separate role has to settle, in its design round: **(a) it starts cold** — it does not hold the lead's context, so a question put to it must stand on its own (the question, the options, the asker's default, where it looked), which is the `steer` envelope and TD-070's suggested answers doing their job; **(b) who may instruct it** — mail from a controller reads as instruction, and a cheap-model lead must not be able to instruct the high-trust one on what to answer, so its `controllers` is the person alone, and the lead's part is lifecycle only, through `ao team stop`; **(c) the mail edge** — workers must be able to `ask` and `steer` a teammate that is not their controller; **(d) its context grows all night** and a wake after the cache has lapsed pays for all of it, so its brief keeps answers short and it may be better started per batch than kept; **(e) one account, one usage window** — on the same account as the grinders it is capped when they are, which is exactly when questions pile up; **(f) its name** — not *lead* (taken, and renamed days ago); the anchor suggests **`advisor`**, Paul to choose.
