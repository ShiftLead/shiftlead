**Why:** Every end-of-rounds card reaches the person, even clear-cut ones. Paul, 2026-11-16, approved this entry (*"file"*): he wants to decide far-reaching designs (a new seat or flow) but not routine ones (*add a button that does X*). **Fix:** Designed in §4.9c *Who ends a design's rounds*, §4.9b *It judges a design's verdict* and §4.4 *A delegated answer*, after agent-cadence TD-281 and TD-288 (the counted verdict and its judge):
- the techlead seat is the judge of a design's counted verdict: it confirms or raises it by the cadence's fixed judge prompt, never choosing among outcomes; the cadence's default judge stands in where the seat cannot judge;
- a routine design's confirmed Go is the judge's Go, its builds filed with no item and the person told by a note; a far-reaching design's end-of-rounds item is the person's;
- what is far-reaching here is the designer's mark (a design whose PR changes a section that says who acts for the person) and `docs/cadence.toml`'s `[far-reaching]` paths (the briefs, roles, flows, glossary and org schema); listing `"**"` there sends every design to the person, so there is no `design_go` key in settings.yml;
- a seat answering a far-reaching design's item for the person waits on TD-1910's `decide` delegation.
The entry's first ask (a `design_go` setting, the techlead choosing among four outcomes, a delegated Go record) is the Log's 2026-11-18 and 2026-11-19 lines; builds TD-1960 (the briefs) and TD-1961 (the `[far-reaching]` table).

**Done when:** The techlead judges a design's counted verdict, the default judge standing in where it cannot; ShiftLead's `[far-reaching]` table is written, so a far-reaching design's Go reaches the person and a routine design's judge's Go reaches no one; listing `"**"` there sends every design to the person.
