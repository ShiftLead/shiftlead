**Why:** A design's cost is the author's own turns, above all the turns that apply each review round's fixes on Fable at 140k to 300k tokens of context; the reviewers are a few percent of it. The analysis of 3,120 findings from 41 review records (docs/decisions/2026-11-11-design-review-findings.md) says where the rounds' work goes: 16% of findings need no model, 13% are a false claim about what exists (two thirds of them found after round 1, and at least 177 of the 394 in text a fix had changed, since a later round's fact-check reads only what changed), 37% are detail a builder would meet anyway, and 25% are the hard situations (two actors at once, interaction with another rule, restarts, endings, timing, actors, error paths) where a design review finds what nothing else would; a quarter of all findings follow from an earlier fix. Paul holds two Fable limit resets that lapse in March and wants the Fable allotment spent on what needs Fable (Paul, 2026-11-11, session fabplan: *I think this is the right shape*).

**Design PR:** #3524

**Ask:** Will you let the plan for a cheaper way to design (PR #3524: Fable drafts, a pre-review script and a Sonnet seat carry the rounds' fixes, tried first on three replayed designs) go to its designs and builds once its review round has ended?

**Fix:** The plan is docs/decisions/2026-11-11-fable-reset-plan.md (PR #3524, a draft until its review round ends and Paul says go). Design, then file each as a build with `Part of:` this entry:

1. The pre-review script (agreed by Paul): called by the author, it must pass before any reviewer reads. Its checks, largest first: one statement in two places drifted apart, a path or symbol that should exist, entry fields out of step with the design, counts, ids, section references. Open: whether the author gives it the terms to look for or it parses the PR.
2. The author's checklist, distilled from the general questions in docs/decisions/2026-11-11-design-review-findings/patterns.csv and weighted to the hard situations; and the rule that a claim about code is checked as it is written, in the draft and in every fix.
3. A second, Sonnet seat that collates a round's findings and applies the fixes from the design's branch and review record, and hands the hard findings back to the Fable designer seat as one narrow question each; reviewers name the situation a finding is about, which routes it.
4. The trial: three past designs replayed from the commit before each design's PR in worktrees never merged (one short, one of median length, one long), their review records the answer key; then the next real designs on the new shape, read against the baseline in the analysis.
5. A reading of the Fable limit on the home, beside TD-1589's fix of the week's reading.

The review rounds keep their cap and their stop rule. Rule changes to the rounds go upstream to agent-cadence as its own entry (`ao td add --repo agent-cadence`). The messaging review and what each role is made to read are in the plan and get entries of their own when Paul starts them.

**Done when:** Done when the three replays and the first real designs on the new shape are read against the baseline (findings only a design review would catch for each design by round, rounds to the stop rule, the share of findings following from a fix, each seat's spend for each design), and Paul has said whether the new shape stays.
