## TD-1848: The briefs teach the person-text rules before the writers refuse them

**Priority:** Medium
**Type:** feature
**Added:** 2026-11-14
**Owner:** grinder
**Kind:** build
**Status:** Open
**Id take:** 18f0b66eed9c4046a9645b23a8656c81
**Part of:** TD-1845

**Why:** TD-1845's design (design §4.10 *Text a person reads is checked at its writer*): writers should meet the five rules before a writer refuses them, and the parts no check can see — which larger piece of work, what the named thing is for, why the decision matters, a coined term explained or left out — are the briefs' to teach.

**Location:** src/shiftlead/briefs/ (techlead, manager, manager_on_call, grinder); docs/briefs/; the ao --skill text

**Fix:** Each brief that writes to the person — the presets `techlead`, `manager`, `manager_on_call`, `grinder` (src/shiftlead/briefs/) and this repo's designer and grinder briefs (docs/briefs/) — says the rules where it now says the message shape, **replacing** its *second paragraph, what each answer leads to* clause (the consequences now ride on the answers, each `<label> — <consequence>`): the first paragraph is one plain sentence of at most 25 words with no code, a question ending in `?`; every answer, and a steer's `--default`, says who acts (*I'll…*, *you…*, *a session…*) in its consequence after ` — `; the second paragraph opens the context, its first sentence naming the repo and the larger piece of work in plain words, then what the named thing is for and why it matters, a coined term explained or left out; ids, files, functions and finding numbers go behind the link. It does not name `--question`, which TD-1846 adds to the techlead brief with the flag. The `ao --skill` text's mail section says it in one line.

**Done when:** Every preset brief and docs/briefs/ brief that sends to the person states the five rules and `--question`; the brief tests (`grep -ln briefs tests`) pass.
