## TD-1848: The briefs teach the person-text rules before the writers refuse them

**Priority:** Medium
**Type:** feature
**Added:** 2026-11-14
**Owner:** grinder
**Kind:** build
**Status:** Open
**Id take:** 18f0b66eed9c4046a9645b23a8656c81
**Part of:** TD-1845

**Why:** TD-1845's design (design §4.10 *Text a person reads is checked at its writer*): writers should meet the five rules before a writer refuses them, and the parts no check can see — which larger piece of work, what the named thing is for, why the decision matters, a coined term explained or left out — are the briefs' to teach.

**Location:** src/shiftlead/briefs/; docs/briefs/; src/shiftlead/skill (ao --skill)

**Fix:** Each preset brief that writes to the person — `techlead`, `manager`, `grinder` (src/shiftlead/briefs/) and this repo's designer and grinder briefs (docs/briefs/) — says the five rules where it now says the message shape: the sentence is one plain sentence of at most 25 words with no code, a question ending in `?`; every answer says who acts (*I'll…*, *you…*, *a session…*), its consequence after ` — `; the context's first sentence names the repo and the larger piece of work in plain words, says what the named thing is for and why it matters, and explains a coined term or leaves it out; ids, files, functions and finding numbers go behind the link; and a techlead passing a question up to the person writes `--question`, the asker's question in one plain sentence. The `ao --skill` text's mail section says it in one line. Keep each brief's existing message-shape sentence, extended, not a second one.

**Done when:** Every preset brief and docs/briefs/ brief that sends to the person states the five rules and `--question`; the brief tests (`grep -ln briefs tests`) pass.
