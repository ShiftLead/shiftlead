### 4.9b The techlead: a go-between for what would reach the person (TD-075)

The techlead is a role of its own on a high-trust model: it filters what would reach the person,
answers what is obvious, and passes the rest up; a manager's lifecycle work does not spend its
tokens. **It is a ladder, not a new mechanism**: a worker's question goes to the techlead where its
team has one, the techlead answers it or passes it up, and the person is the top. The preset brief says what to do when `ao` refuses a verb it names.

- **The seat.** A team definition may carry **`techlead: {name, profile, brief, home, context}`**
  beside `manager:` — optional, one per team, a session and never `person`; `name` defaults to
  `<team>-techlead`; it takes no `role:` (the seat is the role) and no `grants:`. The preset
  **`techlead`**: brief `techlead.md`, no lane, **no grants**, icon `book`, label *Tech Lead*. It
  carries the `team` badge, so every member may message it and it them (§4.10 *sideways*) — no
  new mail edge. A session of another team, of any project, may put an `ask` to it, and it replies
  on that thread (§4.10 *to a question seat*, TD-995): `ao msg --team <team>` or `--repo <repo>` finds
  it without its id, and another repo's ledger request comes to it as an `ask` (§4.7 *Entries*),
  carrying `entry` but not `handed`, which it judges as it judges any question: it drafts and lands the
  entry by PR, or replies why not, and owes the person no outcome for it. A member's and a manager's brief take **`{techlead}`** — the seat's session id,
  filled at launch as `{lane}` is, `none` where the team has none — and say: *a `steer`, and an
  `ask` that is about the work, go to `{techlead}`; with no techlead, to the person.* `ao team
  start` creates the manager, then the seat with `controllers: [manager]`, then the members, and
  fills `{techlead}` in every brief with the id the seat will take, worked out before anything
  starts (§4.1's `ao-<scope>-<name>`, `@<host>` for a team on another host); should the seat come
  up under another id (a stale tmux session holding it), the start says so. A hand-started
  session (`ao new --role grinder`) reads `none`. `ao team list` names the seat, and a team whose
  members all declared reads *wound down* without the seat's word: the seat is known by the name
  its definition gives it — or that name with the numeric suffix a stale tmux session forces,
  unless a member is defined under it — never by a role badge.
- **Seats with a trigger (TD-098).** The techlead is the first seat, and its trigger is a
  question landing. A team definition may carry more, under **`seats:`** — each `{name, role,
  trigger, brief, profile, home}`, a session and never `person`; a seat's role is never `person`,
  `manager` or `techlead`; `trigger` is required and one of **`asks`** (the techlead's, and any
  review seat's — §4.9c *A review stage any seat may hold*: a question landing, `asks_waiting`
  leaves zero), **`prs: <n>`** (a whole number from 1: n PRs merged to the team's
  repo since the seat last came — its record's `created`, or the team start when it never has — a
  count the home's tick reads from `gh`, §6 rule 3's `seat_count`), or **`every: <duration>`** (in
  `m`, `h` or `d`; since it last came, timed by the home's tick, §6 rule 3), or **`swept: <duration>`** (the
  same units; once the repo's board's `Swept:` stamp is that old, or absent — a sweeper's, *The sweep*, below);
  **`prompt`** is the org's director
  seat's alone, never a team's (*The director seat answers permission prompts for the person*, below):

  ```yaml
  techlead: {name: techlead-ao-1, context: docs/briefs/techlead-context.md}   # the first seat: trigger `asks`
  seats:
    - {name: docs-audit-ao-1, role: auditor, brief: docs/briefs/docs-audit.md, trigger: {prs: 10}}
    - {name: test-audit-ao-1, role: auditor, brief: docs/briefs/test-audit.md, trigger: {every: 6h}}
  ```

  **The seat rule is the same for every seat, and it is the tick's** (§6 *Keeping a team running*,
  rule 3): `ao team start`
  writes the trigger on the seat's record as `seat: {trigger}` — the techlead seat's as `seat:
  {trigger: asks, tags: true}`, which makes it a candidate for the repo's tagging seat (§4.4 *An
  entry names its team*, TD-730: due `by: tag` while an entry of the repo needs a team) — the tick computes `seat_due` from
  it, a seat that is `exited` or `closed` with `seat_due` set is filled (`create` with
  `keep_mail`, so a question that was waiting is still there), the ceiling of six fills an hour
  (`FILL_CEILING`) is over all seats sharing a controller, and a seat that is `idle` with no
  `seat_due`, or with a `prs` or `every` one met while it ran (the next fill's cause), is closed
  as the techlead is. A fourth trigger, **`team`**, is the manager's alone,
  written by `on_call` on `manager:` (the `manager` role's default, §4.9) and refused under `seats:` (§6 rule 3 *A manager on
  call is a seat of this rule*). A seat runs its brief and ends on
  its own — it declares nothing (§4.9a), holds **no grants** (it files and opens PRs with `gh`,
  which is not an act on a session), and is not counted in a wind-down. `ao team start` starts
  each seat after the techlead with the manager as its controller and no grants;
  `teamrun.seat_names` names every seat, not only the techlead; `ao team list --json` carries
  each seat's `trigger` and `after`, as its record's `seat` does, which is what the tick fills them by. **`auditor`** is a
  preset like any other, with a built-in brief — hunter-shaped by default (finds and files with
  evidence, its `findings` on its record, never fixes; a brief may make it grinder-shaped and open
  the PR); the area (docs, tests) is the brief's, and a repo's area briefs (*docs-audit*,
  *test-audit*) are its own to write; the label is drawn as *Auditor · docs* from the preset's
  `label:` and the definition's name. What a seat's card says while on call is in §4.5 (*on call —
  runs after 10 PRs*), and the count toward a `prs:` trigger is drawn from the tick's
  `seat_count` (*· 4 of 10*, §6 rule 3). Not designed: a seat whose trigger is another seat's findings, and a trigger a
  person presses (a seat is asked by mail, which is the person's way in already).
- **The sweep: a seat that runs the stranded-work procedure (TD-1061).** The cadence asks every repo
  for a weekly stranded-work sweep (cadence §3.11), and it ran only when a person remembered
  `/stranded-work`: on 2026-10-26 the roster's seven boards held 58 open items, 38 past the 14-day
  bound, and two repos had never been swept. Detecting staleness was never what was missing; a
  sweep needs a brief and a place to switch it on, which the seat rule above already provides.
  - **The preset.** **`sweeper`** is a built-in seat preset (`kind: seat`, no lane, no grants, the
    worker's context, label *Sweeper*), with a built-in brief, `sweeper.md`. The brief runs the repo's
    stranded-work procedure from where agent-cadence keeps it — `agent-cadence path
    procedures/stranded-work.md` in a pinned repo, the synced `docs/procedures/stranded-work.md`
    otherwise — and copies none of its steps; it runs the deep mode when the board's `Swept-deep:` is
    older than the cadence's `deep-sweep-every` (or absent), else quick, as the procedure says. A team
    adds one with one `seats:` line, `{name: sweep-ao-1, role: sweeper, trigger: {swept: 7d}}`, with
    no `brief:` (a `brief:` replaces the built-in one, as for any preset).
  - **Its trigger** is **`swept: <duration>`**: the seat is due once the repo's board's `Swept:` stamp
    (cadence §3.10) is older than the duration, or the board has none. **The stamp is read from origin**,
    not the checkout, since the seat pushes its stamp there and the home's checkout follows only with
    `pull` on (§6 *Pull*): the boards reading already fetches the default branch and reads the board's
    blob on origin (§4.5 screen 6 *Boards are read against origin*), and it derives **`swept: {at, deep_at}`**
    from origin's content whenever that read has it — whatever the checkout's state against origin
    (behind, local, both, or matching, where the two are one) — and after a write-back's press from the
    tree it wrote, carrying it beside the board's `source`. **Only a read against origin writes
    `swept`**: a pass that reads the checkout alone — the clock's plain re-read (`fetch=False`), or a
    due pass whose fetch was skipped or timed out (`fetch_case: skipped`) — carries the last `swept`
    forward unchanged, in `_read_boards`, which holds the last reading, keyed per board by its `root`
    and `label` as the reading lists boards (the cause reads the board whose `root` is the seat's
    repo). An `error` reading keeps the last boards, as it does today: the cause tests the reading's
    `error` first and sets nothing while it stands, whatever `swept` it carries. `swept` is **null** (no reading, no cause) when origin holds no board
    file, or before any read against origin; an `error` reading sets no cause (below);
    `{at: null}` (due) only when origin's file exists without the line. **The parse is the cadence's
    reader's** (agent-cadence `nudge_user_attention.py`, `SWEEP_RE` and `SWEEP_DEEP_RE`, which cadence
    §3.10 pins to the writer), ported verbatim: `SWEEP_RE.search` over the file (multiline), the first
    `Swept:` line that carries a `YYYY-MM-DD` (and `SWEEP_DEEP_RE`'s for `Swept-deep:`), so the seat and
    `/attention` never disagree on whether a board is swept. A match whose date is not a calendar date
    reads as absent (`{at: null}`, due: the sweep rewrites it); a date in the future is not due until it
    has aged `d`. The
    tick's cause and the Sweep card read that one value. It reads the stamp, not the record's age as `every` does, so a sweep a
    person ran by hand counts, and a team that restarts daily, whose seat records are made afresh,
    still sweeps. A board that cannot be read gives no reading and no fill. A cause fills once (§6 rule
    3): after a fill the seat is due again only when the stamp is older than the duration **and** the
    duration has passed since that fill (`seat_filled.at`), so a run that ended without stamping (it
    failed, or was closed) is not refilled in a loop.
  - **What it may change.** It may close a board item only once it has verified the item done, and
    the close says how (the PR or commit read, the file or the state seen) — **never on a ref's state
    alone** (a branch merged or gone is evidence to read, not a verdict: dev-cadence#TD-152 met two
    such false closes on samscrape). An item past the cadence's `escalate-overdue-days` that is
    work, not a decision, it ledgers (a `TD-NNN` from `ao td reserve`, the board line pointing at it).
    It works, as every team session does, in its own worktree, `<repo>/.claude/worktrees/sweep-<team>`
    (§4.9 *Home and reach*); a PR it opens is on a branch `sweep/<date>` off fresh `origin/<default>`,
    made with `gh`, which is not an act on a session and needs no grant, as an auditor's are; it takes a
    ledger id with `ao td reserve`, which every session may run (§4.7). It stamps `Swept:` (and
    `Swept-deep:` on a deep run) by the procedure's stamp-only push (cadence
    §4.3's carve-out), made from its worktree as the carve-out needs it: fetch, a commit holding the
    stamp line(s) alone on a detached `origin/<default>`, and `ALLOW_MAIN_PUSH=1 git push origin
    <sha>:<default>` — never a push of its branch, whose other commits would void the carve-out; a
    push refused because main moved is redone on the new main. Every other change is a PR of its own,
    reviewed and merged as a docs PR. What
    is the person's — a decision, a due date to move, an item it cannot verify — it brings to the
    person as **one** `note` listing them, or as an `ask` per decision with its recommendation first;
    it answers nothing for the person and moves no `Due:` date. It reads every other repo's board
    and never writes one (cadence §3.7: another repo's work is its own sessions').
  - **The switch** (Paul, 2026-10-26: *on (with a duration) would run it routinely on every team
    with the configured model*). **`sweep: {on, every, profile}`** in `settings.yml` (§5), on the
    Settings page's **Sweep** card (§4.5a). A seat is written by `ao team start`, the client that reads
    definitions (the host agent reads none, §4.9, and a fill replays the launch that start wrote, §6
    rule 3), so the switch works as the tagging seat's mark does (§4.4 *An entry names its team*):
    while `on` is true, **`ao team start` adds a sweeper seat to every team it starts**, named
    `sweep-<team>`, its record's seat mark `{trigger: swept, after: <every>, by: settings}`, unless
    the team's definition has a `sweeper` seat of its own or `repos.<repo>.sweep` is `off` (§5) — so
    a team that is running when the switch is turned on gets its seat from its next start, and the
    card says so. **One is filled per repo, not one per team**, since the board and its stamp are the
    repo's and two teams' seats would sweep it twice: the tick computes the `swept` cause only on the
    `by: settings` seat whose team comes first in the repo's order of teams among those live (§4.4,
    the tagging seat's rule; §4.9 *Starting and stopping*), and every other such seat of the repo is
    never due — on call, its card saying *another team's seat sweeps <repo>*. **What wins** is read on
    the records, not a definition: while a live record of the repo carries a seat mark `trigger:
    swept` that is not `by: settings` (a team's own sweeper), no `by: settings` seat of the repo is
    due; and with the switch off, or the repo `off`, none is — each stays on call, drawn *off*, until
    its team's next start writes none. **`every`** is read from `settings.yml` by the tick at the
    cause for a `by: settings` seat (the mark's `after` is the value at the start), so a change holds
    from the next tick; **`profile`** is a launch parameter, written by the start, so a change holds
    from each team's next start. `profile` absent leaves the role's precedence (§4.9 *Roles carry a
    profile*: the member's, the `sweeper` preset's, which sets none, then the default). `every` is
    `7d` when absent, under the cadence's `sweep-every` (10 days by default, §9.18), at which
    `/attention` warns, so a switched-on repo is swept before it is warned about. `ao team start` reads
    the switch through **`sweep_switch`**, a read open to every caller, the person and a session alike
    (a session does run `ao team start`, §4.9; `settings` is the person's own read): it answers `{on,
    every, profile, off: [<repo>…]}` and nothing else of `settings.yml`, none of it secret. A home it
    cannot reach, or a refusal, gives the team no sweep seat, and the start says so in one line
    (*sweep: the switch not read — no sweep seat*). It is a session-callable read (`identity`'s reads,
    beside `gate`), and a node serves it from its replica of `settings.yml` (§4.4a *Settings,
    replicated*).
    **Live** is the tagging seat's (§4.4): the team has an unattended, supervised, unsuspended
    session live. **A stop** leaves the team's seat a card until Forget, never due, so the repo's next
    live team's seat becomes the one due; a team that starts later on a repo already swept by
    another's seat gets a seat that stays on call until it is first. **A restart** makes the seat
    afresh, so its `seat_filled` is gone; the cause still reads the stamp, which the last pass wrote,
    so a restart does not sweep again sooner. **The fill ceiling** (§6 rule 3) counts a sweep seat's
    fills with its own team's other seats, which share its controller; two teams' sweep seats share
    none, and only one is ever due.
  - **What a sweep seat's card says** (§4.5 seat cards; one list, which TD-1090 and TD-1091 draw):
    on call and due-able, *on call — sweeps when unswept 7 d · swept 3 d ago* (or *· never swept*);
    on call and not the repo's, *on call — another team's seat sweeps <repo>*; off, *off — the sweep
    switch is off for <repo>*. The two held-back lines read the seat's `seat_count`: on a `by: settings`
    seat that is not due-able, the home writes `{swept: off}` (the switch, or its repo, off) or
    `{swept: other}` (another seat sweeps the repo) there, beside §6 rule 3's `{prs, at}`, and clears
    it once the seat is due-able. The Settings page's Sweep card's per-repo lines are §4.5a's.
  - **A repo no team serves** gets no sweep from the switch: the home starts no session in a repo
    without a team (cadence §3.7). The Sweep card lists each registered repo with
    its stamp's age and what sweeps it — *sweep-ao-1 (ao-grind) · swept 3 d ago*, *no team: never
    swept* — so the person sees what nothing sweeps, and runs `/stranded-work` there or gives it a team.
- **The fact-check: a seat another tool fills (TD-857).** Every doc-only PR gets a fact-check against
  the repo before it merges (cadence §4.8), and each author runs it as a subagent of its own tool on
  the repo's `review-model`, so the Claude window pays for every one, and no session of another tool
  does real work (Gemini CLI's adapter, §4.3, is proved only by its conformance walk). The
  fact-check is the job a lesser model on a free account can hold: it reads and never writes, so a
  wrong verdict costs a re-read and never the tree; its input is one diff and the files it cites;
  and its verdict is checked claim by claim, so it can be measured against Claude's on the same PRs.
  - **The preset.** **`fact-checker`** is a built-in seat preset (`kind: seat`, no lane, no grants,
    the worker's context, label *Fact-check*, icon `eye`), with a built-in brief, `fact-checker.md`. A team adds
    one with one `seats:` line naming a profile of any adapter, `{name: fact-check-ao-1, role:
    fact-checker, profile: gemini-reader, trigger: asks}`. Its trigger is the techlead's, a question
    landing, so it is filled by an ask, answers every ask waiting in one fill, and is closed idle as
    the techlead is. It holds no paths, so it is no flow's review stage (§4.9c) and merges nothing.
    Its fills count toward the ceiling of six an hour (`FILL_CEILING`, §6 rule 3) that it shares with
    the other seats under its controller (the techlead's among them, where the team has a manager);
    it is not exempt. A fill the ceiling holds back leaves the ask waiting, and an ask that expires so
    reads `none · fills exhausted`, the record's own word (§6 rule 3). `never filled` stays for a seat
    with no fill on record.
  - **How it is asked.** The author sends **`ao msg --kind ask --bound 1800 --about '#<n>' <seat>
    "fact-check PR #<n> at <sha>: <the claims>"`**, briefed as cadence §4.8 briefs any reviewer: the change's
    load-bearing claims, the figures it has not checked, each to be re-derived. The ask carries **no
    `--pr`**: a fact-check is no reader's read, so the green gate (*How the reader hears of a PR*,
    above) and the Repo page's held standing do not apply, and the ask goes as soon as the head is
    pushed. Its `about` is the PR's reference, written by the author, since `about` is free text
    nothing checks (§4.10 *Outcomes*), and the reply keeps it, so the run that posts knows the PR. The bound, 30 minutes, covers a fill on the next tick, a cold
    start and the read of one diff, and the trial's minutes, with its count of `none · working`, say whether it is enough. The asker and
    the seat are of one team, so the ask passes §4.10's edge to a seat, whose
    rule of **one open question per sender per seat** also holds. A sender whose ask is refused
    because its last is still open asks nothing for this PR and posts the trial line `skipped · one
    ask open`.
  - **What it does.** It works in its home, `<repo>/.claude/worktrees/<name>` (§4.9 *Home and
    reach*). It fetches and reads the head by refs (`git show <sha>`, `git diff
    origin/<default>...<sha>`), never the author's tree, and re-derives each claim the ask names and
    each the diff makes. It edits nothing, opens no PR, posts nothing on the PR, and runs no `ao` but
    `ao inbox` and `ao msg`. **Its reply** (`ao msg --reply-to <id>`) begins with the cadence's own
    line, `cadence-review: SHIP|BLOCK · <adapter>/<model> · docs · <n> findings`, whose
    `<adapter>/<model>` is the first line of `AGENT_MODEL_FILE`, else `AGENT_MODEL`, unchanged (both
    already hold that shape, §4.2a), else `<adapter>/default`. A profile with `model:` unset has no
    `AGENT_MODEL`, and its file is written only once a turn has set the record's model. Then comes each finding: the claim, where it
    bites (file:line), and what the repo says. So the line can stand as the PR's evidence as written,
    and the cadence's reader takes it unchanged. A head it cannot read (not on origin) is `BLOCK`,
    with that as its one finding.
  - **The trial: beside, never instead.** The author's own fact-check stays the PR's review and its
    `cadence-review:` comment the evidence (cadence §4.8–§4.9), and the merge never waits on the seat.
    The trial comment is posted by the author while it runs, rung by the reply. If the author has
    exited or closed, the reply waits in its mailbox, which moves with the name (§4.10's addressee
    table), and the name's next run posts it, where that create keeps the mail (*The name coming back
    adopts it*) and if it comes within a day of the close (`CLOSED_KEEP`). An ask whose bound runs out
    closes **`expired`**, which delivers nothing and wakes no one (§4.10 *One way of being closed*; *How the sender hears that one closed without a reply* lists no note for it).
    The author learns of it from its own next `ao` reply after the bound and posts `none` then.
    **The seat's brief answers only open asks** and skips one its inbox shows closed `expired`. A
    late reply, were one sent, is read and never posted, so the trial stays one comment per PR
    either way. A PR with no `fact-check-trial:` comment is *no line*; a `none` is a line. The run
    that posts compares the reply with the
    author's own reviewer's findings, as the PR's evidence comment and its FIXED follow-up record
    them, and posts **one comment** on the PR, merged or not. For a reply, the comment's first line
    is **`fact-check-trial: SHIP|BLOCK · <adapter>/<model> · caught <c> · missed <m> · false <f> ·
    <minutes> min`**, then a sentence for each missed or false finding. With no reply it is
    `fact-check-trial: none · <reason>` or `fact-check-trial: skipped · one ask open`, with no cells,
    and the evaluation reads the cells from `SHIP` and `BLOCK` lines alone. The run that posts re-checks each of the seat's findings against
    the asked `<sha>` itself, by refs, whichever run it is. *caught* counts the seat's findings it confirmed.
    *missed* counts the other reviewer's findings, confirmed by the evidence comment and its FIXED
    follow-up, that the seat did not raise. *false* counts the seat's findings it found wrong. The minutes run from the ask to the reply. For an
    ask that expired with no reply the line is `none`, its reason the seat's state at the bound, from
    its record in `ao status --json` (`limited`, fills exhausted, never filled, still `working`). **The comment carries the string `cadence-review:` nowhere**,
    not even quoting the seat's reply, since the cadence check reads that line from any line of a
    comment (cadence §4.9): the seat's verdict is the trial line's first word. The tally is these comments, read
    with `gh` on the team's repo, and nothing else keeps one.
  - **Who, and how long.** On `ao-grind` the trial covers every member's doc-only PR (the
    grinders' and the designer's; a seat's own PRs keep their reviewer), with the rule in the repo's
    own briefs (`docs/briefs/`). It runs until there are 20 `SHIP` or `BLOCK` trial lines, or for 14 days
    from the first trial line's date, whichever ends first. Then the designer's evaluation reads the lines
    and asks the person to choose one of three, recommendation first. **Alone**: the reply's line is
    posted as the evidence and the author's own check is dropped. **Beside**: the trial goes on.
    **Back**: the seat line is removed. Under *alone*, the author posts the seat's reply, its
    `cadence-review:` line first, as the PR's evidence comment, which the cadence check reads as it
    reads any (its verdict `SHIP` or `BLOCK`, its model and count free text). It recommends *alone* only where three things hold, each pooled over
    the trial: misses are at most one in ten of the confirmed findings (*caught* plus *missed*);
    the PRs with at least one false finding are at most one in five of the PRs with a `SHIP` or
    `BLOCK` line; and `none` lines
    are at most one in five of the asks with a line (`SHIP`, `BLOCK` and `none`; `skipped` sent no
    ask, and *no line* is left out).
  - **The profile, and the quota.** The seat runs on **`gemini-reader`**, a Gemini profile of the
    person's (§4.2a), whose home is a `config_dir` of its own, never `gemini-conformance`'s (the
    walk's). The person signs it in to a free Google account in a terminal. Its `model:` is unset, so
    it runs the tool's default, the model the walk held on (TD-786). The free tier's limits are the
    account's, read and never assumed: the quota's box makes the record `limited` until it is
    answered (§4.3 *The limit dialog is read off the screen*). Nobody in the team answers it: the
    record stays `limited` and is neither closed (a seat closes `idle`) nor refilled, so every ask
    after it lands, waits and expires, each trial line reading `none · limited` (or `skipped · one
    ask open` for a sender's second PR inside its first ask's bound), until the person
    presses *Stop* on the box (the seat then reads `idle`, is closed as the techlead is, and the
    next ask fills it afresh). The person sees it as the seat's `limited` card. The trial so
    measures how often the seat answers as well as what it answers, and the evaluation counts a
    `limited` stretch by the asks it swallowed. Once the seat stands alone, an ask that expires is the author's to cover: it runs its
    `review-model`, and its evidence line says the seat did not answer.
- **No anchor seat (retired 2026-10-15, reversing TD-381).** **A seat never runs in a main
  checkout**: the one agent there is the **anchor session** (§9 invariant 2), the person's own
  session when it is open, and no team creates a session in a checkout. Every job the seat had has
  a taker that works from a worktree, since each ends as a branch and a PR, the promote is a
  person's press or the home's policy (§6 *Promote*), and §6 *Pull* keeps every registered checkout
  current while it is idle:
  - **work orders** — a decided board line (`board:<key>`, §4.4 *Board write-back*) and a red PR
    nobody holds (`pr:<n>`, §4.4 *A red PR nobody holds is a work order*) — are on **the grinders'
    pick list**, first, as they are today where a repo has no seat (TD-380, TD-613);
  - **builds and live checks** are `Owner: grinder`, a live check picked once its build is live
    (*A live check is a grinder's*, below);
  - **an evaluation** is written as a build whose *Fix* is the write-up, or as `design-first` for
    the designer where its result is a design;
  - **a decision owed** to the designer is the entry's `Kind: design-first` (§6 rule 6); no `decision (designer)` or
    `decision (anchor)` is written;
  - **host chores, `org.yml` and credentials** are the person's (`Owner: paul`), and a decided `act`
    whose chore a session may do is a `board:` order like any decided line.

- **A UI change is verified by its builder; the techlead reads what the builder cannot settle;
  what is left for the person is a look, sent as mail (TD-290).** **One rule: a look a
  machine can settle never waits on the person** — a label reads so, a button is greyed, a toast
  says its words.

  **The builder verifies, before its PR, on a scratch home.** The session that changes a page
  (anything under `src/shiftlead/ui/`) runs the change and looks at it: a host agent and a UI
  from its own worktree, on an `SHIFTLEAD_HOME` of its own on a short path, a tmux server of its
  own and a port of its own, with fixture sessions under the `shell` adapter and a fixture board
  — stood up and torn down by one script, `scripts/look_home.py`, which starts the host agent
  itself, in a child of its own, on a private socket as the suite's fixture does, so that the home,
  its tmux server and its port are made and torn down as one, and nothing of the look reaches the
  default server. The brief's rule is
  therefore *never run the host agent or `ao ui` against the live home*, and the script is the
  one way to run them. The builder drives the page in a headless Chromium under Playwright —
  load, wait for the scripts and the terminal, read values, press, take screenshots — and the
  evidence is in its PR: under the heading **UI check** in the PR's body, one line per check
  (what the change says, what was read), and the screenshots committed as
  `docs/mockups/reviews/<date>-td<n>-<what>.png`, cropped to the change, at most four. A PR that
  changes a page and carries no UI check is not ready to merge; the brief says so and the
  independent review reads it, no tool does.

  **The live copy is read, never pressed.** What only real data shows waits for the promote:
  the builder leaves a **`watch`** line — the kind's own meaning, a check a session makes again
  before acting — naming what to read, and the member that next reads the board with the
  change's commit live (`ao promote status`) makes it: a headless GET of the page, no press,
  no `ao` verb that acts on a session of the live home, nothing under `~/.shiftlead`; it closes
  the line with what it read, or ledgers what is wrong.

  **A live check is a grinder's once its build is live (TD-320).** A built entry whose *Done
  when* only the running system can show becomes `Kind: live-check`, and it does not wait for
  anyone after a promote.
  - **The entry names its build on its `Kind:` line.** The PR that makes an entry a live check
    writes **`**Kind:** live-check #<n>`** — the kind's word, then the last merged PR of the
    build, or several. Both readers take a field's first word as its value and leave the rest of
    the line alone, so cadence's script reads `live-check` as before; shiftlead's reader also
    reads the `#<n>` after that one word. It is not a field of its own — a header field the
    ledger's `Fields:` line does not declare fails cadence's check on the edit that adds it, and
    a declared field takes fixed words — and not an item of `Blocked by:`, which is cadence's
    line, where an item its script cannot read blocks for good.
  - **Live is read, never declared.** A PR is live when its squash commit on the default branch
    (the commit whose subject ends `(#<n>)`) is an ancestor of the repo's live commit, the
    home's promote reading (`promotes.<repo>.live`, §6 *Promote*). The home reads it with the
    ledger, at the repo facts' cadence (§4.4), and keeps `live: yes | no` on the entry's reading.
    **Unknown is never live**: no PR on the `Kind:` line, a PR whose commit is not found, a live commit
    that cannot be read, or a repo with no `promote:` block — each reads `no`, and the entry is
    where it was before this rule.
  - **The lane.** A pickable live check that reads `live: yes` matches `free-pick` (§6 rule 6),
    and the owner word narrows it as it narrows a build: `[free-pick, owner:grinder]` takes the
    live checks whose `Owner:` is `grinder` or unwritten. **What stays the
    person's says so on its `Owner:` line** — a check whose *Done when* is a judgement only the
    person can make, or one that needs the live home's settings changed. A live check is chosen
    as any entry is, by priority first (§4.8 *Choosing in a free-pick lane*), and claimed as one
    (§4.8), by whichever grinder claims it first. A build that becomes live is new work
    in the lane, told once by rule 6 to a member that declared out of work, and counted by
    rule 8 for a team that wound down. **Where it is listed:** `ao repo` lists
    a live check whose build is live among the pickable entries, marked *live check* with its
    build's PRs, and one whose build is not on a `live-check` line of its own with *waits for its
    build to be live*; the page lists every live check under its own
    *live check* kind, the live ones marked, and one blocked by an open entry under *blocked*
    (§4.4 *Repo facts*). The pick list stays `ao repo`'s pickable rows, since
    cadence's `scripts/ledger.py` cannot tell a live build.
  - **What the grinder does** is the rule above: the live copy is read — `ao status -v`, a
    record's fields, `ao promote status`, a transcript, a headless GET of a page — and never
    pressed; a check that needs a press is made on a scratch home (`scripts/look_home.py`), where
    the merged code runs whether or not it is live. It ends one of four ways, each in a PR that
    touches the ledger alone. **It holds**: what was read is written on the entry and the entry
    is archived with `**Resolved:**`. **It does not**: a new entry says what is wrong, and the
    check is `Blocked by:` it. **It waits for an event nobody can cause** (a usage window's
    reset, a node team's next run): what was read so far and the event are written in the
    Status, and the entry gains a `Waits:` line (`Waits: event — <the event>`, §4.4 *An entry says
    what it waits for*, TD-715), so it leaves the lane until the event instead of being picked
    again each run. **It is the person's to judge**: the grinder sends the look (§4.10
    *A look*), and the entry is `Blocked by: decision (paul)` with the mail's id.
  - **What this does not change.** The promote is still a person's press or the home's policy
    (§6 *Promote*; whether `auto` is on is the person's, and only shortens the wait). No member
    acts on the live home, and a live check gives no grant.

  **A reviewer only if needed, and it is the techlead.** What a browser cannot settle — whether
  a layout or a wording is what the design means — goes to the team's techlead before the merge
  as the ordinary `ask` about the work (`ao msg --kind ask --about TD-NNN {techlead} "…"`),
  naming the screenshots and the design section. The seat reads the images against §4.5, §4.5a
  and the mockup and answers one of three ways: ***matches***, with `--source` — nothing is
  left, and the UI check records the reply; ***does not match: <what>***, with `--source` — the
  builder fixes it before the merge; or ***not written***, with its lean where it has one — the
  person's to judge. It does not pass a look up itself: what reaches the person is the
  builder's own message, one road: on a *not written* reply the builder sends it, with the
  seat's lean as its default. No role, key or mail kind is added, and the seat still answers only from what
  is written, saying where; what is new is that it reads images to do so, and that *not
  written* comes back to the asker as a reply rather than going up as mail — a second
  carve-out from *What it may answer*, beside the reader's verdict on a diff, and for this one
  kind of question alone. In a team with no techlead, what the builder cannot settle is the
  person's.

  **What is left for the person is a look, and a look is mail.** After the merge the builder
  sends the person one message naming its screenshots (`--shot`, §4.7; the envelope's `shots`,
  §4.10): the first paragraph is the one thing that is the person's to judge, then what was
  checked and by whom. There are three cases, and they are the three kinds a person is
  written to in (§4.10):

  | what the checks left | what the builder sends | where it draws (§4.5 screen 6) |
  |---|---|---|
  | nothing: every check held and nothing asks for judgement, or the techlead said *matches* | nothing — the PR's UI check is the record | nowhere |
  | a judgement the techlead leaned on (*not written*, with a lean) | a `steer` — `--default "Works"`, naming the seat's reply, a bound no shorter than a night | **Steering**; it lapses to *Works* with no press |
  | a judgement nobody leaned on, or a state neither the scratch home nor a read of the live copy shows | an `ask` with the answers *Works* and *Not right: <what>* | **Needs you** |

  Nothing new happens at either end. A lapse, a **Go with it** or a *Works* closes the message
  and nothing more is owed: the change is merged and its entry archived already. A *Not right:
  <what>* is a reply the builder acts on as on any answer — it ledgers what is wrong as an
  entry of its own and reports the outcome. A look names its entry in `about`, so a builder
  whose record is closed before the answer leaves an **orphaned question** (§4.10 *A question
  about a reference outlives its asker*), as any does: the `steer` still lapses to *Works*, and
  a *Not right* is written on the repo's board, where the member that next reads it ledgers
  it. No board line is written otherwise: a board **`look`** (cadence §3's kind for a live
  look, §3.5's fixed pair, with no default the board falls to) is how a look is written only in
  a repo or on a machine without shiftlead's mail.

  **Send to reviewer** (§4.5a) is the person's way to the same seat, for an `ask` that reached
  them unread by anyone but its builder: the seat is handed the look, reads its screenshots —
  or, where they do not show what is asked, stands up the scratch home at `origin/main` itself
  — and reports one outcome, whose text is its reading: `--outcome done "<line>"` (*matches
  <source>*, *does not match <source>: <what>*, or *not written*, with its lean), or `blocked`
  where it cannot say;
  the look then returns to the person with that line on it, and the answer stays theirs.
- **It answers cold, and is filled on demand.** A techlead is **started per batch of questions
  and ends when it has answered them**: no context piles up, an idle team costs nothing, and composing
  the message forces the worker to pull the needed context. So a question to it **stands on its
  own**: the question, what was tried, where the asker looked, the default (a `steer` has one by
  construction) and up to four suggested answers (§4.10). `ao team start` starts the seat with the
  rest of the team; it finds its inbox empty and stops. Mail to an exited or closed record is
  delivered and waits (§4.10: an `ask` stays *pending*; a `steer`'s bound runs on and the asker
  goes with its default).
  **A seat is empty or filled — never *finished*.** The techlead is its manager's member in the
  graph but holds no lane, so §4.9a does not count it: it is never *out
  of work*, makes no ending declaration, is not among *every member is finished*, and §4.9a's
  *never sent to and never restarted* is about members that declared.

  addressed to the record, computed by the home like `unread`, **a number and never their text**
  (nobody reads another session's inbox), on every view, printed by `ao status -v` (*asks
  waiting: N*) and carried in `wake_digest`, so a manager blocked in `ao wait` returns when a
  question lands on an empty seat. An entry counts when it is open, an `ask` or a `steer`, and
  names the record in `to` — compared whole, host included, since names are unique per host
  (§4.4a); a copy does not, nor does an entry the record has passed up, **nor a handed entry while a question of the
  seat's own on its thread waits on the person** — a handed entry counts while it owes its outcome,
  open or not (`MailEntry.handed_entry`; §4.10 *An entry handed to a seat*): the seat has nothing to do until the answer comes, so it is closed as an
  idle seat is, and the answer closes the question, the entry counts again and the seat is filled.

  `steer`, answered by the person or by a session — or the `system` note of its `steer`'s lapse or *Go with it*, or of
  its `ask`'s decline (§4.10 *How the sender hears that one closed without a reply*), counts once while it is unread
  in the seat's mailbox, so a seat that closed or exited with a question of its own out
  is filled when the answer lands, under the fill ceiling, and its first `ao inbox` reads it and ends the count — before
  it, such an answer waited in the mailbox for a fill only another question caused, and on contractmatch (2026-10-25)
  none came for a day. It is a cause by the question's id (§6 rule 3), as a question is, and the wake digest's *whether any wait* reads it
  too, so a manager blocked in `ao wait` returns when an answer lands on an empty seat. An answer on a handed entry's thread is not
  counted again: the handed entry, owing its outcome, already counts once the question closes. The wake digest carries *whether* any
  wait, not how many: a manager wakes when the count leaves zero, not on 1→2. A node holds no
  inbox, so the home pushes it the count with the unread hint and a node's own view shows that.
  **The rule for the seat is the tick's** (§6 rule 3), never its manager's: `idle` with
  `asks_waiting` 0 → closed (it writes no code; anything dirty or unpushed in its worktree is left
  open, for the board); `idle` with `asks_waiting` > 0 for twenty minutes → rule 4's one nudge,
  naming the number; **`exited` or `closed` with `asks_waiting` > 0 → filled**: `create` with
  `keep_mail`, same name, directory, worktree, profile and brief. Fills have a ceiling of their
  own — `FILL_CEILING`, six in an hour over the seats that share a controller, then
  `restart_ceiling` with `why: fill`, the card's *fills exhausted* — and do not count as crash
  restarts. A full mailbox (`MAILBOX_DEPTH`) refuses the asker as it refuses anyone, and the asker
  then asks the person (*When it cannot answer*, below). The host agent reads the trigger from the
  record, never from `org.yml`, and a fill is a restart, not a start. At wind-down the seat closes
  as any seat does — idle with nothing due, under rule 3's grace — or by `ao team stop`; rule 9 closes
  no seat, and a standing manager that
  winds down with `asks_waiting` > 0 says so on the board.
  **`--keep-mail`** (`create(keep_mail=true)`): a fresh start under a name forgets the old
  record's mail (§4.1 — *the name now belongs to the new session*), which is right everywhere but
  here, where the mail was addressed to the seat and the new session **is** the seat's next
  holder; so this one start, once the new session has started under the name, moves the
  superseded record's inbox, outbox, tallies, `sends` and wake decisions to it by `_move_mail`
  (built for §4.1's resume) and resumes nothing of the conversation. **It is open to a person, and
  to a session only if it is in the held record's `controllers`** — the manager that created the
  techlead is; a sibling is not — and is refused otherwise, naming the rule. The reason is not
  secrecy (`tail` is a never-gated read, §4.8): **handing a record's mailbox to a successor is an
  act on that record**, and an act on a record is its controllers' and a person's (§9 invariant
  11). It keys on the control graph, not on a role (§9 invariant 9): a person restarting any
  session cold may keep its mail. It changes nothing else about the start. `ao new --keep-mail` sends `keep_mail` only
  when given; the host agent refuses it, each in words, with a `resume` (a resume carries its mail
  already), with no record under the name to keep the mail of, and from a session not among that
  record's `controllers`.
- **Its standing context: a primer.** A techlead starts cold and sees only the asker's framing, and a
  design of this size cannot be read per fill. The seat takes **`context: <path>`**, a file in the team's home checkout that the
  techlead's brief names as its **first read** (`{context}`, filled at launch as `{lane}` is and
  as the path is written; `none` where there is none, and the brief then says to read the repo's
  own map — `CLAUDE.md`, the design's headings — instead). `ao team start` **warns, and starts
  anyway**, when a techlead seat has no `context:` or the file is not in its home checkout (not
  looked for on another host when nothing here can read it); the Teams strip shows the same line
  as a toast. **It is an index, never a source**: an answer's `--source` is the document the
  primer pointed to, read there; a primer is never cited. What goes in: what the project is in a page; its parts and
  which depends on which; who may do what; how a change lands (review, merge rights, what is
  never touched); the questions already decided, each with **where it is written**; and what
  always goes up. What stays out: anything that would be quoted as the answer itself, anything
  that changes weekly (the ledger's contents, who is working on what), and secrets. Two to three
  thousand words — it is read on every fill. **When**: before the first `ao team start` with a
  techlead seat; and again **in the PR that changes** the architecture, a standing decision or
  the merge rules — that PR updates the primer, and its fact-check reads the primer against the
  change. **Who**: a session of that repo with its design in front of it, or the person — never a
  session reaching across from another repo. **Held to its pointers by a test** where the repo can: every section, path and
  ledger id it names must exist (`tests/test_primer.py` here; this repo's primer is
  `docs/briefs/techlead-context.md`). `ao team --skill` and the `techlead.md` preset carry the
  same guidance.
- **What it may answer.** A **`steer`**: it answers, or says *go with your default*, which is an
  answer. An **`ask`**: **only when the answer is already written down** — the design, the
  ledger, a brief, a decision of the person's — and it **says where**: `ao msg --reply-to <id>
  --source "<file and section, or the decision's date>" "…"`; `source` is one line of text, at
  most 200 characters, stored on the reply and only ever drawn as text. It **checks the asker's
  claims in the repo** before it answers. It reads its own sent mail first — **`ao inbox --sent`**: a session's own outbox, its own
  and nobody else's, ungated as its inbox is, marking nothing; a person may read any session's,
  as an inbox — so two questions in one night are answered alike; `--keep-mail` carries the
  outbox with the inbox, and a sent reply that carries a `source` is kept there for seven days
  whatever else is pruned. **Never**, whatever it believes is obvious: anything destructive,
  outward-facing, spending, credentials, a change of scope, a permission prompt, or a question
  the asker addressed to the person by name. Everything else goes up.
  **A member's question stays the member's** (TD-1051): what the seat cannot answer from the record goes up by
  `--pass-up`, whose answer goes to the asker. Where it asks the person something of its own instead — a part of the
  question it cannot pass, a look — it names the member's reference in `about`, so an orphaned one reaches the
  lease holder (§4.10 *A question about a reference outlives its asker*), answers the member with a `reply` on the
  member's thread saying that it asked and naming the question's id — which closes the member's question, so it no
  longer counts toward `asks_waiting` and the seat closes idle while the person decides — and when the answer comes (*an answer waiting*, above, fills the seat for it)
  sends the member what came of it, a `note` `--about` that reference, before it reports the outcome. A member
  that reads a reply naming a question the seat asked keeps its claim and waits for that note, as for any answer
  (the member presets say so).
- **Passing up keeps the thread and the asker.** `ao msg --pass-up <id> --recommend "<one
  line>" [--answer …]` — open only to **the addressee of an open `ask` or `steer`, once per passer
  (the entry gains **`passed_up: <time>`**, the latest pass, and **`passes: [{by, at}]`**, one per
  passer; a second pass by the same passer is refused), and only upward: **to the org's director
  seat where the org defines one** (*The director answers what is passed up to it*, below) and the
  passer is not the director, **else to the person**. The
  `pass_up` RPC (a mail call, routed with `msg`) puts **the asker's own entry** — the same id,
  sender, kind, text, default and bound — in the director's inbox, which makes the seat due
  (`asks`), or in the person inbox: an `ask` under *Needs you*, a
  `steer` under *Steering* with **the time it has left**, since passing up buys no time — with the
  techlead's recommendation and suggested answers beside it, **labelled as the techlead's and
  drawn as text**. That copy's `answers` are the passer's, the recommendation first and no more
  than four in all; the asker's and the passer's copies keep their own, and every copy gets
  `passed_up` and `recommend: {by, text}` — the latest passer's, each earlier one kept in its
  `passes` entry as `recommend`, so a question the techlead passed to the director and the director
  to the person shows the person both, the director's first. The person's ordinary reply goes **to the asker**, is
  copied to the passer (in the entry's `to`), and closes every copy; one press for the person is
  the point. A question passed up and answered **owes** on the asker's outbox copy (`owes` reads
  `passed_up` as it reads the person in `to`), never on the passer's or a copy recipient's — a
  copy in a session's inbox owes only when `handed` (`owes_for`), so those copies delete and age
  out like any closed entry. Refused: a copy recipient, a `note`, a closed entry, a second pass by
  the same passer, the person's own question, a person passing up, and a full person inbox
  (counted against the asker, whose question it is) where the pass goes to the person. `ao inbox` prints *passed up by `<passer>`, who recommends: …*,
  and the Inbox page draws the row (§4.5a).
- **Everything answered for the person is told to the person.** A reply that carries a
  **`source`** is one *answered from the record*, and the home files it to the person as an FYI,
  ***answered for you***: a `note` in the person inbox, filed **from the answerer**, on the
  question's thread, with the answer as its text, the question's `about`, and a structured
  **`answered: {question, asker, answerer, source}`**. It keys on the structured field, **never on
  a role** (§9 invariant 9): a manager that answers with `--source` is told the same way.
  `--source` is refused off a reply, from a person (*a person's answer needs no source*), and past
  one line of 200 characters; the FYI is counted in the person inbox's FYI depths as any FYI is, so
  a full inbox refuses the reply itself rather than let the answer land unseen; a reply **to** the
  person files none. `ao inbox` prints a reply's source and the FYI's *who asked what, who
  answered, from where*. A reply to an entry that carries `answered` is addressed **to the asker,
  with a copy to the answerer**, on the question's own thread — a branch in the reply path keyed
  on the replied entry's `answered`, taken before the ordinary reply-to-sender default and
  applying when the person's reply names no addressee; it is what **Overrule** calls. The Inbox
  groups these under *Answered for you* (§4.5a), uncounted, newest first, and the team's header
  carries the number since the person last opened the group — a **mark**. **Overrule** on such a
  row is a reply **to the asker**, marked `[person]`, on the question's own thread, with a copy to
  the answerer; a person's word outranks a teammate's by the rule every brief already has. **The
  debt (§4.10 *Outcomes*)**: an answer from a teammate creates none — the question was never the
  person's; an **Overrule** does, and needs no new case: the overruling reply is mail from the
  person to the asker, and the home marks it **`handed`** on the asker's copy alone (§4.8a *An
  alarm's answers*), so the asker settles it with `--outcome … --for <the overruling entry's id>`,
  which is in its own inbox; a question **passed up** and answered by the person owes one in the
  ordinary way. The failure this guards against is a confident wrong go-between steering a team
  all night unseen.
- **Who may instruct it — said plainly, because it is not enforced.** A cheap-model manager must
  not be able to instruct the high-trust session on *what to answer*. The manager is the seat's
  controller — `ao team start` writes it into the seat's `controllers` (*The seat*, above), and

  that without the host agent reading the team definition, which it does not. What bounds it instead: the techlead's brief takes instruction
  on *what to answer* from **the person alone** and reads a manager's words as lifecycle; it holds
  **no grant**, so it can act on no session; its never-list; and the FYI above. An answer to one's own question is an answer whoever gives
  it; **an unsolicited message from a techlead is information, not instruction** — it is not a
  controller of the members (`[other]`).
- **When it cannot answer.** One account has one usage window (§4.2a): a techlead on the
  grinders' account is capped when they are, which is when questions pile up. A `steer` lapses to

  number and the home does not time it, so a team with no manager, or a manager that is itself
  capped, still reaches a person. `--thread` names the worker's own **open** `ask` or `steer` to a
  session as well as its answered question to the person; the new question lands in the person
  inbox on the first one's thread, and the first **closes on every copy as `asked_person`**, so
  the techlead's `asks_waiting` stops counting it and a late answer from the techlead closes
  nothing — the person's answer is the one owed on. Refused, in words: a note, a question already passed up (the person holds it), or one already closed.

  ```yaml
  roles:
    grinder: {brief: docs/briefs/grinder.md, lane: free-pick, profile: grind,
              review: {reader: techlead}}           # every PR waits for the reader; `held:` defaults to `**`
    hunter:  {review: {reader: techlead, held: [src/**]}}   # only a PR touching these paths waits
    plain:   {review: {reader: techlead}}           # a person's own `--team` session, when its role says so
  ```

  `review: {reader, held, bound}` is the one-reader form; a flow writes `{chain: [{stage, reader,
  held}, …], bound}` (§4.9c *The record carries the chain*), and what follows reads the same for a
  chain of one. `reader` is `techlead` (the team's seat) or `person`; **`held` is a list of path globs matched against the PR's changed files and
  defaults to every PR (`**`)**; `bound` defaults to two hours. The record carries it as `review`,
  home-owned, set at start and shown on the Focus header as text. **Whether a PR is held keys on
  the record's `review` and the PR's paths** — never on who wrote the PR or its session's mode
  (mode decides only who executes the merge): a person's own interactive session inside a team
  (`ao new --team` or the form's Team field — with a role whose preset carries `review:`, else the
  team's own default, §4.9 *A person in the team*) gets the same reader a grinder
  does, *a safety net for when I am not intimately familiar with an architecture*. A session whose
  record has no `review`, or whose PR touches no held path, merges as §3's cadence says (the
  author, on a green `scripts/check_cadence.py`). **The record's `review` is read by the author's
  own `ao`** (`ao pr held <n>`, §4.7, which checks a PR's files against `held:`), never by the
  host agent, which only stores it. **The repo's mark beside it** (TD-787): the home's reading
  reads each open PR's files against the repo's `held:` — for a repo whose `.shiftlead.yml` writes
  none, the union of its live records' `review` globs, a key and no reader — whoever wrote the PR, and tags a green one
  nobody has asked *unasked* (§4.4 *A held PR nobody asked is marked, never ordered*), where the
  author's `ao pr held` reads its record's `review`. Where the two differ, an author whose record
  carries a `review` asks the reader the tag names, and one with none merges on the cadence and
  lets the tag stand.
  **How the reader hears of a PR — the seat is filled by mail, so a PR is an `ask`**: when a held
  PR is green and carries its author's own `cadence-review:` comment, the author sends **`ao msg
  --kind ask --pr <n> {techlead} "…"`** — **and the home refuses it until the PR is green**
  (TD-658; Paul, 2026-10-13: *I would expect grinders to make the PR green before handing it off to
  the techlead (or other depending on the workflow)*): an `ask` carrying `pr`, sent by a session, is
  read once as a `done` naming that PR is (§4.8 *A `done` with a PR is told what its checks read*,
  the same read, the same required set) — a required check **failed or still running** on the head
  refuses it, naming them, *PR #842's required checks are still running on 9c1e0f2: test (3.12) —
  ask again once they pass*, and nothing is sent — in a repo that sets `precheck`, an open PR's head
  with no `cadence/precheck` is refused the same way, with §4.8's *run `precheck.py --pr <n>`* line
  (TD-1033); green, no checks (and no `precheck`), merged or closed is sent; a
  required check that **never started** (the read's `not_run`, §4.4 *CI that never started is the
  person's*) is sent with *PR #<n>: checks never started on <head>: <reason>* in the reply, since its
  cure is the person's and a reader can still read the diff; a read
  the home could not make is sent with *checks unknown: <why>* in the reply, never refused on a
  failure of the home's own. It keys on the entry's `pr`, never on who reads it, so every hand-off
  of a PR — to the techlead seat, a flow's stage reader (§4.9c) or the person — is gated alike, and
  so is the ask again after findings, and the ask to the person past a reader's bound; a person's
  own `ask` with `pr` is not read. In the `ask`, `pr` is a structured field on a mail entry, an integer,
  the way `answered` and `passed_up` are, so a row can link it and a header can count it; the text
  is the author's summary, drawn as text. The seat fills as for any question (`asks_waiting`),
  reads the diff and the design it is measured against, and answers on the thread with one of the
  three verdicts (§4.9c *The reader's answer carries a verdict*): **`merged`** — the last reader,
  which merged it; **`pass`** — nothing against it on its paths and it did not merge, a reader
  before the last or a recommendation to an interactive asker; or **`findings`**. **This is the one `ask` the seat answers from its own reading** — a carve-out from
  *What it may answer*, since a verdict on a diff is nowhere written down before the read;
  `--source` on that reply names what the diff was measured against (the design's sections, the
  ledger entry), and the reply is still filed to the person as
  *answered for you* with **Overrule** — which, on a PR the reader has already merged, unmerges
  nothing: it reaches the asker as any overrule does, and a revert is then that session's next
  task, said in the overrule's text. The reader's merge is visible on the row (*merged #n*) so the
  person knows which kind of overrule they are sending. Who merges is the one place the asker's
  mode matters: for an ask from an **`unattended`** session the reader merges the PR itself (a
  `gh` act on the repo — not a session act, so no grant covers it and none is needed) and replies
  so; for an ask from an **`interactive`** session the reply is a **recommendation**, `pass` or
  `findings`, and the person merges or overrules. Findings go
  back on the thread; the author fixes and re-asks on the same thread — as a reply to the
  findings (`--reply-to`), whose root is the first ask's; `--thread` takes up a question put to
  the person and is refused toward a reader — so the queue is one entry
  per PR. **The queue is the seat's inbox**: a held PR waiting is an unanswered `ask` with `pr`
  set — counted in `asks_waiting`, and in a second structured field on the seat's view,
  **`prs_waiting: {n, oldest, asks}`** (a number, a time and `asks: [{from, pr}]`, each ask's sender
  (in the home's form: a node's session is `id@host`) and PR in the order they came — never their text, §4.10), which the team's header draws as
  **`n` PRs waiting · oldest `<age>`**, and from which `ao team flow` names what a switch leaves with
  the reader (§4.9c). The entries themselves are read with
  `ao inbox <seat>`, which a person may always run; the person's own Inbox page draws none of it,
  and **no board line names a held PR** — the wind-down's note (rule 9's, or a standing
  manager's) and a manager's escalation lines leave the reader's queue where it is (§4.9a). **The bound** is the repo's `review.bound` (two
  hours unless said): **an `ask` that carries `pr` is bounded by it and not by `TECHLEAD_WAIT`** —
  a read of a diff takes longer than a question. Past it the author asks the person on the same
  thread (`--thread`, which closes the seat's copy as `asked_person`), and the PR is the person's —
  a team never idles on one reader, it says so instead; the author's brief carries the number,
  the home does not time it. **The author is not idle meanwhile**: a sent ask is a claim released
  — it picks the next item; when `main` moves it rebases its own open held PRs (the reader merges
  oldest first and resolves only a ledger conflict itself; anything else is findings). **What
  shiftlead enforces**: it **records and shows** — the ask, the reply with its source, the count,
  the age; it does not stop a `gh pr merge`, and cannot, since merging is GitHub's. Where the
  reader has a GitHub account of its own, branch protection with a `CODEOWNERS` that mirrors
  `held:` is the enforcement, and the reader's approval is the review GitHub requires; where every
  session runs as the person (this repo) there is none, the rule is the brief's and this inbox's,
  and `scripts/check_cadence.py`'s `review` row stays the author's self-attested review; the
  reader's reply on the thread is shiftlead's record of the read, and a second line in the cadence
  script is agent-cadence's to add. **`reader: person`, and a team without a
  seat**: with `reader: person` the same ask goes to the person (`ao msg --kind ask --pr <n>
  person "…"`) and lands under *Needs you* as an ordinary counted `ask` with `#<n>` drawn as a
  link — no seat, no header count; a team whose repo says `reader: techlead` but defines no
  `techlead:` seat is the same case (`{techlead}` reads *none*), and `ao team start` says so as it does for a seat without a primer. **Not the reader**: a
  PR from the person's own anchor session outside any team (no `team` badge); a
  session whose record carries no `review`; and a read is never a re-review of what the author's
  reviewer found — it is the high-trust read the rule exists for. `review:` on a role preset is
  checked when the file is read and again by the host agent, and carried onto the record at
  create by `ao new --role` and `ao team start`; `pr` on an `ask` (`ao msg --pr`) is refused on any
  other kind and on anything but a positive integer.
- **The trial, and the wall.** A less-trusted model beside a high-trust one waits for the

**The director seat answers permission prompts for the person** (TD-636; Paul, 2026-10-13: *I like the
idea of filtering permission prompts — essentially allowing a director seat to handle them for the
user*). An unattended member launches with its prompts skipped today (§6 *What is left is judgement*: the
Claude Code adapter's `--dangerously-skip-permissions`), so almost no prompt reaches anyone, and the
safety an unattended member has is its tool settings' allowlist alone. The point of a director is to
let a profile launch unattended **without** the skip — a profile's **`permission_prompts: ask`** or **`all`** (§4.2a: under `ask` the tool's own mode answers first, under `all` every prompt comes this way; `skip`, the
default, keeps today's flag; the adapter adds it only under `skip`, since an empty `unattended_args`
falls back to it today) — and still not wake the person for what is routine. Three lines stand between such a prompt and the person, in
order, and the first that answers answers:
1. **The person's written rules** (built by TD-641): `permissions:` in `settings.yml` (§5), per role,
   `allow` and `deny` lists in the adapter's own rule syntax (§4.3: Claude Code's `Bash(pdm run test:*)`,
   `Edit(src/**)`), matched by the host agent at the hook (§4.2) against the prompt's tool and input
   — deterministic, no tokens, a `deny` winning over an `allow`. A match is answered at once, through
   the hook, with the rule as its reason. **On a node** the hook is the node's and the rules its
   replica of the home's `settings.yml` (§4.4a), but the *Answered for you* FYI is mail, whose one
   writer is the home (§9 invariant 15): a rule answers only while the node's link is up, and only
   once the home has filed the FYI over it; a link that is down, or a filing refused, leaves its
   member's prompt going on down this list.
2. **The director seat** (built by TD-642): one per org, defined by the org's `director:` (§5 `org.yml`:
   its profile and brief; absent, there is no director and this line is skipped). **The first seat of
   no team**: every other seat is a team's (*Seats with a trigger*, above), and this one is the org's,
   above every project heading (§4.9, TD-722), with no team badge and no `project` badge; its brief
   carries the **Org block** in place of a Project block — every registered checkout on its host,
   built from the repo registry at each fill (§5 *Repos*), each named with its project heading where
   it has one, and never from `projects:`, so adding a repo to a heading changes nothing it sees —
   since a prompt it weighs may come from any repo. **Its directory is its own**: the record's `dir`
   is a plain directory outside every repo, `~/.local/share/shiftlead/director/`, a directory
   session no other session shares (§9 invariant 2), and it reads a repo by absolute path in the
   home's checkout of the asker's repo (*Its first read*, below). Its card is drawn in a row of its
   own at the top of the Sessions page beside the person's own row, only where `director:` is defined,
   never under a heading and with no Start or Wind down — its controls are the Settings page's
   **Director** card (§4.5a) — written, as a seat is, by a client that reads `org.yml`: any `ao team start`
   (or Apply) writes it when the org defines it and no record holds it, since the host agent reads no
   `org.yml` — with its own trigger, **`prompt`** (the director's alone: a prompt no rule answered
   pending on an unattended record), and like a seat on call, filled by the home on it and closed
   when it has nothing due (§6 rule 3) — holding the **`permit`**
   grant (§4.8), which lets it `allow` or `deny` a pending prompt of any unattended supervised record
   of the org and nothing else. It reads the prompt, the member's role and brief and its claim, and
   answers by its own judgement with a reason, or leaves it, saying why, for the person. Its brief is
   the package's `director.md`: allow what the member's brief plainly covers inside its own worktree;
   deny what touches another worktree, process control, or the network beyond the forge; pass up what
   it cannot weigh.
3. **The person**, under *Needs you* as today.

**What always reaches the person, whatever a rule or the seat says**: a question or a menu (never a
prompt to answer, §9 invariant 6); a permission of a session whose tool has no permission channel
(§4.3 `permission_channel`, Gemini CLI), which only the terminal answers; a prompt whose input names `~/.shiftlead`, `~/.claude`, a systemd
unit, or a path outside the member's own worktree and its repo's checkout — another member's worktree,
the repo's `.git` and a `.claude/settings*` file counted outside too, and a path it cannot place (one
through a variable, a `cd` it cannot follow) as well; a stream such as `/dev/null` is no path — read from
the command lines and paths the adapter says the prompt touches, never a file's content, **unless denied** — the
host agent refuses a rule's or the director's *allow* there, and a deny stands, as a manager's deny
of the same does today (its brief), so nothing a manager refused now wakes the person; and every prompt of an
**interactive** session, which no session acts on (§9 invariant 5) and no rule answers either, since
the person is at it. **Everything answered is told**, as the techlead's answers are (*Everything
answered for the person is told to the person*, above): each answer by a rule or by the director is an
FYI in the person's Inbox's *Answered for you*, written by the host in the shape a sourced reply's
entry has (`answered: {question, asker, answerer, source}` — the prompt as the question, the member
as the asker, *rules* or the director as the answerer, the rule or the reason as the source), so a
wrong rule shows itself the first time it fires. **Overrule** is not drawn on these rows, since the
prompt is already answered and the member is no asker to reply to: a rule is changed in
`settings.yml`'s `permissions:` (the row names it), the director's judgement in its brief. **This is not *Allow for this session*** (§10, declined
2026-09-23): nothing changes a session's own permission settings, every prompt is answered one at a
time and seen in the Inbox, and the rules are the person's, written once per role, never learned from
a press. **Who answers today stays the default**: with no `permissions:` and no `director:`, a prompt
goes to the person, and a team's manager triages its members' prompts by its brief as now; with a
director, the manager's triage of permissions stands aside (its brief says so) — one judge, not two.

**The director answers what is passed up to it, from the person's preferences** (TD-657; Paul,
2026-10-13: *the original thought was that techleads would pass questions that they could not
answer to it*). A second techlead adds little — it reads the documents the first could not answer
from — so what a director adds is **what the person would say**, read from what the person has
written of their own preferences. The path is worker → techlead → director → person: a techlead
passes up what nothing written answers (*Passing up*, above), and with a director the pass lands
in the director's inbox, its `asks` cause (§6 rule 3) — never refused as full, since a pass is a
question already counted on the asker's side; a director seat that cannot be filled (suspended,
or at its fill ceiling) is passed by, and the pass goes to the person as if there were none. The director answers with `--source` — an
*Answered for you* FYI like any sourced answer, its `answered` carrying **`level`** — or passes it
on to the person with its own recommendation (`--pass-up`, its pass the last). **Passing up buys no
time** here either: a `steer` keeps its bound and lapses to its default as any steer does.
- **Preferences, in layers.** Markdown files, one preference per bullet under a heading per topic
  (*Spending*, *Scope*, *Risk*, *Style*, *Always ask me*, *Permissions*), each bullet a general
  sentiment rather than a case, where it can carry its origin (*Paul, 2026-10-13*). Read in this
  order, the most specific last and winning: **the package's defaults** (`src/shiftlead/briefs/preferences.md`,
  shipped with the briefs); **the person's own**, across repos, **`shiftlead/preferences.md` in their agent-cadence layer
  repo** (§4.8 *The person's own additions*; [ADR 2026-10-25](../decisions/2026-10-25-shiftlead-requires-dev-cadence.md), *Decision* 7; TD-1016) — private
  as the layer is, following the person to every machine their layer is cloned on, read from their
  own layer alone: a layer followed from someone else contributes no preferences, since a preference
  decides on the person's behalf, so the floor below stays the person's; never copied into a repo or
  shown in a shared one; **a repo's own**, **`.shiftlead/preferences.md`** in
  the repo, checked in, beside its flows and roles (§4.9c); and **the person's own section for that

  floor no layer lowers**: a bullet under *Always ask me* in any layer, §4.9b's **Never** list
  (*What it may answer*, above) and *What always reaches the person* go to the person at every
  level, whatever a later layer says. A question with no repo (a directory session's) reads the
  package's and the person's general layers alone.
- **Its first read, and what it reads.** The package's and the person's layers are filled into the
  director's brief at each fill as two file slots, **`{preferences}`** (the package's) and
  **`{my_preferences}`** (the layer's `shiftlead/preferences.md`, read by the home from the layer
  clone's working tree), by rule 7's fill, so each
  is among the fill's `sources` and an accepted change is read on the next fill; the person's file
  absent, or no layer set, fills *none written* rather than failing the fill — the one slot that may be
  missing. The
  repo's layer and the asking team's primer (`context:`, above) it reads in **the home's checkout of
  the asker's repo**, both paths absolute on its *filled for* line. **The director never reads
  `~/.shiftlead`**: the person's layer comes in the fill and remembered answers in its inbox. `director:`'s
  profile skips prompts (`permission_prompts: skip`, the default, §4.2a) — a start refuses a director
  on an `ask` or `all` profile, since nothing above it would answer its own — and it writes nothing but mail
  and `ao prefs propose`, so *What always reaches the person*, which is about members' prompts, never
  meets its own reads. **An index is never a source**: a primer is never
  cited; a preference is cited where it is written — `--source "preferences (person) § Spending"`,
  `"<repo>/.shiftlead/preferences.md § Scope"` — or the design, ledger or decision a preference

- **A level the person sets**: **`conservative`** (the default) answers only what a preference or a
  written decision plainly covers; **`extrapolate`** also what one covers by a reasonable extension,

- **Grown and groomed, and every change the person's press.** The director's **groom pass** is a
  fill of its own (`groom`, §6 rule 3): due `director.groom` after the last pass concluded (`7d` by
  default, `off` for never) — with none ever concluded, once at the first tick the seat exists, which
  is the seed — or at once on **Groom now** (`ao prefs groom`); **never while a proposal stands**, so
  a pass that has proposed is not filled again until the person's Accept or Discard concludes it. It reads the person's
  file, the answers the person marked **Remember this** since the last pass, and — the first pass,
  or one with the file empty — **the seed**: every registered repo's board `Decided:` lines and the
  person's dated quotes in its ledger and design history. It writes the person's file again as
  general sentiments — merging, generalising, dropping what a later decision overturned — and
  hands it to the home with **`ao prefs propose <file> --why "<n added, m merged, k dropped; the
  gaps>"`** (the `prefs_propose` RPC, the director seat's and an interactive session's — the person's — and refused from any other: kept as
  `~/.shiftlead/preferences.proposed.md`, one at a time, a newer replacing it: a proposal is this
  install's state, not the person's text). The person sees it as
  the Inbox row **preferences proposed** (§4.5a): **Accept** (the home moves it over the layer's file
  and commits *preferences: <why>* in the person's layer clone, the person's own act), **Discard**, and
  **Open file** on the proposal. **The home commits, the person pushes** (*Decision* 10, Q2b; TD-663):

  uncommitted edits or the clone is behind its upstream (*preferences.md has edits not committed in

  it never writes over an edit made on another machine. The write fetches the upstream first (bounded
  by a `timeout`, a fetch that fails refusing, *could not fetch ~/cadence-layer: <why>*), then commits
  `shiftlead/preferences.md` alone, by pathspec, so nothing else staged in the person's clone is swept
  in; it creates `shiftlead/` where it is missing, and refuses a clone with no upstream, a detached
  HEAD, or a merge, rebase or cherry-pick in progress, each naming which. Either concludes the pass. The director never writes `~/.shiftlead` or the layer, and a session never
  writes the person's layer: a proposal and the person's own hand edit (the person commits it in their
  layer, as any edit of theirs there) are the only ways in. A repo's layer changes by PR, as any checked-in file does.
- **Remember this.** The person's reply to a question whose `passes` name the director, and an
  **Overrule** of a director's sourced answer, carry a **Remember this** tick in the compose (off until
  ticked), stored as `remember: true` on the reply entry — the person's answer, which reaches the
  director as the reply's copy to a passer, or the overrule's copy to the answerer. A remembered copy
  is kept in the director's inbox until a groom pass concludes rather than aging out; with
  `director.groom: off` only **Groom now** concludes one, and the Director card counts them
  (*3 remembered*) so none waits unseen. The pass turns each into a general line, under the asking
  repo's `## <repo>` heading when it is about that repo alone.
