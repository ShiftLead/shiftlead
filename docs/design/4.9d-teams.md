### 4.9d Teams: the flow a team runs, its logins and when it runs

**Designed by TD-1153, with Paul; its builds are listed in `docs/reviews/TD-1153.md`.** The frame is
[ADR 2026-11-18](../decisions/2026-11-18-tools-flows-teams.md); this is the third of its three pages,
after Tools (§4.2b) and Flows (§4.9c), and it is written to agree with both. Every part of it is built
and promoted **before the cut-over** (§4.9c *What this replaces*, TD-2056) and most of it does nothing
until then (*Before and after the cut-over*, below). Until the cut-over a team is what §4.9 says it is —
a definition with members, sizes and seats — and that text stands; this section says what a team is and
what its page shows and writes **after** it. The working prototype is
`docs/mockups/reviews/td1153-src/prototype.html`, its views
`docs/mockups/reviews/2026-11-21-td1153-team-{1440,420}.png`; both were drawn before the built-in sets, so
they show the five seats under *Beside the path* where the page draws one line, and there is no drawing of
the Built-in seats page: its bands are the text's (*The built-in seats and their page*).

#### What a team is

**A team is a repo, a flow and a list of logins.** Under a format-3 flow every session is started for
one entry in one stage and ends with it (§4.9c *A stage is one kind of work, one model, one fresh
session*), an audit's session for one run, a built-in seat's for one call. So a team has **no members, no seat names and no brief of its
own**: who works is whichever session the home starts next, what it is told is the flow's brief and the
repo's supplement (§4.9c *What a stage's session is told*), the audits are the flow's (§4.9c *Audits start on a clock*), and the five built-in seats
are a set's that the team's repo uses (*The built-in seats and their page*). What
is left is the team's, and all of it is a setting:

| What | Key under `settings.yml` `teams.<team>` | Changed |
|---|---|---|
| its **name** | the key itself | never: fixed when made |
| its **label** | `label` | at once (✎) |
| the **repo** it works | `repo` | never |
| the **host** it runs on | `host` | never |
| the **flow** it runs | `flow`, the flow's name (§4.9c) | by a switch (*The flow pick and a switch*) |
| its **logins**, in order | `logins`, a list of `<tool>:<name>` (§4.2b) | at once |
| **sessions at once** | `at_once`, by stage key; `most`, the whole team | at once |
| **when it runs** | `schedule`, `until`, `reserve`, `balance` (§5, §6) | at once |

**Nothing of a team is in a repo's file or in `org.yml` after the cut-over.** The `teams:` blocks of
`org.yml` and `.shiftlead.yml` are what defines a team until the cut-over and are moved by it; a flow
that lives in a repo is still the repo's and still changes by a pull request (§4.9c *A repo's flow
changes by a pull request*). **A team works one repo and a repo may have several teams** (§4.9c *One
flow per team; more kinds of work are more teams*); they hand work on through the ledger. A name is
unique in the org, compared case-folded as the ledger compares it, and follows a login's rule (§4.2b
*Terms used below*: at most 24 characters, letters, digits, `-` and `_`, the first a letter or digit),
since it is a key, a path on the state branch (`flows/<team>.yml`), a field of every stage record and
the word an entry's **`Team:` line** carries (§4.4 *An entry names its team*: an entry names its team
there, never in `Owner:`, which holds a role word). **One thing about a team is still a pull request:
the repo's ledger declares its teams**, `Team = a | b` on its `Fields:` line (§4.4 *The move from one
team to two*), and that line is ledger text. A repo with one team declares nothing and its entries
carry no `Team:` line, so a first team needs no pull request; **a repo's second team needs that one**,
which adds both names, and until it is merged the new team is *undeclared* and its Start is refused as
it is today. **A name is fixed; a label is what the person reads**, exactly as a login has both
(§4.2b *Terms used below*): ✎ beside the team's name changes the label and nothing else, and wherever
a label is set the pages, `ao` output and commit lines show it with the name beside it (`ao team
list`: `ao-grind (shown as "ShiftLead builders")`). Nothing is keyed by a label, two teams may carry
the same one, and an empty label clears it. There is no rename of the name.

**What the cut-over's move must produce** (TD-2056 carries it out; this says only the result). For
each team a definition gives today: `repo` and `host` as the definition and `place:` resolve them
(§4.9 *Where a repo's team lands*); `flow` the format-3 flow **the person picked for the team before
the window** (below); `at_once` for the stage that takes ready-to-build
entries the **sum** of today's sizes of the team's builder lines (each line's `teams.<team>.size`, else
its `count:`), every other stage 1, and `most` left unset; `logins` the logins
that §4.2b *What the switch-over makes* makes from the profiles the team's members and seats ran on,
each as `<tool>:<name>`, in the order the cut-over's design gives (TD-1976), the members' first; and
`schedule`, `until`, `reserve` and `balance` as they stand (**`on_work` is dropped**, *When it runs*, below), the schedule's `profile:`
rewritten to the login made from it. `teams.<team>.size` is used as above and then dropped; **`teams.<team>.max` is dropped
and nothing takes its place** (a stage's number is its ceiling). **The move maps no flow by itself.**
A format-3 flow of the same name is not the first shape's flow: the format-3 `td` has no fact-check
seats on other makers' models, which `ao-grind` runs today. So before the window the person **picks
each team's format-3 flow** — on the cut-over's page of prepared moves, which is the cut-over's to
design — the built-in of the same name suggested, with the seats the team has today that it lacks
listed beside it. **A team with no pick stops the window, named**; nothing is moved for any team
until every team has one. The move also writes the one built-in set, `builtins.default`, whose models and
logins the person gives on the same page (*The built-in seats and their page*).
**The cut-over writes one marker**, `cut_over: <date>` at the top of `settings.yml`, as its last
step; one function reads it, and it is what gates this page's writes (*Before and after the
cut-over*). TD-2056 must write it. **The move also writes the home's team record for every team as
`stopped: {by: person}`** (*Started is a mark the home keeps*, below): every team is stopped for the
window, and a team with no `stopped` record would be started by its schedule at the next reset, so
after the window no team runs until the person's Start.

#### Sessions at once

**One number per stage, and an optional one for the team; no range.** `at_once.<stage key>` is the most sessions
the home runs in that stage at one time, **1 where the key is absent**; `most` is the most sessions of
the team's stages and audits together, and **has no default**: absent, which is how a team is made
and is drawn *no team limit*, the team is bounded by its stages' numbers alone (Paul, 2026-11-21: a
default *is likely too low … with the td flow with the ability to scale some of the seats*). This is
what §4.9c's *Offer* act means by *within the team's size*: the home starts a stage's session only
while the stage holds fewer than its number and, where `most` is set, the team fewer than `most`; an
audit's session is held back only by `most`. **Under `most`, stages go first**: in a pass with fewer
free places than there are sessions to start, the home offers entries to stages before it starts an
audit, so an audit whose clock has come round waits for a place and starts in a later pass.
**Built-in seats are not counted** against either: they answer what a counted session waits on, so
counting them could stop the team on itself. A number is a ceiling and nothing is kept running to meet
it: with no entry to take, a stage holds no session.

A range meant *keep this many, grow to that many* for members that stayed running between entries
(§4.9 *A team's size*, *Scaling with the work*); with a session per entry there is nothing to keep, so
the floor has no meaning and the ceiling is the number. Those two passages, the run-only member and
`teams.<team>.size` and `max` describe the first shape and end at the cut-over. **A changed number
applies from the next session the home starts**: lowering one ends no session, and the stage runs over
its number until enough of them end. A number is a whole number of 1 or more; a `most` below the largest
`at_once` is accepted, the page saying which stage it holds back. `scale.live` (§5), the host's own
bound on live sessions, still holds above both.

#### The logins, in order

The frame's rules stand (*A flow names its models; the team lists its logins*):

- **One ordered list across tools.** `logins` names logins of the team's host, each by its key
  `<tool>:<name>` (§4.2b *Where it lives*: a name is unique per tool alone, and is resolved on the host
  that launches), on a node's team as on the home's; `schedule.login` holds the same form. The page
  shows a login by its label, or its name, with its tool.
- **Every launch takes the first usable login that offers the model** of the stage or audit
  it is for (§4.2b `models(home, plan)`, narrowed by a login's pin). **Usable** is the frame's
  word: signed in with its account named; not at its reserve (§6 *Usage gate*, the team's `reserve`
  added) and not `limited`; and not on an account an earlier unusable login of the list shares. The
  smoke pass the frame adds to *usable* is TD-2046's design and is not read until it is built.
- **A session whose login stops being usable moves down the list** (the frame, *A stopped seat
  moves*): the tick of the host the session runs on relaunches it on the next usable login that
  offers its model, the same record and entry, handed over from the transcript. **A move is not a
  loss**: the holder is neither lost nor silent (§4.9c *The home acts only where no session will*),
  and the move spends no offer, no limit and nothing of the total cap. **The usage gate holds a
  session only when no login in the list is usable for its model** (§6 *Usage gate*, *After the
  cut-over*): then it waits where it is, as a gated session does today.
- **A metered login** is used in its place, bounded by its amount reserve, and is marked *spends
  money* wherever the page draws it.
- **A reordered or changed list applies at each next launch**; it moves no running session, and a
  login removed from the list keeps the sessions already on it until they end.

**What this design builds of that, and what the cut-over's design does.** The **pick** — given a
team's list and a model, the first usable login, or why there is none — is one function this design's
store build writes (TD-2060), and the page, `ao team show` and *cannot run* read it. **Calling it at a
launch**, writing the record's `login`, `account` and `seat_model`, the move down the list and the
transcript handoff are the launch path's, which is **the cut-over's design and its builds** (TD-1976;
§4.2b *What the switch-over makes*). No build of this design launches a session on a login.

**A team that cannot run says why, with the model named.** A team **cannot run** its flow while its
list is empty, or while a model the flow names — a stage's or an audit's — is
offered by no login in the list: *ao-grind cannot run td: no login in its list offers fable*. The home
then starts nothing for the team, the page says the reason in those words on the flow band and on each
stop that needs the model, and `ao org` lists the team with it. A model that is offered only by logins
that are unusable right now is not *cannot run*: the stage **waits**, and the page says for what.

**A new subscription joins in three steps**, and the page shows where each stands: its login is made
and signed in on the Tools page; it is added to the team's list here; and a flow the team runs must
name one of its models, since a flow names models and never tools (the frame). A login in the list
none of whose models the team's flow names is drawn with *td names none of its models, so nothing runs
on it yet*.

#### The flow pick and a switch

**One pick.** The flow band's pick lists every flow the team could be given: the package's built-ins,
the org's, and its own repo's (§4.9c *Where flows and roles live*). A flow the team cannot run is listed
**disabled, with its reason**, *cannot run here*: it fails its check (§4.9c *A flow is usable only when
it flows*); a model it names is offered by no login in the list; or, for a team on a node, one of
§4.9c's reasons in its words (*An org flow or role is not usable by a team on a node*) — an org flow
(*not followable: an org flow, and `<team>` runs on `<node>` — define it in the repo*), a repo flow the
node's checkout does not hold yet (*not followable*, by name), or a node that does not answer
(*unknown*, never offered). There is no list of flows a team *may* run (the
first shape's `flows:`): every usable flow is offered.

**Picking another flow opens a preview, and Switch now is the switch.** The preview lists every open
stage record of the team (§4.9c *The stage record*) and what the switch does to it, then **Switch
now** and **Keep `<flow>`**. There is no separate Apply: sessions belong to entries, not to the team,
so nothing is relaunched for a new definition, and the switch takes effect when it is pressed. It is
one call to the home, `team_switch`, a person's own act. **A switch is recorded before it is made**:
the home writes `switching: <flow>` on its team record (*Started is a mark the home keeps*, below),
pushes the new flow as the team's copy on the state branch (`flows/<team>.yml`, §4.9c), writes `flow`,
and clears `switching`. **The push is the commit point**: a push the forge refuses refuses the switch,
`switching` cleared and nothing else changed. A home that restarts with `switching` set finishes that
switch — the push again where the copy is not yet the new flow's, then the key — and nothing is
inferred from a copy that merely differs. The records are then dealt with by the tick's reconcile
(§4.9c *The home acts only where no session will*), whose cases are the rules below. **The rules
apply to whatever sessions are live, whether the team is started or not**: a stopped team may be
switched, and so may one wound down with sessions still finishing; where no session is live the
preview says none is ended. Each record is dealt with by these rules, which the preview draws from
the same function:

- **A record in a stage whose key and kind the new flow also has stays there, and its session carries
  on.** The record is rewritten to the new flow: its counts for moves the new flow also has stand, those
  for moves it lacks are dropped, and its count against the total cap stands; the holder ends its stage
  with the new flow's destinations.
- **A record in a stage the new flow lacks** — no stage of that key, or one of another kind — **has
  its session ended by the home and goes to the person**, by §4.9c's reconcile case (*a record whose
  stage key its flow no longer has*): the entry's decision gate, its answers the new flow's stages.
  Its branch and pull request stay as they are; **what the ended session had not pushed is lost**, and
  the preview says so on that record's line, so the person can wait for the stage to end first.
  **Where such a record has made no move yet** (offered, nothing ended) it does not go to the
  person: its session is ended and the entry re-enters by the new flow's way in for its `Kind:`,
  offered afresh, keeping its branch and its pull request where it has opened one. A record with no
  move yet in a stage both flows have stays, by the rule above.
- **A record marked `merging` is not touched.** The merge gate reads the pull request and needs no
  flow (§4.9c *The merge gate under a flow*), so the merge finishes as it would have; the preview
  draws it *merging, not touched*. A merge that is refused takes the **new** flow's move for a refused
  merge, or goes to the person where the record's stage is one the new flow lacks.
- **A record that already waits on the person is left until its answer**, even where its stage is
  gone. When the answer is pressed, a label that maps to a stage the new flow has is applied; one
  that maps to a stage it lacks is not, and the entry is asked once more, with the new flow's stages.
  A waiting record whose stage the new flow lacks has no stop to be drawn at, so the page draws it in
  a last group under the map, *waiting on you, its stage is gone*.
- **An entry with no record** is not touched: it enters the new flow when it is first offered.

`at_once` keeps its numbers for stage keys the new flow has and drops the rest. **A stage renamed on
the Flows page keeps its number**: the save that re-keys the records (§4.9c *A switch or a rename
re-keys the records*) re-keys `at_once` for every team that runs the flow. A repo flow's rename
arrives by a pull request, which re-keys nothing: the renamed stage starts at 1, and the page draws
the old key, which names no stage, as *no such stage* with a **Remove**. An audit's run that is
under way finishes; an audit the new flow lacks does not start again, and one it has under the same
name keeps its clock. The built-in seats are not the flow's and a switch does not touch them. **The switch is refused**, in
the preview, while the team cannot run the flow picked. A repo flow changed by a merged pull request
and an org flow saved on the Flows page are not switches: they reach the team's records by the rules
§4.9c gives for them.

#### When it runs

The rows of the Settings page's team card move to the team page, their keys and their `ao` verbs
unchanged (§5 `teams.<team>`, §6):

- **Starts** — the schedule (§6 *Schedule*): *when I press Start* or *at each week's reset*. After the
  cut-over the rule names a **login of the team's list** where it named a profile (`schedule.login`),
  the first by default, and reads that login's account for the reset; a metered login is refused for
  it, as a metered profile is today. **Removing that login from the list is allowed**: the rule then
  reads the first login of the list, and the page says so on the row (*the schedule now reads
  `<login>`*); a list left empty leaves the schedule naming none and starting nothing, said the same
  way. **For a team on a node *at each week's reset* is refused**, *a
  node's logins are not read here*, until the home reads a node's logins (§4.2b *Not here*): the home
  has no reading of the login to wait on.
- **Stops** — *never*, or *at* a time (`until`, §6 *Team stop time*). *Never* is the key absent, and
  is what a new team has. At the time the team's started mark is cleared and its running sessions are
  stopped as §6 says: each takes the instant as its `run_until`, the wrap-up then the kill. A stage
  record whose holder is ended this way stays where it is and is offered again at the next Start,
  spending no offer (§4.9c: a holder ended by a Stop is offered again when the team runs); **an
  audit's run the stop time killed is not its one loss** either, and the audit starts again on its
  clock once the team is started.
- **When work appears** — `on_work` (§6 rule 8): *start the team*, *ask me*, *do nothing*. **This row
  is the first shape's and goes at the cut-over**: it is drawn only for a team that is not switched. A
  first-shape team winds down when its lanes run dry, and the setting says what brings it back; a
  format-3 team that is started holds no session while it has no work and takes an entry the moment
  one is pickable, so nothing winds down for lack of work and the setting has nothing left to do. The
  cut-over's move drops the key, and `ao team on-work` goes with it.
- **Reserve priority** — `reserve`, added to each login's account's reserve for this team's sessions
  (§6 *Usage gate*).
- **Holds new work** — `balance` (§6 *Balance*), under the name of what it does after the cut-over:
  while the team is over a line the home **offers no entry that has no stage record yet**; an entry
  already in a stage carries on through its stages, its merge and its questions. Its lines are
  `prs` and `oldest`; the first shape's third line, the reader's queue (`review`), has no seat to read
  after the cut-over and is dropped by it.

**Started is a mark the home keeps.** With no members, *is this team running* cannot be read off live
sessions, so the home keeps **`started: {since, by}`** per team in its own state — the home's `host`
record, `{teams: {<team>: {started}}}`, beside the balance mark (§6 *Balance*), kept across a restart
and served on the `host` read; it is not a setting and is not in `settings.yml`. It is **set** by
**Start** (the page's, the Sessions card's, `ao team start`) and by the schedule's start at a reset;
it is **cleared** by **Wind down** (`ao team stop`) and by the stop time, and the clearing writes
**`stopped: {at, by}`** beside it, `by` the person or the stop time, which the head reads (*Stopped by
you*, *Stopped at its stop time*). **`stopped` is written only when a set mark is cleared**: a stop
time that passes on a team that is not started is spent and writes nothing, so it never overwrites
*by you*; and a Start, the person's or the schedule's, deletes it. **Who may start a stopped team**:
the person's Start starts any team, and the schedule's start at a reset follows the one rule §6
*Schedule* states for a switched team (*After the cut-over*) — it starts a team that is not started
and either has no `stopped` record or was stopped by its stop time, and never one the person wound
down, which waits for the person's Start.
**A stop time is one instant and is spent once it has passed**: it stops the team once, and a Start
after it, the person's or the schedule's, is not stopped by it again; the page draws a spent one as
*passed*, with **Clear**. So a team that starts at each week's reset and stops at an instant has its stop time set again each
week.
The same record carries `switching` while a flow switch is under way (*The flow pick and a
switch*). *Running* and *Stopped* on the page, Delete's refusal and
every *while the team is started* in §4.9c read the started mark and nothing else; whether a repo's
seats run reads the marks of all the repo's teams (*The built-in seats and their page*). **A live team**,
wherever a rule reads one after the cut-over (§4.4 *An entry names its team*), is a started team.

**Start and Wind down** keep their names and change their meaning with the cut-over. A **started**
team is one the home may start sessions for (§4.9c *The home acts only where no session will*).
**Wind down** clears the mark: the home starts no new session, and each running one ends its stage,
the entries then waiting where they are for the next Start. **A wound-down team may still have
sessions**, so the head reads *Stopped by you · n finishing* until they end; while they run, its tech lead
and manager still answer them (*The built-in seats and their page*), a login that stops being usable still moves them down the list, and the
tick's acts that start no session still run for their records. The stop time is the harder stop
(above). Neither closes a member, there being none.

#### The page

**Where the settings pages sit.** Tools, Flows, Briefs, Teams and Built-in seats are settings pages, not places work
is done, so none has a tab in the top bar. The gear opens a **settings area**: a rail at the left,
on every one of its pages, listing **Settings** (today's page, whole), **Tools**, **Flows**,
**Briefs**, **Teams** and **Built-in seats**, the gear lit on all six. On a phone the rail is a row above the page.
`/teams/<team>` opens a team; the teams are **tabs across the top of the page, ordered by repo and
then by name** (names compared case-folded, the order every *by name* here means), a dot on one that cannot run its flow, and **+ New team** at the row's end; `/teams`
opens the first of that order. A
team's card on the Sessions page links its name's flow chip to its team page (today the chip lands on
the Settings page's `#team-<team>`).

The page has these bands, in order:

1. **The head** — the team's label, or its name where it has none, with ✎, and the name beside a label; *works `<repo>` on `<host>`*; *Running · n sessions* (the
   counted sessions live now; *n of m* where `most` is set), *Stopped by you* or *Stopped at its stop time* (plain *Stopped* for a team never started), with
   *· n finishing* while a wound-down team's sessions end; **Start** or **Wind down**.
2. **The flow it runs** — the pick, with **Edit on Flows**; the switch preview while another flow is
   picked; then **the route map** (below); under it **Beside the path**, read-only, the flow's audits
   each with its clock and when it next comes round, and the line *Built-in seats: set `<set>`*, a link to
   the set's page;
   then the whole-team limit: *no team limit* (with what the stages' numbers add up to) and **Set
   one**, or *at most n stage and audit sessions at once* with −, + and **Remove**.
3. **Logins, in the order they are tried** — a numbered row per login, *if not usable, then* between
   rows: its name, *spends money* on a metered one, its tool and account, the models it offers, the
   Tools page's gauge for its account (§4.2b *The gauge*, display only here), **Usable** or **Passed
   over** with the reason, the *names none of its models* line where it applies, and ▲, ▼ and ×.
   Below, **Add a login**, a pick of the host's logins not in the list, and a link to Tools.
4. **When it runs** — the rows above: four for a switched team, five, with *When work appears*, for one that is not.
5. **Delete `<team>`**.

**The route map draws the flow as lines.** Each way in is a line, headed by what starts there
(*Entries that need a design first*, *Entries ready to build*, or *Every entry* for a stage that takes
`any`). A line is derived from the flow, never stored: from the stage that takes the kind, follow
`work-pushed` (a design or build stage) or `ship` (a review stage) to the stage it names, and so on,
until the destination is `merge` or the person or a stage already on the line; each stage passed is a
**stop**, in that order, and the line ends in *merged*, or in *you* where its last move goes to the
person. A stage on no such line is drawn on a last line, *reached only by other outcomes*. A stop
shows the stage's name, its kind in the kind's colour (§4.9c *A claim's chip is its stage*), *only for
protected files* where it is `only-when`, its model and the login a session would start on now (*opus
on claude-paul*; *waits for a usable login*; *no login offers it*), its number with − and +, and the
entries in it: a filled pill per record held in the stage, with how long, and a **dashed pill** per
record that waits on the person, drawn at the stage that asked, with how long ago. The map says only
what the stage records show (§4.9c, Paul, 2026-11-12: *the user needs to be able to trust the ui*).

**New team.** **+ New team** asks for a name, an optional label, a repo (a registered checkout, §5), a host (the home,
or a node that has the repo) and a flow (the built-in `build` chosen), and makes the team **stopped,
with an empty login list**, on its page, which says it cannot run until a login is added. Where the
repo already has a team, the form says what a second team needs: **one pull request to that repo's
own ledger that declares both teams** on its `Fields:` line (`docs/ledger/README.md` in the repo the
teams work, never a file of ShiftLead's, agent-cadence's or ledgerboard's; §4.4 *The move from one
team to two*), the line to write shown for copying. Until it is merged
the page marks the team *undeclared in `<repo>`'s ledger* and its Start is refused.

**Delete** is refused while the team is started; while any session of the team is live, an audit's,
its tech lead's and its manager's included; and while it has open stage records, naming them (*TD-2101 is in
Build; TD-2084 waits on you*). Otherwise a second press removes `teams.<team>`, the home's team
record and the team's copy on the state branch. **Every entry in flight has to leave
first.** On a repo with several teams an entry leaves by merging, by being archived, by the person's
answer, or by its `Team:` line being changed to another team, which closes its record here and enters
it there with its branch and open pull request carried over (§4.9c, reconcile). **On a repo with one team there is no `Team:` line to change**, so every
entry in flight must merge, be archived or be answered before the team can be deleted. Entries with
no record stay in the ledger untouched. Where the ledger declares teams, the deleted name stays in
the declaration, and on the entries that carry it, **until a ledger pull request changes them**: the
ledger's check reports those entries as naming a team that is not defined (§4.4 *The move from one
team to two*), and a new team made under the same name takes them.

**The built-in seats are no part of a team.** Tagging, the tech lead, the manager, board answers, pull
requests nobody holds and the sweeper take their models and their logins from the built-in set the
team's repo uses (*The built-in seats and their page*, below), never from the team's flow or its
login list. The team page draws one read-only line for them under *Beside the path*: *Built-in seats:
set `<set>`*, a link to that set's page, with *cannot run `<seat>`: no login in its list offers
`<model>`* beside it where that is so.

**A team on a node.** The home does not read a node's logins (§4.2b *Not here*), so for a team whose
host is a node the logins band lists the names with *on `<node>`: not read here* in place of the tool,
models, gauge and state, **Add a login** takes a typed `<tool>:<name>`, and the page makes no *cannot run* check
on models, saying *not checked: a node's logins are not read here*. The node's own launch still takes
the first usable login that offers the model. **A node that finds no login of the list offering the
model reports that on the record**: the home ends the holder, spending no offer, and marks the team
*cannot run*, naming the model, until its list or its flow changes; a node whose logins offer it but
are not usable now holds the session waiting, as the home does. Drawing a node's logins is the later
work §4.2b names.

#### The built-in seats and their page

**Five seats are built in, and neither a team nor a flow sets them.** The tech lead, the manager, board
answers, pull requests nobody holds and the sweeper (§4.9c *Built-in seats are no part of a flow*) each
need a model and a login as any session does, and both come from **a built-in set**: a name, a model
for each of the five, and a list of logins in the order they are tried. **A repo uses one set**, and
every team of the repo is served by it. **One set, `default`, always exists**, and a repo uses it unless
it names another. So a person who wants the same models and logins behind every team sets `default`
once and points nothing at it; a person who wants one repo's built-in seats on other models makes a
second set and picks it for that repo. Nothing is taken from a team, so a repo's second team inherits
nothing from its first and no page has to say whose models are in use.

**The store.** `settings.yml` holds `builtins.<set>`: `label`, `models` (a model family under each of
`tech-lead`, `manager`, `board-answers`, `unheld-pull-requests` and `sweeper`; a seat left out runs on
the package's default) and `logins` (`<tool>:<name>`, in order, as a team's list is). A repo names its
set in `repos.<repo>.builtins`; absent, it is `default`. A set's name follows a team's rule (unique
case-folded, a login's characters, fixed once made) and carries a label that changes freely.
`builtins.default` is written by the cut-over's move and can never be deleted; **where the file holds
none**, before the move, `default` reads as the package's models and an empty list, and `ao builtins
show` says *after the switch-over*.

**When they run.** The tech lead and the manager act for one team's sessions, and run while that team
is started **and on while a wound-down team's sessions finish**, so a finishing session's question is
still answered; they start nothing once the team's last session has ended. Board answers, pull requests nobody holds and the sweeper act for a repo and are **the
repo's seats**: they run while **any** of the repo's teams is started, wait only while none is, and the
first Start of any of them brings them back; winding one team down leaves them running for the others.
**Tagging** a repo's untagged entries (the frame, *Tagging is the repo's, not a flow's*; §4.4 *An entry
names its team*) is a call on the set's `tech-lead` model, made for the repo on the same terms as the
repo's seats, one call for the repo and never one per team. A tech lead's or a manager's session is its
team's: Wind down lets it finish its call, and the team's stop time ends it with the team's others. A repo's
seat's session finishes its call when the repo's last started team is wound down, and a stop time ends
it only when it stops the repo's last started team. Built-in seats are counted against no team's
numbers (*Sessions at once*).

**The login a seat starts on** is the first login of the set's list that offers the seat's model and is
usable now, by the same function a team's stage uses (*The logins, in order*), given the set's list.
**No team's `reserve` is added for a set's list**: a login there is usable by its own account's reserve
and limits alone.
Where none is usable now the seat waits, as a stage's session does (§6 *Usage gate*). **A set cannot
run a seat** while its list is empty or no login in it offers that seat's model: *default cannot run
the sweeper: no login in its list offers haiku*. The home then starts nothing for that seat, the set's
tab carries a dot, and the team pages of every repo that uses the set say it. **A team is not stopped
by it**: its stages and audits run on its own flow and list.

**Where a seat's session launches.** A tech lead's or manager's on the host of the team it acts for; a
repo's seat's, and a tagging call, on the host of the first, by name, of the repo's started teams. The
home does not read a node's logins (§4.2b *Not here*), so for a session launched on a node the set's
list is taken as names, the node's own launch takes the first usable one that offers the model, and
the home makes no *cannot run* reading for it, as for a team on a node; a node's report that none
offers the model is the launch path's (TD-1976).

**The page.** **Built-in seats** is the sixth item of the settings area's rail (*The page*, above).
`/builtins/<set>` opens a set; the sets are tabs, `default` first and the rest by name, a dot on one
that cannot run a seat, and **+ New set** at the row's end; `/builtins` opens `default`. The bands, in
order:

1. **The head** — the set's label, or its name where it has none, with ✎, and the name beside a label;
   *used by `<repo>`, `<repo>`* or *used by no repo*.
2. **The seats** — a row for each of the five: its name, what it does in a few words, whom it acts for
   (*each team* or *the repo*), a **Model** pick of the models the Tools page lists, and the login a
   session would start on now, or the *cannot run* words.
3. **Logins, in the order they are tried** — the team page's band, on this set's list: the same rows,
   ▲, ▼, × and **Add a login**. A set has no host, so **Add a login** lists the home's logins and also
   takes a typed `<tool>:<name>` for a node's; a name the home cannot read is drawn *not read here*, as
   a node team's is. The seats band's login now, the tab's dot and *cannot run* are the home's reading
   of the home's logins only.
4. **Repos** — a row per registered repo with a pick of the set it uses; the same band on every set's
   page, so the pick is made in one place.
5. **Delete `<set>`** — refused for `default`, and for a set a repo names until that repo's pick is
   moved; otherwise a second press removes it.

**+ New set** asks for a name and an optional label and makes a set with `default`'s models and an
empty list, which says it cannot run until a login is added. A new repo, and a new team's repo, use
`default` with no question asked.

**Writes** are the page's alone, each a person's own act (`mail.PERSON_ONLY`), version-checked and
gated on the `cut_over` marker as the team page's are (*Controls and verbs*, *Before and after the
cut-over*): `builtins_new {name, label?}`, `builtins_delete {set, version}`, `builtins_set {set, label |
models | logins, version}` and `repo_builtins {repo, set, version}`. **`ao builtins show [<set>]`**
prints what the page shows. Before the cut-over the page is drawn from the rail with *after the
switch-over* in place of its bands and writes nothing.

**At the cut-over** the move writes `builtins.default`, and no repo names a set. Its models and its
logins are the person's to give beforehand, on the cut-over's page of prepared moves beside each team's
flow pick (*What the cut-over's move must produce*); **a `default` with an empty list stops the
window, named**.

**The Tools page** names the sets as it names the teams: a login's row lists the built-in sets whose
list holds it, each a link, and Forget is refused while a set's list names the login (§4.2b).

#### Controls and verbs

Every control has a row in §4.5a. **Writes of `repo`, `host`, `flow`, `label`, `logins`, `at_once` and `most`
are the page's alone**, a stated exception to *every control has an `ao` verb*, as
the Flows page's saves are (§4.9c *The Flows page*): `team_new`, `team_delete`, `team_switch` and
`team_set {team, label | logins | at_once | most, version}`, each a person's own act
(`mail.PERSON_ONLY`) and version-checked as the Tools page's writes are (§4.2b *Controls and verbs*),
so a page drawn before another change is refused and drawn afresh. The running settings keep
`set_settings` and their verbs (`ao schedule`, `ao team until`, `ao team reserve`, `ao team balance`,
§4.7; `ao team on-work` until the cut-over). **`ao team show <team>`** prints what the page shows: the head, the flow
with each stage's model, login now and number and the entries in it, the audits, the built-in set its repo uses, the
logins in order with each one's state, and the running settings; `--json` gives the same read the page
draws from. `ao team flow`, `ao team size`, `ao team max` and `ao team add` are the first shape's and
go at the cut-over.

#### Before and after the cut-over

The builds land with no format-3 flow running, because the cut-over waits on them (TD-2056). **A team
is switched** when `settings.yml` holds `teams.<team>.repo` — one function, as §4.2b's `switched` is
for a host — which the cut-over's move writes for every team and **New team** writes for a new one.
It is what splits a team's page (below), and **`teams.<team>.flow` is read as a format-3 flow's name
only for a switched team**; for any other it is the first shape's key. **The page's writes are gated
on the cut-over's marker**, `cut_over` in `settings.yml` (*What the cut-over's move must produce*),
not on a host's `switched`: until TD-2056 has written it, `team_new`, `team_delete`, `team_switch`
and `team_set` are refused, *after the switch-over*, and **+ New team** is drawn disabled with those
words.

- **Before**, for a team that is not switched: its tab draws no *cannot run* dot, **+ New team** is
  drawn disabled with *after the switch-over* (the first build draws it so), and the page draws the
  head (the state word the Sessions card shows for the team, and its counts from the team's live
  members, as that card counts them) and **When it runs**, live, on today's keys — these
  rows leave the Settings page's team card when that build lands, the card keeping the first shape's
  flow pick and **Apply** and a link to the page. In place of the flow band and the logins band it
  says *after the switch-over*. The rail, the routes and `ao team show` are live.
- **After**, the page is whole, the Settings page has no team card, and the Members dialog and its
  rows are gone with the first shape (§4.9c *What this replaces*).

The logins a team lists exist only on a switched host (§4.2b), and the stage records the map draws
only once a format-3 flow runs, so the page's later builds are tested on fixtures and seen live only
after the window.
