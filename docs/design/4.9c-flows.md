### 4.9c Flows: the path an entry takes through a team

**TD-307, TD-314; built by TD-309, TD-313, TD-315; the repos moved onto it by TD-310.** The dated record is `scripts/history.py 4.9c`; this section says what is true now.

**Terms used below.** A **flow** is a team's path: an ordered list of **stages**, each a role, the
lane it gives and its **stage brief** (the words that tell the stage's members the path). A flow is
**usable** when it is whole — every brief present, every role resolved and of the kind its stage
wants. A team lists the flows it may run; the one it runs now is its **current flow**, a setting. A
listed flow is **followable** by a team when its members can staff every stage. A member whose role
has a stage only in another listed flow **sits out** under the current one. **Apply** brings a live
team's sessions in line with its current flow. A role's **kind** (`worker`, `seat`, `manager`,
`plain`) is all a stage asks of it.

**A flow is a path and its words, defined once.** A flow is an ordered list of **stages**; each
stage names a role, the lane it gives, and **its brief** — the words that say what the stage takes,
how it ends and where its work goes next. A team lists the flows it may run and runs one of them at
a time; which one is a setting the person turns, and **Apply** moves the running team onto it. A change to the
path is a change to a flow — its stages or its stage briefs — or a switch to another flow, never
an edit to a repo's prose.

**Roles and flows are orthogonal, and a role's kind is their whole interface.** A **role** says how
a session works in shiftlead — its template's mechanics, its profile, grants, icon, label, message
line, prompts and context bound (§4.8) — and nothing of any path. A **flow** says the path — its
stages, their lanes and their briefs — and names roles only by name. What a flow needs of a role is
one fact, its **kind**, the role key **`kind:`**, one of

- **`worker`** — a member that takes a lane and declares (`grinder`, `hunter`, `designer`); the
  default for a role that writes none;
- **`seat`** — filled when its trigger fires and never declares (§4.9b: `techlead`, `auditor`);
- **`manager`** — the team's `manager:`; and **`plain`** — a person's own session.

The kind is checked where a team is read too, not only where a flow is: a definition's `manager:`
names a `manager` role (or `person`), each `members:` entry naming a role a `worker` (a
`{team: …}` member is refused, §4.9), each `seats:` entry and the `techlead:` seat a `seat`, and each
`entries:` value a `worker` or a `seat` — so a page that offers roles for a slot offers the kind the slot takes,
and a role a person defines is placed by its kind alone. **`kind` is a definition's key**: written
in a preset or a role directory's `role.yml`, and refused in the `roles:` overlays of `org.yml` and
`.shiftlead.yml`: no install or repo changes what a flow may ask of a role it did not define.

A stage that gives a lane names a `worker` role; a stage that gives none is a **review stage** and
names a `seat` role; `manager` and `plain` roles are in no stage. **A review stage is held by one
seat of its role**: the team's `techlead:` seat for the role `techlead`, and one `seats:` entry on
`asks` for any other (*A review stage any seat may hold*, below) — that seat's td-max extras included, which
are its one reader since they share its role, brief and profile (§4.9 *Scaling with the work*, TD-1529),
where a pool's members are not. So a new role is usable in every flow whose stages
want its kind, and a new flow in every team whose members have the kinds it wants — the two are
written apart and meet only at a stage's `role:` and its kind. **A role knows nothing of flows**:
under a flow, a role's own `review:` is set aside (the flow says what waits, item 2 below) and its
default `lane` is only the last fallback; a team with no flow reads both as written.

**Where flows and roles live: each a directory, whole.** A flow is a directory holding **`flow.yml`**
(its stages) and its stage briefs; a role defined outside the package is a directory holding
**`role.yml`** (its `ROLE_KEYS`, `kind` among them) and **`template.md`** (its mechanics, with the
template slots), which makes it a peer of a preset. They live in three places, resolved as a team's
name is (§4.9 *The org is an aggregate*) — the org's over a repo's over the package's, so an install
overrides a repo's without a PR:

- **the package**: `shiftlead/flows/<name>/` for the built-in flows; the built-in roles are the
  presets (`repoconfig.PRESETS`, `shiftlead/briefs/<role>.md`), each with its `kind`;
- **the org**: `~/.shiftlead/flows/<name>/` and `~/.shiftlead/roles/<name>/`, every team's to use, kept
  in the home's history beside the three files it already tracks (§4.9 *What is left at the home has
  a history*), committed by the home as `org.yml` is;
- **a repo**: `<repo>/.shiftlead/flows/<name>/` and `<repo>/.shiftlead/roles/<name>/`, its own teams'
  only (two repos may each define one name, since neither sees the other's), changed by PR.

A flow's or a role's name is its directory's. **Each is replaced whole, never merged by key**: two
stage lists have no one merge, and a role defined in two places is the org's. **A directory may not
take a preset's name** — refused when read and by `ao org check` — since a preset is the package's
and is redefined by key only (§4.8); nor may a flow directory take a built-in flow's name (`td`,
`build-review`, `build`): a repo or an install that wants other words defines its own flow under its
own name, so a built-in's stage briefs are never a repo's prose by another road. A repo's role
directory the org's shadows is said by `ao org`, as a shadowed team is. What layers by key is unchanged and is not a definition:
`org.yml`'s `roles:` overlay (a role's profile for this install) and a repo's `.shiftlead.yml`
`roles:` (its supplement brief, §4.8), each over the role wherever it is defined. The two orders
differ on purpose — definitions resolve the org's over a repo's, whole; overlays lay the repo's over
the org's, per key (`repoconfig.resolve_role`). **A `roles:` key naming
no preset and no directory is refused** when the file is read (*unknown role*, naming the
directories that would define it): an overlay is not a definition. A repo's `.shiftlead.yml` is
refused as it is read; `org.yml`'s `roles:` may lay over a role a repo defines, which the org file
cannot see, so its key naming nothing is refused where the role is resolved (a start, a flow's
check) and named by `ao org check` against every role directory it can see. A role directory that
is not whole is left out of `ao roles` and the pick-lists, and named by `ao org check`. A role defined in a directory writes its `kind` in `role.yml` (`worker`
where unwritten); an overlay never does. The YAML files keep no `flows:` key: a flow is found by its directory.
**An org flow or role is not usable by a team on a node** (§4.4a): a node team's roles and briefs
are read on the node, and the org's directories are the home's; such a team is told so as *not
followable* (*hunt cannot be followed by cm-grind: an org flow, and cm-grind runs on contractmatch —
define it in the repo*; likewise a repo flow of cm-grind's naming an org role, *security is an org
role*). Whether a node team can follow a flow is read as its start reads the node — the client
asks the node's files (`teams.Files`, `host_files`) for the repo's flow and role directories and the
briefs, so a repo-local flow or role the node's checkout does not hold yet (a branch not merged
there) is *not followable* by name, and a node that does not answer reads *unknown*, never
followable. A package flow rides the wheel and a repo's the checkout, so both reach a
node; the org's directories are not copied to nodes.

**Made to be edited from a page, later.** Nothing here builds a page that creates a role or a flow,
but the shape is the one such a page needs: a role or a flow is a directory of whole files, so a page
writes, replaces or removes whole files and never splices a line into a shared YAML file as
**Members…** must (§4.9); the checks of *A flow is usable only when it is whole* (below) are the
form's errors, in the words `ao org check` prints; the org's directories are the home's to commit,
as `commit_defs` commits `org.yml` today; and a repo's are a PR's, drawn read-only as a repo-defined
team's **Members…** is. When that page is designed it adds controls, not a format. **Until then a
person creates a flow or a role by writing its directory** — under `~/.shiftlead/` at once, or in a
repo by PR.

A built-in flow's `flow.yml`:

```yaml
# shiftlead/flows/td/flow.yml — design → build → review; briefs beside it
stages:
  - {name: design, role: designer, lane: [design-first, "owner:designer"], brief: design.md}
  - {name: build,  role: grinder,  lane: [free-pick, "owner:grinder"],     brief: build.md}
  - {name: review, role: techlead,                                          brief: review.md}

# shiftlead/flows/build-review/flow.yml — build → review
stages:
  - {name: build,  role: grinder,  lane: [free-pick, "owner:grinder"],     brief: build.md}
  - {name: review, role: techlead,                                          brief: ../td/review.md}

# shiftlead/flows/build/flow.yml — build only, nothing reviewed
stages:
  - {name: build,  role: grinder,  lane: [free-pick, "owner:grinder"],     brief: build.md}
```

A stage is one mapping in `stages:` — `name`, `role`, `lane`, `brief`, an optional `color`, and on a review stage `held:` (the name of a path set: *A review
stage any seat may hold*, below), and nothing else, a stray key
refused naming it as in a team, a stray top-level key in `flow.yml` likewise:

- **`name`**: the stage's word, unique in the flow, shown in the brief's `{flow}` lines, the card's
  flow strip (the flow chip's tooltip), `ao team list` and the **stage chip** of a team card's *TDs in
  motion* rows (*A claim's chip is its stage*, below), and read by no rule (item 4 below finds the design stage by its
  lane, not its name).
- **`role`**: a role by name, whose kind fits the stage: a `worker` where the stage gives a lane,
  a `seat` role where it gives none, held by the team's one seat of that role on `asks` (above). Two stages of one role are refused when the flow
  is read: a member would have two lanes.
- **`lane`**: the lane words (§6 rule 6, or the role's own lane shape, §4.8's table: a hunter's
  area or `free`) the stage gives each member of its role; required on a worker's stage, absent on a
  review stage (a seat has no lane, §4.8).
- **`brief`**: the stage brief, a Markdown file, relative to the flow's directory; `../<flow>/<file>`
  reaches a sibling flow's brief **in the same place** (package, org or one repo), and
  **`package:<flow>/<file>`** reaches a built-in flow's brief from any place — the package is
  versioned with the code that reads it, so a reference into it cannot drift as a copy would. A brief
  never reaches into the org's flows from a repo, or into another repo's. Required: a stage with no
  brief is no stage.
- **`color`**: the stage chip's colour, a **palette name** — `pink`, `indigo`, `blue`, `amber`,
  `teal`, `green` or `violet`, each a light and a dark value in the UI's stylesheet (`--hue-<name>`),
  never a hex value, which would hold one theme's. Red is not in it: it is the stalled and alarm colour.
  A word outside the palette is refused naming it, as a stray key is. Optional; a flow's colours are
  given in three passes: every written `color` first; then each unwritten stage whose name has a
  default — *find* pink, *design* indigo, *build* blue, *review* amber — takes it unless a written one
  took it; then each stage still without one takes, in stage order, the first palette name no stage
  of the flow holds, and past the seventh the palette again from the start. So two stages share a
  colour only where one writes it or the flow outgrows the palette. Grey is no palette name: it is
  the chip of a claim no stage holds (*A claim's chip is its stage*, below). A live copy older than
  TD-1765 refuses `color` as a stray key, so a repo flow that writes it waits on that promote.

**A claim's chip is its stage (TD-704).** Each row of a team card's *TDs in motion* (§4.5a) leads
with a chip that says where the claim is now, read from the team's flow — its name, its colour and
its place in the order — so a flow with a stage of its own gets a chip of its own, and a repo that
wants another word or colour writes its own flow (*More flows, and more roles*, below; its briefs may
still be a built-in's, `package:`), the one way a repo overrides a flow. Paul, 2026-11-12: *accuracy
is key here, the user needs to be able to trust the ui* — so a chip says only what a reading shows,
and where no reading says, it falls back to what the record does say, never a guess.

**Stage names are read** only where the team's flow is there and usable — read against the team's
`repo` from the home's own checkout (`repo_board.team_stages`); a node team's checkout, a read that
fails and a flow that is not usable are not read — and, for one member, only while no pending Apply
would move it to another stage: its flow view's difference (`teamrun` *Difference*) is a `relaunch`
with a `lane ` line, or a `rename`, and it still runs what it was applied with, which no record
names. A difference whose lines are only `stage ` (a brief's path), `reader `, `profile ` or `seat `
changes nothing here; a `sit_out` or `left` member has no stage of the flow. Where a holder's or a
reader seat's stage name is not read, the rules below read its role in grey. A claim's chip is the
first of these that applies:

1. **A PR no longer open** — the holder's stage by rule 4 (grey by rule 5), marked *merged* /
   *closed*, until its holder marks the claim done or dropped (§4.4); never *you* and never a
   reader's stage — no reads are kept past the close, and the mark says what happened. A question
   about the PR is not read for such a claim.
2. **The person has it** — grey **you**. **The PR**: the claim's PR (its `pr`, `review_pr`, or the
   open PR whose branch names the reference, §4.5a), looked up in the reading of the claim's own
   record's repo, has a `person` entry in its reads (`pr_orders.reads`, §4.4 *The PR's reads*) that is
   `open` — a chain whose reader is `person`, an ask passed up past a reader's bound, a seat's own
   pass-up to the person — or `answered` without `again` while no other addressee's entry is newer
   (§4.9b: *I'll read it* and a typed reply leave the PR the person's until their merge or a newer
   ask; *Ask the reader again* hands it back). **Or the work**: the holder has an open `ask` or
   `steer` to the person whose `about` is the claim's reference or its PR as `#n` — §6 rule 5's
   *waiting* reading, the match §4.10 *A question the work settled closes itself* makes too (TD-814:
   a design waiting on the person is not being written; a build waiting on an answer is not being
   built).
3. **A seat has the PR** — the review stage that seat holds (a scaled seat's extra read as its seat,
   `held.extras_of`), where an entry of a seat is `open`; where two are open, the later stage in the
   flow's order, and a seat that holds a stage before one that holds none. A seat that holds no
   stage — a team with no flow, whose `review:` is a role's own (§4.9b); a reader the flow dropped at
   a switch (*What a switch leaves alone*); the org's director seat holding a pass-up (§4.9b) — or
   whose stage name is not read, reads its role in grey.
4. **Otherwise the claim is its holder's** — the worker stage (one that gives a lane) whose `role` is
   the holder's role: *design* for a designer, *build* for a grinder, *find* for a hunter under the
   hunt flow — by the holder's role, never the entry's `Kind:` (a grinder holding a design-first entry
   is building it). This covers a claim with no PR, a PR nobody has asked (not yet green, or on no
   held path), and a PR every addressee is done with: any answer of a seat while the PR is open,
   whatever its verdict (a `merged` read before the PR reads merged included), an ask closed or
   expired unanswered, and the person's *Ask the reader again* — the PR is its author's again. A
   seat's `pass` to an interactive asker is such an answer: its row reads its holder's stage, beside
   the person glyph its holder already carries.
5. **Grey, a role's name**, where rule 4 finds no stage: a holder whose role is in no worker stage
   of the flow (a hunter outside the hunt flow, an auditor seat, a member that *sits out*), a team
   with no flow, or a holder whose stage name is not read — its role; a session with no role,
   *session*. Rule 2 and a seat's role read the same with no flow at all.

Elsewhere *in review #n* (the Queue card, a design's *its builds*, §4.8) still means only that the
claim has a PR, a different word from the chip's.

A reference whose holders read different chips is one row per chip, each naming its holders and the
PR their claims name; the facet's count, *TDs in motion (n)*, counts references, and every reader of
the rows by reference (the Repo page's *held by* and a design's builds) joins a reference's rows. The
chips sort in the flow's stage order, the grey ones after them by word, *you* last; the heading counts
them in that order (*2 design · 2 build · 2 review · 1 hunter*), and the chip column is as wide as the
card's longest chip. The view derives every chip (`shiftlead.ui.org.motion_rows`) from the records,
each claim's repo's `pr_orders.reads` and the team's stages, read as the Repo board reads them
(`repo_board.team_stages`, §4.5 screen 11) — never declared, and never a reason for any rule to act. A
stage name is only a word: mockup B's *fact-check* is a review stage of a reader role of the repo's,
never the `fact-checker` seat, which reads no PR (§4.9b *The fact-check*).

**"If needed" is the entry's, never the team's.** Every stage of a team's flow is the team's;
which entries pass through it is said by what the stage reads: only a `design-first` entry is in
the design stage's lane, so a debt entry starts at build; only a PR on a held path waits for the
review (§4.9b), so a PR elsewhere merges at build. How a stage *ends* is not configured: each ends
on the fact the home already reads for it — a design entry when it reads *Designed* and the build entries
that carry it out name it in `Part of:` (it archives with the last of them, below), a build claim when its PR merges, a review when the reader answers
(§4.9b). There is no mover between stages.

**The designer records its PR** (TD-1526). Once a design PR is open — its number is the forge's — the
designer appends it to the design entry's **`Design PR:`** section in that same PR: `ledger.py edit
TD-<design> --section "Design PR" --from <file>`, the file the old list with `, #<n>` after it. A later
round's PR (a bought round's own entry is `Part of` the design) is appended to the design's list, never to
the round's entry; a design PR closed unmerged never reaches the list, its edit landing only with its merge. The
section lands with the merge, since the ledger is read against origin; until then the PR's title links it,
so the title leads with the entry's id and the word *design* — *TD-1526 design: …*; a bought round's, and a `Hold`
revision's, leads with its own entry's id (*TD-<round>: TD-<design>'s …*, its entry `Part of` the design) and is linked once it
merges and appends its number. The section is what an item about the design links
(§4.5 screen 6 *An item about a design links the design's PR*); it stays when the design archives.

present tense, so it says what the design says at that PR's head. It is written last, after the round's fixes,
and is in the closing fact-check's diff like any file of the PR, which checks it says nothing the design does
not and leaves out none of the design's choices the person would weigh. **Its shape:** a `# TD-<n>: <the
entry's title>` heading (the title with its backticks dropped), then four `##` sections in this order — *What it does*, *The key choices, and why*,
*What it rules out*, *What you will see* — each a few sentences or short bullets, the whole at most 40
non-blank lines as committed, its headings counted, each line wrapped at 120 columns (a synopsis's own rule, the test's), so it reads on one screen. **Its writing rule** is a message to the person's (§4.10 *How a message to
a person is written*), read cold: plain words, no finding id (`F12`), no function, file, path or line
reference, no backticks; a ledger id (`TD-1476`) only where it names other work the person knows by it.

**What a design's author prepares before a review** (TD-1720, a part of TD-1711's cheaper way to design; Paul,

design's claims and repeated rules in **the prep file**, and run **the pre-review script**, whose pass on the commit
is **the gate** every reviewer meets first. They are this repo's alone for the trial TD-1722 runs, written in the
designer brief (`docs/briefs/designer-ao-1.md`) and one script, `scripts/design_prep.py`; cadence §4.8's rounds,
fact-checks, cap, stop rule, record and end-of-rounds item are unchanged, and the shape goes upstream to
agent-cadence only if the trial passes ([plan](../decisions/2026-11-11-fable-reset-plan.md)). Below, *the fixer* is
whoever applies a round's fixes: the designer, the design's only writer (no seat applies them in its place, TD-1721).

**The checklist: fifteen questions, weighted to the hard situations.** The designer brief holds, under *The author's
checklist*, a numbered list of at most fifteen questions of at most twelve words each, drawn from the `q` column of
`patterns.csv` (beside [the review findings analysis](../decisions/2026-11-11-design-review-findings.md): the
question the author should have answered, for each of the 1,949 findings about a situation a design had not
handled). The places go by how many of a situation's findings only a design review or the live system caught (395 in
all, the one such row of no named situation left out), largest remainder: interaction 3, concurrency 2, when 2,
scope 2, and one each for ends, who, failure, restart, which and shape, so eleven of the fifteen go to the hard
situations (interaction, concurrency, when, ends, who, failure, restart); empty-first, multi-node, display and proof
get none. The places are fixed from all 395 rows and do not move with the replays' pick. Within a situation the
questions are those covering the most rows, rows asking the same thing counted together, and each question's
situation and the ids of the rows it covers are kept in `checklist.csv` beside `patterns.csv`. **No row of the three
designs TD-1722 replays is drawn from**, so a replay never meets its own findings as a question. The three are
Paul's pick (2026-11-12, TD-1722's Log): TD-1557, TD-1569 and TD-1470, of which only TD-1470's rows are in
`patterns.csv`, so its rows are the ones left out. The author reads the checklist before drafting and again before
the first review, and answers a question in the design itself wherever the design meets its situation: there is no
answers block, since an answer worth writing is a rule a reviewer reads. The list changes only by a design PR, and
until TD-1722's trial ends never takes a row from a replayed design.

**The prep file: what the design claims, and what it says twice.** Every design here has
**`docs/reviews/TD-<design>.prep.yml`** beside its review record (a bought round or a `Hold` revision writes the
design's own file, as it writes the design's record; for a design whose rounds began before the gate, below, the
file is optional and a run on it is `--advisory`, its FAIL stopping no review). The author writes it with the draft,
before the first review; it is YAML with four keys, each a list, any of them empty:

```yaml
claims:   # what existing code or docs do, each where it rests
  - says: lane_matches routes a decided design line to the design-first lanes
    at: src/sessionorc/ledger.py:359         # a path at the commit, a line in it
    has: "def lane_matches"                    # optional: text the file must hold
  - says: the design is 30 section files
    count: {glob: "docs/design/*.md"}          # files matching; grep: narrows them, lines: true counts lines
    is: 30
rules:    # a rule the design states in more than one place
  - name: the gate before a reviewer
    find: "(?i)no reviewer is sent"            # a regex locating every statement of it
    terms: ["pass", "commit"]                   # what every such statement must hold
new:      # a path the design names that a build will create
  - path: scripts/design_prep.py
    by: TD-1755                                 # a build the PR files, whose Fix names the path
waive:    # a flag the author holds false, for the fact-check to weigh
  - check: rules
    rule: the gate before a reviewer           # a rule waiver names its rule
    file: docs/design/4.9c-flows.md
    text: "No reviewer is sent a mockup it has not seen"   # the paragraph's opening, not a line number
    why: a different rule that shares the phrase
```

**Who writes it, and when it grows.** The author declares every claim the design makes about what existing code, a
doc or another section does, and every rule it states in two or more places (the design's sections, the briefs, the
build entries' Fix and Done when, the synopsis); a rule's `terms` are the author's, never inferred (Paul's lean,
2026-11-11), and are the words, numbers and names that make it that rule. **The fixer keeps it true**: a fix that
writes a claim adds it, a fix that changes a rule's terms changes them in the file first, so the script then names
every statement still saying the old thing, and a fix that states a rule in a second place declares it. **The
fact-check reads it**: each round's fact-check checks every claim added or changed since the last one (round 1's
every claim) at its `at`, reads every waiver, and raises as a finding a claim about code in the changed text that
the file does not declare (`minor`, and the fixer declares it) or a waiver that hides a real fault (`build-changing`
when what it hides is). A false claim is a finding as today.

**The pre-review script: four checks, largest first.** `scripts/design_prep.py --entry TD-<design> [--rev <commit>]
[--base <commit>]` checks the design at a commit, never at a work tree, and reads the prep file at that commit too:
`--rev` defaults to `HEAD`, and every commit is read in a temporary detached worktree, added to the repository of
the worktree the script is run from at a fresh `mktemp -d` path and removed when it ends (its tests run with that
tree's `src` first on `PYTHONPATH`); a run killed before it removes the tree leaves a prunable entry, and every run
first lists the repository's worktrees and removes each of its own, under its `design-prep-` prefix, whose directory
is gone (`git worktree remove --force`), never running a bare `git worktree prune`, which would touch every
session's. A run with `HEAD` while the work tree holds uncommitted or untracked changes under the paths below or to
the prep file (`git status --porcelain` on them) stops NOT PREPARED, since its pass would name a commit that is not
the text checked. **The changed text** is the lines the PR adds against `--base` (by default the merge base of the
commit with the local `origin/main`, which a fetch can move, so the pass line names the base it used), in
`docs/design/`, `docs/briefs/`, `docs/ledger/`, `docs/synopses/` and `docs/glossary.md`. **A rule's statements** are
looked for in every file under `docs/design/`, `docs/briefs/` and `docs/synopses/`, in `docs/glossary.md`, and in
the ledger entries the PR adds or changes (the rest of the ledger is left out: its old entries keep the words of
their day), paragraph by paragraph: **a paragraph** is a block between blank lines, except that each top-level list
item with its continuation lines and each table row is one of its own, and in a ledger entry each `**Field:**` body
(Why, Fix, Done when, Ask and the rest) is one paragraph, any list inside it included. **Every match is on a
paragraph with each run of whitespace read as one space**, so a phrase wrapped across a line still matches — a
`find`, a term, a pointer and a waiver's text alike; a claim's `has` is matched exactly, within one line of its file
as it stands, fenced lines included. Fenced blocks are read by no check. The checks, with how many of the 510
findings the analysis calls a script's to catch each would have caught, as two Sonnet judges read them against round
1's draft of it, in which a count was a shell command rather than a glob (`prep-yield.csv` beside `patterns.csv`,
its prompt beside it):

1. **rules** (130): every paragraph matching a rule's `find` (a Python regex) holds each of its `terms`, a term
   matched as whole words and ignoring case (`pass` does not match *bypass*); a rule whose `find` matches fewer than
   two paragraphs is flagged too, as declared but not repeated.
2. **claims** (89): every claim's `at` file exists at the commit and its line, which is optional, is in the file — a
   path in this repo, or `agent-cadence:<path>` for a file of the pinned agent-cadence tree (`agent-cadence path`) —
   and its `has` text is in the file; every `count` equals its `is` — a count is a glob of repo paths at the commit,
   narrowed by an optional `grep` regex to the files holding it, or with `lines: true` the number of matching lines,
   and runs no command, so a reviewer running the script runs nothing the author wrote; every repo path written in
   the changed text — a maximal run of letters, digits and `_./-` that holds a `/` and ends in a file extension, a
   `:<line>` after it and any trailing `.,;:)` dropped, not starting `~` or `/`, not part of a URL, and not inside a
   token holding `<`, `*` or `$` — exists, a markdown link's target resolved against its file's directory and any
   other path against the repo's top or the pinned agent-cadence tree, or is a `new` path. A `new` path passes while
   it does not exist and its `by` names an entry in the ledger or its archive at the commit, or one the PR adds,
   whose Fix holds the path; once the path exists the item is a flag, so the author drops it. 86 of the 89 come from
   what the author declared (17 of them a count), 3 from the paths alone.
3. **pointers** (4): every design reference in the changed text names a section in `docs/design.md`'s index, and a
   `§<n> *Title*` a bold lead or heading in that section's file, the title equal, ignoring case and trailing
   punctuation, to the lead whole or to its part before a colon or an opening parenthesis (*The fact-check* names
   *The fact-check: a seat another tool fills (TD-857).*); a `§` is another document's, and skipped, when the word
   before it, or before the first `§` of the comma- or *and*-joined list it is in, is *cadence*, *ADR*, *SPEC* or
   *rationale*, or a link or a file name. Every `TD-<n>` is in the ledger, its archive, or filed by the PR.
4. **tools** (2): `agent-cadence run ledger.py --check --since <the merge base>` and the doc-bound tests
   (`tests/test_synopses.py`, `tests/test_design_doc.py`, `tests/test_history.py`) pass at the commit, each under
   five minutes; the ledger check's errors and pytest's exit 1 are flags, and a tool that will not start, a timeout,
   or any other pytest exit (collection, usage, no tests) is one that could not run.

That is at most 225 of the 510, 44%, and only 9 of them with no declaration: the other 216 count as caught because
the judges assumed an author who had declared the very rule or claim that later went wrong, which is hindsight, so
the prep file, not the script, is where any catch is, and TD-1722's trial measures what a real author's declarations
catch. Each judge read half the rows, so there is no agreement figure. Of the other 285, 282 needed a reader — a

end-of-rounds item and the PR body restate rules but are outside the text it reads, and the closing fact-check reads
them.

**Its result is a pass line.** NOT PREPARED is decided before any check runs; then every check runs, and the exit is
the worst that is left. Exit 2 prints `design-prep: NOT PREPARED · <why>` — what the author controls: no prep file
at the commit, a malformed one, uncommitted changes — which the author mends. Exit 1, any flag left after the
waivers, prints `design-prep: FAIL · …` and one line for each flag, `<check> · <file>:<line> · <what>` (a rule's
`<what>` leading with the rule's name and the terms missing) (a paragraph's flag on the line the paragraph starts),
`<what>` holding the flag's text verbatim, and one line for each tool that could not run. Exit 3, no flag but a tool
that could not run, the script's own crash, or a commit or base it cannot resolve (which a fetch or the sender's
push mends), prints `design-prep: CANNOT RUN · <why>`. **`--advisory`**, for a design the gate does not hold, prints
the same lines under `design-prep: ADVISORY · …` and exits 0 whatever it found. Exit 0 prints `design-prep: PASS ·
TD-<design> · <commit, 12 characters> · base <commit, 12 characters> · claims <n> · rules <n> · new <n> · waived
<n>` (*waived* counts the flags waivers hid). **A flag's file and text**, which a waiver names to hide it: a rule's
flag is its rule, its paragraph's file and the paragraph as its text, and a rule waiver names the `rule` too, so it
hides that rule's flag alone, matching a paragraph that begins with its text — text of at least eight words, or the
whole paragraph, any shorter making the prep file malformed — so a waiver names one paragraph (or, if two begin
alike, the author lengthens it) and a later drift elsewhere in the file is still flagged; a rule not repeated is the
prep file and the rule's `name`, which a waiver with that file and that text hides, the eight-word floor being a
paragraph waiver's alone; a claim whose `at`, `has` or `count` fails is the prep file and the claim's `says`; a path
in the changed text is its file and the path; a pointer is its file and the reference as written; a ledger check
error is the entry's file and the message; a failing test is its test file and the test's name. A waiver hides every
flag with its check, file and text, whatever line it is on; a waiver that hides no flag is itself a flag (the prep
file, the waiver's text), and that flag no waiver hides, so before a later round's first run, a bought round's or a
`Hold` revision's in a PR of its own, the author drops the waivers the new PR's text no longer needs. **Whoever runs
it** runs `pdm run python scripts/design_prep.py` from its own worktree of the repo, never the main checkout — a
reviewer's worktree with no environment of its own runs it with the main checkout's `.venv` interpreter — and the
script runs its tools under its own interpreter; any output whose first line is not `design-prep:` is read as a
CANNOT RUN.

**The gate: no reviewer reads a design the script has not passed at that commit.** Whether it holds is decided once
for a design, by the designer brief its round 1 starts under, and recorded: before round 1's first run of the script
the sender writes `gated (TD-1720)` on the design entry's Log (`ledger.py note`) and commits it, so the note is in
the commit that passes, and a later round's sender reads it, a design with no note being ungated (the closing
fact-check raises a round 1 whose Rounds lines carry pass lines and whose entry has no note): a design whose round 1
starts under a brief that says it (from TD-1756's merge, for a designer started after it) is gated in every later
round, its bought rounds and `Hold` revisions included; a design whose round 1 began earlier is not, in any round —
its prep file is optional, its sender runs the script with `--advisory` where the file exists, and its reviewers'
prompts carry no script step. Whoever sends a review — each round's fact-check and design reviews, the closing
fact-check, and not the fact-check trial's ask to `fact-check-ao-1` (§4.9b *The fact-check*), which is outside the
rounds and the gate, with no row and no line — runs the script on the commit the review will read and sends none but
on a PASS, or on its own CANNOT RUN, which it files (`ao finding`) and sends in the pass line's place. Each
reviewer's prompt makes its first step `git fetch origin` and the same run with `--rev <the commit it reads> --base
<the base the sender's pass line names>`, the one command a reviewer here runs that is not a read (it makes and
removes a worktree and runs the tests); on exit 1 or 2 its whole reply is `NOT PREPARED` and the script's lines, the
review ends unread, and it gets no row in the record (an aborted review, cadence §4.8); on exit 3 it goes on if it
can read the commit, else its whole reply is the CANNOT RUN line and it gets no row, its reply leading with the
CANNOT RUN line when it goes on, and the sender files the fault (`ao finding`). A reviewer's NOT PREPARED where the
sender's run passed is the script's fault: the sender runs it again with the same `--rev` and `--base`, and if it
still passes files the difference (`ao finding`) and sends the review again, telling the reviewer to go on as on a
CANNOT RUN. **The rounds list:** the PR body holds a `**Rounds**` list, one line for each review, written when its
row is, `R<n> · <round> · <role> · <the pass line it ran on, or the sender's filed CANNOT RUN line>` — `R<n>` the
record's id for the review, `<round>` cadence §4.8's round; a review that ended NOT PREPARED, or that its sender's
run kept from being sent, has no line, as it has no row, and one sent again after a reviewer's NOT PREPARED has the
one line of the review that ran — and a last line `final · <pass line>`, run on the commit that merges: the PR's
head once the closing fact-check's fixes and every commit after them are in (the end-of-rounds item, the builds'
blocks, Asks and Fix, the Status), and run again whenever the head moves before the merge; a FAIL there is mended
with no new review (a mend that changes what the design says, or a waiver added or widened, is a fix no review has
read, listed as cadence §4.8 lists those, so a waiver no fact-check weighed is in front of the person or the
builder), and a CANNOT RUN there is filed and written in the line. The closing fact-check reads the review lines
against the record's rows, the only check after the fact that the gate was kept, a mismatch a `minor` finding the
fixer mends; the `final` line comes after it and no reader checks it. **The ways out when it cannot pass:** a path
that rightly does not exist yet is a `new` path its build names; a false alarm is a waiver, which the fact-check
weighs; a NOT PREPARED is mended. Nothing else passes the gate.

**Who reviews a design's rounds** (TD-1721, a part of TD-1711; re-scoped by Paul, 2026-11-13, from a second seat
that applies the fixes, which the cost model showed would save about 2% of a design's spend; the rule is his answer of
2026-11-13). **Fable reads a design in round 1, and Sonnet in every round.** Round 1's design review is a Fable
review, then a Sonnet one, as every round's was before; from round 2 on, a bought round's and a `Hold` revision's
rounds among them, a round's design review is one Sonnet review, with two exceptions, where Fable reads again:
- **a long design**: in a round past round 4 by the review record's count (a bought round's and a `Hold` revision's
  rounds carry the count on, though a revision's cap starts again), once its Sonnet review has raised a build-changing finding, the fixer
  applies that review's fixes and a Fable review reads the design in the same round, so a round past 4 that still
  finds build-changing work has both reviewers before it ends (Sonnet first here, since its finding is what calls
  Fable back), and such a round never ends them by the stop rule, since Sonnet's finding is build-changing; it adds nothing to a round where Fable has already
  read (a major release's, below). A Fable finding that reopens Sonnet's names its id and is adopted as any finding is (the designer brief's
  *Adopt each finding by the template's three tiers*), and a fix of it that no review reads falls under the brief's
  *A fix no review has read*;
- **a design for a major release**: every round is Fable then Sonnet, each review's fixes applied before the next
  reads, as in round 1. A major release is a version of any repo the
  design's builds change (shiftlead, ledgerboard, agent-cadence) whose first
  number changes (ledgerboard v3.0, for example). The designer states it beside the design's class, before round 1,
  in the design entry's Log; one that becomes a major release's later (its builds or the person's word say so) is
  stated then, and every round from the next is Fable then Sonnet. A design not so stated is not one.
The fact-checks, the cap, the stop rule (read over that round's design reviews, so a round with only Sonnet's ends the
rounds when Sonnet's raises nothing build-changing), the record and the end-of-rounds item are cadence §4.8's and
unchanged; each reviewer is still a fresh subagent given the worktree's path, and Fable's review stays Paul's
exception to the cadence's *never the same* (the designer brief). A design whose round 1 has run takes the rule from
its next round to start after the brief that writes it merges. **Why**, from [the cost
model](../decisions/2026-11-12-design-cost-model.md) (*For TD-1721*): a Fable review cost $3.58 on the mean and a
Sonnet one $0.54 (33 and 98 reviews over five designs, Sonnet's counting its fact-checks), so Fable is about two thirds of a design's reviewers' spend
and two fifths of its whole; and Fable's yield of findings only a design review would catch falls after round 1 —
2.12 for each review in round 1, 1.25 in round 2, 0.66 and 0.77 in rounds 3 and 4 (41 designs), though 1.59 again in
the long designs' rounds past 4, which the first exception answers — while Sonnet, reading second, still raises 1.27
in round 1. On the 41 designs' review counts, a design's reviewers cost about $21 with Fable then Sonnet in every
round and about $10 with Fable in round 1 alone (the long designs' Fable rounds past 4 add to that); Sonnet alone,
round 1 included, about $6, was the third choice. What a round without Fable leaves silent is not measured, since
Sonnet has always read second: the 139 findings of that kind Fable raised after round 1 (3.4 for each design) are the
ones at risk, and the trial's escapes (below) are where a loss would show.

**What the designer carries from one design to the next** (TD-1721; Paul, 2026-11-13). The designer's **context

  author itself, to a session the ask is addressed to, and to a person. So the line reads
  *ui-review · ui-reader-ao-1 · src/shiftlead/ui/\*\* · passed 14:02* and *review · techlead-ao-1 ·
  src/sessionorc/\*\* · **your turn to ask — it merges***; `--json` carries `chain`, each link's
  `state` (`passed`, `asked`, `findings`, `next`, `later`), `turn` and `merges`. A reader runs the
  same command with `--id <the asker>` to learn whether it is the last. The home answers the read
  and times nothing; the check is still the author's own, and a session with the older `review`
  shape reads a chain of one. `pr_reads` lists each ask's `id`, `to`, `at`, `open`, and its last
  reply's `verdict` and `replied_at`, oldest first, and is refused to any other session; a mail
  read, so a node's session reads it over the link. `ao pr held` walks the links that hold the PR
  against the author's asks (`review.walk`), a link's reader matched to an ask's addressee by the
  seat's name, the longest that fits (`addressed`); a `pr_reads` that is refused (a reader not
  asked yet) or that an older home does not know leaves each link *not read* and says why, with no
  turn, never a guess.
- **The words.** The build stage's brief says *run `ao pr held <n>`; ask the reader it names; a PR
  with a link still to read is never yours to merge*, in place of naming `{techlead}`; a review
  stage's brief says how its reader answers — the verdict, and `ao pr held <n> --id <asker>` to
  know whether the merge is its own. `{flow}` draws every review stage with its seat and its
  paths (*… → ui-review (ui-reader-ao-1, on src/shiftlead/ui/\*\*) → review (techlead-ao-1, on
  src/sessionorc/\*\*) → you, through techlead-ao-1*). Questions still go to the `techlead:` seat
  (§4.9b): a review seat of another role reads PRs and answers nothing else — an `ask` that
  carries no `pr` and reaches it (the sideways edge admits any teammate's mail, §4.10) is answered
  with one reply naming the `techlead:` seat as where the team's questions go, never passed up,
  and the review brief says so; the mail gate keys on nothing of it (§9 invariant 9), since a
  seat's role is a badge. `{flow}` is drawn from `flowdefs.review_links` — the techlead stage by
  the seat's id, any other by its seat's name, each with its set's paths.
- **The bound and the person.** Each ask is bounded by the repo's `review.bound`; past it the author asks the person on that thread and **the PR is the person's**, the rest of
  its chain with it: their merge or their word ends it — the question saying why its reader did not answer, and its answer *Ask the reader again* handing the PR back to the reader `ao pr held` then names, as a fresh first ask (§4.9b *Why it did not answer*, TD-1423).
- **A switch mid-chain.** An ask already sent stays with its reader, who answers it (*What a
  switch leaves alone*, above). What is left to read is whatever `ao pr held` says under the
  record as it now stands: a link the new flow dropped is no longer asked, a link it added is, and
  a `pass` already given by a reader the chain as it now stands still names stands — keyed on the
  reader, never on a stage's `name` (the `name` bullet above: read by no rule), which is how `ao pr
  held` matches an ask to a link; whether the paths that reader now holds grew is the last
  reader's judgement, which reads the whole final diff. A reader holding an open ask at a switch
  that leaves it no longer the last answers `pass`, as `ao pr held --id` then tells it. **A seat
  never sits out**: *Worker members the flow does not use sit out* (above) is a rule for worker
  members, which run until wound down, and a seat is none — it is filled only by an ask, costs
  nothing between them, and answers what it was asked, as a `techlead:` seat under a flow with no
  review stage already does.
- **What is shown.** The team header's *n PRs waiting · oldest* already counts every seat's
  `prs_waiting`. The Repo page's PR standing (§4.5 screen 11) and `ao repo`'s name the reader and
  say the verdicts: *passed by ui-reader-ao-1 · waiting on techlead-ao-1 · 40m*, *findings from
  techlead-ao-1*, *merged by techlead-ao-1* (`review.standing`, one reading for both), drawn from the
  repo reading's `pr_orders.reads` (§4.4 *The PR's reads*, TD-704), which the stage chip reads too.
  One word per addressee, in the order the asks were sent, a seat by its record's name, else its id:
  `open`, *waiting on <name> · <age>*; `answered`, *passed by*, *findings from* or *merged by <name>*
  by its verdict, *reviewed by <name>* for none; `closed`, nothing — an ask closed unanswered stands
  for nothing. The person's entry reads *waiting on you · <age>* while open and *with you* once
  answered without `again` while no other entry is newer, both `wait` (`review.standing`'s `cls`);
  otherwise it stands for nothing. An extra's answer is its seat's. A held PR no seat has been asked reads *unasked · 17h*
  in its place, naming the reader of the first servicing team whose review holds a touched path
  (§4.4 *A held PR nobody asked is marked, never ordered*, TD-787). No control is added.

**One flow per team; more kinds of work are more teams.** A team runs one flow at a time. Different
kinds of work on one repo — building the ledger's entries, checking production, a UI-design pass —
are different teams on that repo, each with its own flow, and they hand work on **through the
ledger**, which is already the queue every team reads and where a claim is a lease. A *prod-check*
team whose flow is one stage — a hunter whose lane is the area it probes — files what it finds as
ledger entries, and the TD team's grinders pick them up like any other; neither team knows of the
other, and a TD team that wound down is woken by those filings as §6 rule 8 wakes any team whose
lanes gain work (its `on_work`). Two teams on a repo cost little: their managers and techlead seats are on call (§6 rule 3),
filled only when something needs them, and each team keeps its own schedule, stop time and
reserve (§5 `teams.<team>`), so a nightly prod check runs on a small reserve beside the day's
building. A `balance:` line on either team reads the repo's open PRs, the other team's included
(§6 *Balance*), so it is set with both in mind. What they do not share is the techlead seat: each team's held PRs are read by its own,
both reading the repo's one primer; if one reader for every held PR on a repo ever matters, the
step is teams sharing a seat, not one team running several flows. Add entry hands to the first of the
repo's teams, in definition order, that has a techlead seat (§4.9 *Add an entry to the ledger*).

**Two consequences of the built-ins.** Their lanes carry `owner:grinder`, and an owner word narrows
a lane only where an entry writes an `Owner:` line (§6 rule 6: an entry with none matches), so a
repo whose ledger writes none picks exactly what `free-pick` picks; one whose ledger later writes
`Owner:` lines keeps a member `lane:` of its own if it wants the old reach, since a member's lane
wins. Under `build` a seat reads no review brief: nothing is held, and the seat answers questions
only. A `held:` or a `flows:` key is read only by a live copy that knows it — `repoconfig` refuses an
unknown top-level key — so a repo writes them after the promote that brings the reader, never before.

Rejected: **an engine that moves an entry from stage to stage** — the host agent would key on a
stage, which is a role by another name (§9 invariant 9), and it would take the judgement the
designer's obvious / steer / ask split and the techlead's filter exist for; the facts that end a
stage are already read. **A flow per entry** (per team). **Stages merged by key across layers** (two
lists have no one merge). **The UI check and escalation as stage keys** (the check's tooling is the
repo's; escalation is §4.9b's route for every team). **`flow` and `stage` on the record** (a stage
on the record is a role badge twice). **A stage a team may skip** (the flow is said plainly and
changed). **`td` as every team's default** (opt-in needs no skipping). **The flow as a definition
key alone** (a switch would be a file edit and a restart). **Per-repo stage briefs**, and **copying
a built-in's brief into a repo flow** (the path described per repo again; `package:` reaches it).
**Flows and defined roles as keys of `org.yml` and `.shiftlead.yml`** (a page would splice a block
into a shared file; a directory of whole files is what a page can write). **A role's `review:`
winning over the flow** (roles and flows would not be apart). **Several flows running at once on one
team** (which flow an entry belongs to, a binding of each member to one flow, followability over a
set and switches per flow are each *which team* when a team runs one flow; the ledger hands work
between teams, and a shared seat is the smaller step if one reader per repo is wanted). **A switch
that waits for every member to idle** (a busy member would hold the team's path; each moves when it
can, and the card shows who has not).
