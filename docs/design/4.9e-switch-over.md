### 4.9e The switch-over: one window from profiles and members to logins and flows

**Designed by TD-1976, with Paul; its builds are listed in `docs/reviews/TD-1976.md`.** The frame is
[ADR 2026-11-18](../decisions/2026-11-18-tools-flows-teams.md) (*One window, no dual read*). The three
pages it frames are designed: Tools (§4.2b), Flows (§4.9c) and the team page (§4.9d). Each left to this
section how a home moves from the shape it runs today to theirs, and how a session is launched on a
login. This section says both. It has no page: the move is three commands, run by the person or by a
session of the person's own that asks them what it needs (Paul, 2026-11-22: a page would be used once).

Three words are used here and in the sections that cite this one:

- **the window**: the one run of the move on a home, with every team stopped;
- **the cut-over**: the moment the window writes the marker `cut_over` in `settings.yml`. *Before the
  cut-over* and *after the cut-over*, in every section, mean before and after that marker is written;
- **the removal build**: TD-2056, which takes the first shape's code and text out, after the window.

#### What decides which shape runs

**One shape runs on a home at a time, chosen by the marker.** Until the removal build both shapes are
in the code. Before the cut-over everything the first shape does is what runs, and every launch is a
profile's. After it nothing of the first shape runs: no lane, reader chain, member or profile is read,
and every launch is on a login. No rule reads both shapes for one team, repo or session.

- **A host is switched** when it holds a `logins.yml` (§4.2b), which the window writes.
- **The home is cut over** when `settings.yml` holds `cut_over` (§4.9d *Before and after the
  cut-over*), which the window writes last.
- **After the cut-over a built-in flow's name is resolved in `shiftlead/flows3/`** (§4.9c *Where flows
  and roles live*), a flow file with no `format:` key is refused, and a `teams:` block in `org.yml` or
  in a repo's `.shiftlead.yml` is not read. The window removes `org.yml`'s; `ao org check` names a
  repo file's as *left from before the switch-over*, a note and not a failure.
- **After the cut-over no team definition of the first shape is read, so no session is a member, no
  lane exists and no team defines a seat**; the one function that reads the marker (§4.9d) is wired
  into each mechanism by a build of its own (TD-2076), none relying on a list that happens to be
  empty. What that leaves of §6's rules:
  - **Rules for members and lanes act on nothing**: 2 (a wanted restart), 6 (new work in a lane), 7 (a
    brief changed), 8 (work for a team that wound down), 9 (finished: nothing winds a team down for
    lack of work, §4.9d *When it runs*, and the page's *concluded* is drawn for no team), 10 (the
    cadence check, the merge gate under a flow taking its place, §4.9c), 11 (merged without its
    read), 12 (conventions relayed: a stage's session is fresh and reads them at its start) and 14
    (scale with the work), with §4.9b *The reader*'s chain; and 1, 4 and 5 as far as they act on a
    member. **The reading of which entry is pickable, which rule 6 uses, is kept**: the Offer act
    calls it (§4.9c *Ways in*); only the telling of lanes is off.
  - **Rule 3 stays, for three built-in seats and the director.** The tech lead (on `asks`, and on
    `tag` for the repo's tagging), the sweeper (on `swept`), the prompts seat (on `prompt`) and the
    director (on its own) are filled, and their sessions ended, as rule 3 and §4.9b give it today.
    §4.9d *How each one runs* says what is new: the home writes each seat's record, since no
    first-shape Start does; the record carries `builtin`, and its model and logins are the repo's
    built-in set's (TD-2066's function); a fill is made only while §4.9d *When they run* allows it
    (TD-2065) and is launched on a login (*The launch on a login*, TD-2068); a tech lead's fills are
    bounded at 25 an hour. **What goes is every seat a team defined** — the reader seats, pools,
    extras, the two auditors (§4.9c) — **and the manager**: no record is due by `team`. **Board
    answers and pull requests nobody holds are no seats of rule 3**: each is a job with a record of
    its own, run by §4.9c's acts (§4.9d *How each one runs*, TD-2053).
  - **A stage's and an audit's session is ruled by §4.9c alone**: none of rules 1 to 5 acts on it,
    its loss and its silence are §4.9c's *Lost* and *Silent*, and it ends by `ao stage end`, so
    `ao progress restart` from one is refused, *a stage's session ends its stage* (§4.7).
  - **Rule 13 (stuck work) stays, with two readings changed.** A decided board line is read against
    its job record and no lane (TD-2053 builds this reading with the job; TD-2076 only stops the
    lanes' one): its actor is the record's holder, and a line past the rule's wait
    with no record, or with a record that has no holder and does not wait on the person, is reported
    with why — *no team of `<repo>` is started*, *`<set>` cannot run board answers*, or the state of
    the seat's one running job. An open ask is reported as before, except one whose recipient is a
    member or seat of the first shape — a session with a `team` on its record, no `seat_model` and
    no `builtin`, not the person's own, whose record was made before the cut-over.
  - The tick runs no first-shape sweep and launches nothing by a profile.
- **The first shape's verbs and controls are refused after the cut-over.**
  `ao team flow`, `size`, `max`, `add` and `on-work`, the Settings team card's flow pick and
  **Apply**, and the Members dialog are refused, *this went at the switch-over*, with the team page
  named; `ao pr held` answers that no pull request is held. A team is started and stopped by the
  team page's own acts (§4.9d), never by the first shape's start.
- **A fresh home is switched by the same move.** On a home with no profiles and no teams the move
  writes an empty `logins.yml`, `builtins.default` with the package's models and an empty list, and the
  marker, and asks for no answers. The removal build makes a new home start so with no command.

#### The three commands

Each is a client command that reads and writes the home's files, as `ao migrate` is (§4.7); none is an
RPC, and no page calls one.

- **`ao switch-over check`** changes nothing. It prints, per host, the logins the move would make from
  that host's profiles (§4.2b *What the switch-over makes*), then each team with the flow, logins and
  counts the answers give it, the built-in set, the work in flight and what becomes of each piece (*Work in flight*), and
  every **blocker**, each by name with what cures it. `--answers` writes the answers file where there
  is none, filled with what the home can suggest. It exits non-zero while a blocker to cure
  beforehand stands (*Blockers*).
- **`ao switch-over move`** runs the window (*The window*). `--rehearse` runs it on a copy of the
  home's files in a scratch directory, stops and starts nothing, and prints what the home would hold
  afterwards.
- **`ao switch-over undo`** puts the home back (*Going back*).

**The answers file** is `~/.shiftlead/switch-over/answers.yml`. It is what the person decides, in one
place they can read before anything runs:

```yaml
hosts:
  kmaster:
    logins:                       # one per folder the check found, keyed <tool>:<folder>
      "claude-code:~/.claude": {name: paul, label: Paul}
      "gemini:~/.gemini-reader": {name: reader}
teams:
  ao-grind: {label: ShiftLead, flow: design-wise, logins: ["claude-code:paul"], at_once: {build: 2}, most: 4,
             schedule_login: "claude-code:paul"}    # only where the team's schedule names a profile
builtins:
  default:
    models: {tech-lead: opus, sweeper: sonnet}     # a seat left out runs on the package's model
    logins: ["claude-code:paul"]
director: {model: opus, logins: ["claude-code:paul"]}   # only where org.yml has a director
```

What `--answers` suggests: for a login, the first profile's name on the folder; for a team, what
§4.9d *What the cut-over's move must produce* gives — the built-in format-3 flow of the name its flow
has today, and `design-wise` where that is `td` (TD-2075), the logins made from the profiles its members and seats ran on, the members' first, and
`at_once` for the stage that takes ready-to-build entries the sum of today's builder sizes, with
`most` left out; for the set, the package's models and the logins of the team named first. Beside
each team's flow it lists **the seats the team runs today that the picked flow lacks** (ao-grind's
fact-check seats on Gemini and Grok are the case: `design-wise` has none, and they go; Paul,
2026-11-22. Their logins are still made, and a flow that names their models can use them later).
A team whose schedule names a `profile:` gets a suggested `schedule_login`, the login that profile's
folder becomes, which the move writes as `teams.<team>.schedule.login`. A schedule is a team's alone
and starts no session of its own, so it takes no model and no list.

**Blockers.** The move runs only when the check finds none. They are of two kinds. **One to cure
beforehand** makes the check exit non-zero and refuses a rehearsal. **One of the day** — a team not
stopped, a live session that is not the person's own, an ssh node not yet switched — is printed
under *to do on the day*; the check still exits zero and a rehearsal runs, and only `move` itself
refuses on it.

- on a host, each case of §4.2b's *Refused beforehand*, to be cured in the old shape;
- a folder with no name in the answers, or a name the rules of §4.2b refuse;
- a team with no flow in the answers, or one that names no format-3 flow the team's host can follow
  (§4.9d *The flow pick and a switch* gives the reasons); a team whose list is empty; for a team on
  the home or on a container node, a login in its list that its host would not have; for a team on
  the home, a model its flow names that no login in its list offers (a node's team is *not
  checked* for models, and an ssh node's for its logins too, §4.9d *A team on a node*);
- `builtins.default` with an empty list, on a home that has a team; a seat's model that no login in
  the set's list offers;
- a node whose ShiftLead is not the home's version;
- of the day: an ssh node that is not switched, or does not answer when `move` asks, the home
  reading no earlier report for it (*The window*, step 2). A node that will not come back is removed
  from the home first, as any node is (§4.4a), and a team whose host is gone is then a blocker to
  cure beforehand, its definition taken out in the old shape; a team that is not stopped,
  or a live session that is not the person's own — a member's, a seat's or the director's. A
  person's own session, in a team or not, is no blocker and stays in tmux through the
  window.

#### The window

`ao switch-over move` does these in order and writes each step it finishes to
`~/.shiftlead/switch-over/journal.json`, which also holds a hash of the answers file. Run again after
a failure, it starts at the first step the journal does not hold; a step is written so that running
it twice leaves what running it once does. **A run that finds the answers changed since the journal
began is refused**, naming the file: the cure is the file as it was, or an undo and a new window.
**A step that fails leaves the host agents stopped** and says so, with the two ways on, each of which
starts them: run the move again, or undo. A person's own session meanwhile finds no host agent, as
during any restart of it: its hooks pass and an `ao` call is refused until the agent is back.

1. **Check.** The check above, the blockers of the day among them. Nothing has changed if it refuses
   here. **It is read once more right after step 3**, when the stopped host agents can start
   nothing: a first-shape schedule may have started a team since the wind-down, and if it did the
   move starts the agents again and refuses, with nothing changed.
2. **Nodes first.** A container node on the home's machine keeps its home in a directory of the
   home's (§4.4a), so this command moves it, in step 5, and the answers' `hosts:` holds its folders
   beside the home's. An ssh node is moved on the node: `ao switch-over check --host --answers` there
   writes that node's own answers file, holding its `hosts:` part alone, and `ao switch-over move
   --host` does steps 3 to 5 for that host and starts its host agent again, with its own journal
   and backup, once the home's teams are stopped. Its host agent reports `switched` when it
   connects, the home tells each node in the same greeting whether it is cut over, and the home's
   move is refused until every registered ssh node reports `switched`. Between a node's move and the
   home's that node holds no profile, so nothing can be launched on it; with every team stopped only
   a person's own `ao new` there is refused, *this host is switched and the home is not yet*.
3. **Stop the host agent**, the home's and each container node's, so no tick writes a record or a
   setting under the move. Sessions stay in tmux.
4. **Back up.** `settings.yml`, `org.yml`, `hosts.yml`, `profiles.yml`, the stored session records,
   the home's record of its teams and each container node's same files are copied to
   `~/.shiftlead/switch-over/before/`. A run that finds the copy leaves it as it is.
5. **Each host: logins from profiles.** `logins.yml` is written as §4.2b *What the switch-over makes*
   says, with the answers' names, and only then is `profiles.yml` removed. **No folder is moved or
   renamed**, on the home or in a container node (a node's logins keep their folders under
   `/shiftlead/profiles/`), so no sign-in is lost. **The move is the one writer of a container
   node's file in the window, from that node's own profiles**; `ao host up` afterwards only adds a
   login a list names that the node lacks (*A container node's `logins.yml`*), and never touches one
   the node has. Each stored record gets the `login` and `account` it ran under, and its `profile`
   is rewritten to that login's name (*A record's `profile`*). A run that resumes here, with
   `profiles.yml` already gone, reads the profiles from the backup's copy.
6. **The home's settings.** For every team, `teams.<team>` whole, as §4.9d *What the cut-over's move
   must produce* lists it, from the answers; `builtins.default`; `sweep.profile` dropped. In
   `org.yml`, where the director seat is defined (§5 `director: {profile, brief}`), `director.model`
   and `director.logins` are written from the answers and `director.profile` removed; the `teams:`
   block and each `roles:` entry's `profile:` are removed.
7. **Every team's record at the home** is written `stopped: {at: <the window's time>, by: person}`,
   so no schedule starts a team before the person's Start.
8. **The marker**, `cut_over: <date>`, at the top of `settings.yml`.
9. **Start the host agent** again, the home's and each container node's.

**Repos are no step of the window** (Paul, 2026-11-22; the frame had six pull requests held unmerged
and merged inside it). After the cut-over a repo file's `teams:` block is not read, so nothing waits
on a repo. Each repo's block, its `techlead:` and its first-shape flow values are taken out by an
ordinary pull request afterwards, filed as an entry in that repo's own ledger.

#### Work in flight

The window runs with every team stopped, so no session is carried over, and **the window writes
nothing for the work**: no stage record, and no change to a claim. The work is taken up by one rule
of the flow's, which holds for any entry and not only at the window.

- **An entry's first record takes the open pull request that is its work** (§4.9c *Ways in*, amended
  with this section; the Offer act's build, TD-2051, builds it as one function). When the home first
  offers a pickable entry and such a pull request exists, the record is written with its branch and
  pull request. The session started is of the stage that takes the entry's `Kind:` and is told of
  the branch and pull request as a session of a stage entered again is. So an entry in flight at the
  window is picked up with its work at the team's first Start; work that was under review is read
  again by the new flow's review stages, and no first-shape verdict is carried.
- **Which pull request is an entry's work.** An open pull request whose **branch name** holds the
  entry's id as a whole token, whatever its case (`td-205-…` is not TD-2051's); the title is not
  read. **Never an entry's work**: a fork's, and one whose every file is a ledger or board file (a
  filing pull request, §4.4); the entry is offered afresh as if they were not there. A branch that
  holds two entries' ids is nobody's work, and the check names it. Where several match, the newest
  is taken and the others stay where they are. **Two cases make the entry wait, offered to
  nobody**, so no second branch is made for work that is someone's: the newest match is held by a
  live session, while that session lives; or it is a **draft**, while it stays one. A person who
  wants a pull request left alone makes it a draft, and a cloud session's parked pull request is one
  already. A draft left for good is surfaced as any long-open pull request is, by the reading that
  names pull requests open past two days; nothing else chases it.
- **The seat for pull requests nobody holds comes second.** It skips a pull request that is the work
  of a pickable entry with no record, taken or waiting; any other it treats as §4.9c says.
- **An entry that is not pickable is not taken up**, whatever its pull request: one behind a
  blocker or a decision, or a part that waits. It is offered when it becomes pickable, with its pull
  request, by the same rule.
- **A claim with no pull request lapses with its session.** A lease is held only by a live session's
  record, and every member's session has ended before the window, so there is nothing to drop: the
  entry is offered afresh by its way in. A pushed branch with no pull request is read by nothing;
  the check names each.
- **The check lists all three** — the entries that will be taken up with their pull requests, the
  ones that wait, and the branches nothing will read — by calling the Offer act's own function with
  every session the window will have ended (the blockers of the day) taken as gone, so the list is
  what the home will do after it. A repo whose pull requests cannot be read is listed *not read*,
  and is no blocker.
- **A design whose builds are open** needs no record: it is not pickable while its parts are open, by
  the rules a lane reads today, which the Offer act reads (§4.9c *Ways in*).
- **Mail to a member or seat of the first shape** is left where it is. The check lists each open ask
  to one, for the person to answer or close before the window; nothing answers it afterwards.

**A person's own session in a team** (§4.9 *A person in the team*, TD-160) is the person's after the
cut-over as before. It holds its claim as a lease with no stage record, the Offer act offers that
entry to nobody while the lease holds, its pull request merges by the cadence and not by a flow, and
it counts toward neither `at_once` nor `most`. Its chip is its holder's name in grey (§4.9c *A
claim's chip is its stage*).

#### Going back

`ao switch-over undo` stops the host agents, puts back every file of the backup but the session
records, removes `logins.yml`
where the backup held none, removes the marker, and starts the agents. It works on a window that
finished and on one that stopped part-way. **Of a stored session record it puts back only the three
fields the move rewrote** — `profile`, `login`, `account` — where the backup holds the record, so
what a person's own session wrote to its record since is kept; a record made since the cut-over is
left as it is, its `profile` naming no profile, which today's readers take as a profile that was
deleted — **except a seat record the home wrote** (§4.9d *How each one runs*: one that carries
`builtin`, and a director's made since the cut-over), which undo removes with its launch, so no
prompts record reads as a second director under the first shape and a first-shape Start writes its
own seats again. **It then moves the journal and the backup aside**, to
`~/.shiftlead/switch-over/undone-<date>/`, so a later window starts from its first step with a
backup of its own. An ssh node is put back by `ao switch-over undo --host` on it, after the
home's.

**It is refused once any team has been started since the cut-over** (Paul, 2026-11-22), and while a
session launched since the cut-over is alive. The first is read from the teams' records at the home,
where the journal holds step 7: a team whose record is not the one the move wrote, `by` and `at`
alike (a Start removes it, and a later Stop writes another `at`). A window that stopped before step
7 has started no team and is never refused for it. A team made since the window does not refuse it
and is lost with the rest. Before it puts anything back it prints
what is lost and asks once: every setting, team and login changed since the window. After the first
Start the only way is forward, by fixing what is wrong.

#### The launch on a login

Built and promoted before the window, and read only after the cut-over.

- **Every launch the home makes for a team or a built-in set calls the pick** (TD-2060's function):
  given the list — a team's, or a built-in set's — and the model of the stage, the audit or the seat,
  the first usable login that offers it (§4.9d *The logins, in order*). With one, the session is
  launched in that login's folder (`home_env`, §4.2b), on the tool's word for the model, with the
  login's `extra_args`, `unattended_args` and `permission_wait`. With none usable the launch waits,
  held by the usage gate. With none that offers the model the team *cannot run* (§4.9d) and nothing
  is launched.
- **The record** of a session so launched holds `login` and `account` (node-owned, the ones it runs
  under) and `seat_model` (home-owned, the model the flow or the set named). The record's `model`
  stays the one observed.
- **A session the person starts** takes a login where it starts: `ao new --login <tool>:<name>` and
  the + New form's pick, each defaulting to the login last used there; `--model` is optional and the
  login's pin, else the tool's default, stands without it. After the cut-over `--profile` is refused,
  *profiles went at the switch-over: name a login*.
- **The director seat** takes its `model:` and ordered `logins:` from `org.yml`'s `director:` and is
  launched by the same pick.

**A session moves down the list.** The tick of the host a session runs on reads, for each unattended
session with a `seat_model`, whether its login is still usable. When it is not (its account at its
reserve, or `limited`), and a later login of its list is usable and offers its model, the tick moves
it: it marks the record `moving`, reads the handoff text from the old session's transcript, ends
the old session, launches the new one on the next login **under the same record**, with what the
stage's session is told (TD-2049) followed by the handoff text, and clears the mark. The record's
`login` and `account` are rewritten and the move is added to the record's `login_moves`. **A holder
whose record is `moving` is neither lost nor silent** (§4.9c *The home acts only where no session
will*), and no crash restart applies to it (§6 rule 1), so nothing else acts on the record between
the old session's end and the new one's start. A launch that fails clears the mark with the session
ended, and the holder is then lost by §4.9c's own rule. **A mark is never left standing**: a host
agent that starts, and any tick that finds a `moving` record with no live session ten minutes after
the mark was set, clears it, and the holder is lost the same way. **The list a move reads is the
one the session was launched with**, kept on its record (`login_list`), on a node as on the home,
which is why a changed list moves no running session (§4.9d). **Who owns each field**: `seat_model`
and `login_list` are the home's, sent with the launch; `login`, `account`, `login_moves` and
`moving` are the node's, written by the host the session runs on and read at the home as any
node-owned field is (§4.4, `models.NODE_OWNED`). A move spends no offer, no limit and nothing of the total cap
(§4.9d). With no later login usable the session waits where it is. An interactive session is never
moved (invariant 5). A session does not move back up while it runs; the next launch takes the first
usable login again.

**The handoff is one adapter method**, `handoff(transcript)`, on the host that ran the session (§4.3):
it reads the tool's own transcript and returns the session's turns as plain text, newest last. The
core keeps the newest 24,000 characters of it, cut at a turn's start, under the heading *Where the
session before you stopped*. No tool's own resume is used (the frame). An adapter that does not
implement it hands over nothing, and the new session starts from what its stage is told and the
branch.

**A team on a node.** The home sends the launch with the list as names and the model, and the node's
host agent calls the same pick on its own logins. It answers with the login it took, or *none
usable*, which the home treats as a wait, or **`no login offers <model>`**. On that the home starts
nothing, or ends the holder it had named, clearing it and spending no offer, and marks the team
*cannot run*, naming the model, until the team's list or flow changes (§4.9d *A team on a node*). A
built-in seat launched on a node is the same, the set marked *cannot run* that seat. The spend a node
sends the home names the `login` beside the `account` (§4.4a).

**A container node's `logins.yml`** is written by the home's provisioning, as §4.2b *On a node*
says: `ao host up` adds an entry for each login a team on that node lists, or a built-in set lists
for a repo with a team on that node, that the node lacks, under the home's name, with the home
login's `account` key and `billing`, its folder `/shiftlead/profiles/<name>/`, not signed in. The
person signs in inside once, as today. It removes none.

#### A record's `profile`

Today's code reads a record's `profile` in many places (the usage gate, spend, the Sessions card).
The window rewrites each stored record's `profile` to its login's name, and a launch on a login
writes the login's name there too, so those readers keep working through the stretch between the
cut-over and the removal build. **On a switched host the profile reader answers from `logins.yml`**:
a lookup of a profile by name, and of its account, gives the login of that name on that host, with
the login's folder, billing, prices and arguments where a profile had them (the launch build writes
this, TD-2068). **A record whose profile made no login** — a `shell` profile's, or one whose adapter
is not registered (§4.2b) — keeps its `profile` and gets no `login` or `account`; the reader answers
for a `shell` profile as it does today, with no file. The removal build takes the field and those
readers out.

#### The runbook

What a session of the person's follows, in this order. It asks the person at each *ask*.

1. **Before the day.** Every build this section, §4.9c and §4.9d list is merged and live. On a
   scratch home and a scratch repo: run the move (a fresh home), add a login from an existing folder,
   make a team, start it, and take one entry from filed to merged through a format-3 flow. Then on the
   real home: `ao switch-over check --answers`, the interview that fills the answers (*ask* each
   team's flow, its logins in order, its counts, the set's models and logins), cure each blocker, and
   `ao switch-over move --rehearse` until its print is what the person wants.
2. **The day.** Wind down every team and wait for its sessions to end, so work reaches a pull request.
   *Ask* before stopping what is left. On each ssh node, if the home has one: `ao switch-over check
   --host --answers`, its names filled, then `ao switch-over move --host`. Then on the home run
   `ao switch-over move`. Read its report.
3. **After.** `ao org check`; each team's page reads as switched with the flow, logins and counts
   picked. *Ask*, then start one team and watch one entry go from filed to merged. Until that Start,
   `ao switch-over undo` is open. Then start the others.
4. **Clean up.** File the entry in each repo's ledger that takes its `teams:` block out. Tell the
   person the removal build (TD-2056) is unblocked; it is theirs to release.
